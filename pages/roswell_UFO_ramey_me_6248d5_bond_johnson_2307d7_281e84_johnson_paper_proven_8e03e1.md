## Introduction

The best-supported answer is **we do not know whether J. [Bond Johnson]({{ 'johnson/' | relative_url }}) brought the sheet seen in Brigadier General Roger [Ramey]({{ 'ramey/' | relative_url }})’s hand into the office**. Johnson did at one stage entertain exactly that possibility: he said he might have carried with him a copy of the Associated Press “flash” that prompted his assignment and handed it to Ramey. By 2001, however, he described this as an early speculation and explicitly withdrew it, saying he had been mistaken.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/11/j-bond-johnsons-statement-on-his-visit.html?showComment=1447183915509" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: J. Bond Johnson&#x27;s Statement on His Visit to General RameyNovember 9, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 9, 2015</span></span></span>

That reversal matters because the paper’s [provenance]({{ 'provenance/' | relative_url }}) governs how much can reasonably be inferred from it. If Johnson supplied a newsroom wire-service sheet, the photographed document could simply be contemporary press copy about Roswell. If it was already in Ramey’s office, possibilities widen to an internal message, incoming teletype, draft statement or other office document. The photograph proves that Ramey held a text-bearing sheet during the photographic session; it does **not** record how the sheet reached his hand. Even an official US Air Force attempt to extract its text from archival photographs concluded that the imagery was insufficient for the requested reading.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.users.globalnet.co.uk/~hex/ufo/text/pages026-050/page33.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: users.globalnet.co.uk">[GlobalNet]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">users.globalnet.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Global Netpage33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">page33 - Air Force Roswell Report...</span></span></span>

## Johnson's earlier press-copy speculation

The newsroom part of Johnson’s [story]({{ 'story/' | relative_url }}) has relatively firm foundations. In his 1989 interview with Roswell researcher Kevin Randle, Johnson recalled returning to the *Fort Worth Star-Telegram* on 8 July 1947 when his editor asked whether he had his camera and sent him to Ramey’s headquarters. Johnson said the newsroom had just received an alert over the Associated [Press wire]({{ 'press-wire/' | relative_url }}) — although he allowed that it might instead have been United Press — reporting that material from Roswell was being flown to Fort Worth.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/11/j-bond-johnsons-statement-on-his-visit.html?showComment=1447183915509" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: J. Bond Johnson&#x27;s Statement on His Visit to General RameyNovember 9, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 9, 2015</span></span></span>

A 1997 radio discussion independently preserves the same basic mechanism: Johnson’s city editor had pulled information from the wire saying the recovered material was heading to Ramey’s office, then dispatched Johnson with his camera. That establishes an important distinction. There is good retrospective evidence that **a wire report triggered Johnson’s journey**; it does not automatically follow that Johnson **carried that wire report with him**, much less that it was the particular sheet later photographed in Ramey’s hand.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fight.fudgie.org/search/show/ab/episode/19970702_Wed" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fight.fudgie.org">[Fight]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fight.fudgie.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Fight Art Bell</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Art Bell - Coast to Coast AM with Art Bell - Jamie Shandera - Roswell Crash - TranscriptJuly 2, 1997...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 2, 1997</span></span></span>

The stronger claim emerged in Johnson’s later recollections. In Dennis Balthaser’s 2001 interview, Johnson was asked about a previous statement that the paper in Ramey’s hand was a press release Johnson had handed him. Johnson acknowledged the underlying recollection but reframed it carefully: it had been an “early speculation” that he might have handed Ramey the AP flash his editor had given him concerning the Roswell material being flown to Fort Worth. He then said that speculation had been erroneous.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/11/j-bond-johnsons-statement-on-his-visit.html?showComment=1447183915509" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: J. Bond Johnson&#x27;s Statement on His Visit to General RameyNovember 9, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 9, 2015</span></span></span>

That wording is significant. Johnson was not merely changing his identification from one positively remembered document to another. In the 2001 formulation, he downgraded the original claim itself from recollection to speculation. Consequently, the strongest defensible version of the AP-paper hypothesis is not “Johnson remembered handing Ramey the AP flash”, but rather **Johnson once thought that he might have done so**.

Researchers sceptical of extraordinary readings of the photographed text have nevertheless regarded this possibility as important. Barry Greenwood argued that the sheet’s apparent formatting had characteristics consistent with journalistic teletype material. His comparison focused on such features as punctuation and the apparent time/date-style ending, contrasting them with examples of military teletypes. Greenwood also noted Johnson’s earlier AP-flash account as a possible explanation for how ordinary news copy could have reached Ramey’s hand.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fr.scribd.com/document/314034746/2004-I-11-An-Observation-on-the-Ramey-Memo-ed-Barry-Greenwood-pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fr.scribd.com">[Scribd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fr.scribd.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Analysis of the Ramey Memo &#124; PDF &#124; Travel &#124; Young Adult</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Analysis of the Ramey Memo &#124; PDF &#124; Travel &#124; Young Adult...</span></span></span>

That is an argument for plausibility, however, not documentary proof of transfer. No surviving AP sheet has been securely matched to the photographed page, and Johnson’s later withdrawal prevents his testimony from closing that evidential gap.

## Why Johnson withdrew the explanation

Johnson's 2001 answer is unusually clear about the fact of the reversal but frustratingly unclear about its basis. He said he had been wrong in his earlier speculation, yet the available quotation does not provide a contemporary record, newly discovered witness or physical observation from 1947 that demonstrated the mistake.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/11/j-bond-johnsons-statement-on-his-visit.html?showComment=1447183915509" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: J. Bond Johnson&#x27;s Statement on His Visit to General RameyNovember 9, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 9, 2015</span></span></span>

That makes the chronology of his testimony important. The earliest detailed interview material cited by Randle, from 1989, firmly places an AP or other wire alert in the newsroom but does not establish that the photographed paper was that alert. Johnson's subsequent involvement in attempts to interpret the photograph came much later. The peer-reviewed *Journal of Scientific Exploration* paper by James Houran and Kevin Randle noted that Johnson's retelling of events in Ramey’s office underwent “considerable evolution” and concluded that there was no consensus about either the source or content of the message.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

This is where provenance needs to be separated from motive. Some critics have suggested that Johnson’s changed position was convenient because a civilian newswire origin would greatly reduce the memo’s potential importance. Randle has made that argument explicitly, observing that Johnson repudiated the wire-copy possibility after becoming involved with researchers attempting to extract significant Roswell information from the photograph. Greenwood similarly argued that Johnson’s later position developed alongside the Roswell Photo Interpretation Team’s more consequential interpretation of the document.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/12/jan-aldrich-barry-greenwood-and-ramey.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: Jan Aldrich, Barry Greenwood and the Ramey MemoDecember 4, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 4, 2015</span></span></span>

That chronology is relevant to assessing reliability, but it does not prove why Johnson changed his mind. There is no sound basis for treating an inferred motive as a substitute for evidence. The safer conclusion is narrower: **his later denial prevents the earlier AP-flash scenario from being treated as settled eyewitness testimony, while the unexplained reversal prevents the denial from settling the matter in the opposite direction**.

The result is an awkward evidential middle ground. Johnson is evidence that an AP alert existed in his immediate newsroom environment and that he once considered carrying it to Ramey plausible. He is not reliable evidence that the photographed sheet definitely was that alert.

## Could the paper still have been news copy?

Yes. Johnson’s withdrawal does not eliminate the civilian-document hypothesis because provenance can be investigated independently of his memory.

Greenwood's argument is the main example. Rather than trying primarily to decipher dramatic individual words, he compared the document’s visible structure with period communications. He argued that what some interpreters regarded as a signature at the bottom looked more like the sort of time-and-date ending found on teletype material. He also considered the apparent punctuation more characteristic of journalistic copy than of the military teletypes he examined.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fr.scribd.com/document/314034746/2004-I-11-An-Observation-on-the-Ramey-Memo-ed-Barry-Greenwood-pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fr.scribd.com">[Scribd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fr.scribd.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Analysis of the Ramey Memo &#124; PDF &#124; Travel &#124; Young Adult</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Analysis of the Ramey Memo &#124; PDF &#124; Travel &#124; Young Adult...</span></span></span>

This matters because an AP flash concerning Roswell would naturally contain Roswell terminology. Finding words apparently connected with the incident would therefore not, by itself, distinguish a military message from news copy. A newsroom dispatch could mention Roswell, Fort Worth, a “disc”, Ramey or other actors in the story without possessing any privileged military information.

There is also a practical obstacle to demanding an exact match between the photographed sheet and a published newspaper article. Raw wire-service material could be edited, rearranged and rewritten before appearing in print. Greenwood therefore cautioned that failure to find a verbatim newspaper story need not disprove a newswire origin.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fr.scribd.com/document/314034746/2004-I-11-An-Observation-on-the-Ramey-Memo-ed-Barry-Greenwood-pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fr.scribd.com">[Scribd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fr.scribd.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Analysis of the Ramey Memo &#124; PDF &#124; Travel &#124; Young Adult</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Analysis of the Ramey Memo &#124; PDF &#124; Travel &#124; Young Adult...</span></span></span>

But the argument works in both directions. Typography or punctuation that appears journalistic cannot demonstrate that **Johnson personally transported the page**. Ramey’s headquarters was dealing with a fast-moving national news story, so press material could conceivably have reached the office through other channels. Conversely, even if the sheet originated inside the headquarters, that would not establish that it was secret or classified. Randle has pointed out several mundane office possibilities, including a draft press statement, an unclassified communications-centre message or a summary intended to help Ramey handle press enquiries.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/10/ramey-memo-and-victims-of-wreck.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: Ramey Memo and Victims of the Wreck</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: Ramey Memo and Victims of the WreckOctober 27, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 27, 2015</span></span></span>

The provenance question therefore cannot safely be reduced to a binary choice between “Johnson’s AP flash” and “secret military memo”.

## What the photograph can prove about provenance

The photograph supplies unusually strong evidence for the **existence and setting** of the document. Ramey is visibly holding it during the Fort Worth photographic session associated with the Roswell debris. The surviving photographic record has archival provenance through the *Fort Worth Star-Telegram* collection, and investigators working for the Air Force obtained first-generation prints and negatives from the University of Texas at Arlington when examining the Roswell material in the 1990s.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/decade-1990s-roswell-report-fact-vs-fiction-1994-dtic-ada326148" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">decade 1990s roswell report fact vs fiction 1994 dtic ada326148</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyRoswell Report: Fact vs Fiction, 1994, U.S. Air Force Headquarters (Office of Air Force History) · 1994 · UFO Transparency...</span></span></span>

But photographic provenance is not the same thing as document provenance. The image establishes:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Ramey possessed the sheet at the moment Johnson photographed him.
* The sheet contained lines of text.
* It was physically present in the same office and photographic sequence as the Roswell debris.
* Its visible text was recorded incidentally rather than reproduced as a document for the camera.

</div>

What the image does **not** show is where the paper was minutes earlier, who typed it, who received it, whether Johnson touched it, whether it arrived through [military communications]({{ 'military-communication/' | relative_url }}) or whether it was press material.

That limitation became concrete during the Air Force's 1994 Roswell investigation. After obtaining archival photographic material, investigators noticed the text-bearing sheet and sent the image to what the report called a “national level organisation” for digitisation and interpretation. The organisation reported on 20 July 1994 that the photographs were of insufficient quality to visualise the requested details.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.users.globalnet.co.uk/~hex/ufo/text/pages026-050/page33.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: users.globalnet.co.uk">[GlobalNet]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">users.globalnet.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Global Netpage33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">page33 - Air Force Roswell Report...</span></span></span>

Later private researchers have claimed substantially more success in deciphering individual words and structural features, but the published literature itself illustrates the problem. Houran and Randle surveyed competing readings and emphasised the confounds involved in extracting language from an image operating near its resolution limit. Their discussion also specifically identified Johnson’s shifting account as a complication in determining where the document came from.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

The photograph therefore gives the Ramey document excellent **event provenance** — it really was in Ramey’s hand during the Roswell episode — but incomplete **document provenance**. Those are different claims, and much of the controversy comes from sliding from the first to the second.

## The most defensible answer

There is no adequate evidence for stating as fact that Johnson brought Ramey the disputed paper. There is equally no contemporaneous evidence proving that he did not.

The AP-flash hypothesis has a coherent mechanism behind it. Johnson was dispatched because his newspaper had received wire-service information about the Roswell material; he later acknowledged having speculated that he carried that copy to Fort Worth and handed it to Ramey; and Greenwood subsequently identified features he regarded as compatible with journalistic teletype copy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/11/j-bond-johnsons-statement-on-his-visit.html?showComment=1447183915509" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: J. Bond Johnson&#x27;s Statement on His Visit to General RameyNovember 9, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 9, 2015</span></span></span>

Against that stands Johnson’s own later rejection of the idea. Because he characterised the original account as speculation rather than firm memory, it cannot responsibly be elevated into a settled eyewitness fact. At the same time, his unexplained change of position — amid broader changes in his later Roswell recollections — means the retraction should not be treated as independent proof that the sheet originated within military channels.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

The provenance should therefore remain **unresolved**. A civilian AP or other press document remains plausible; so does an unclassified document already present in Ramey’s headquarters. A military message is also possible, but merely locating the sheet in a general’s office cannot establish that status, still less that it was classified.

That restraint has an important consequence for interpretations of the Ramey memo. Before purported words on the photograph can be used as evidence of what the military privately knew about Roswell, the document’s origin and function have to be considered. Johnson’s changing testimony does not answer that question. It is, instead, one of the principal reasons the question remains open.
