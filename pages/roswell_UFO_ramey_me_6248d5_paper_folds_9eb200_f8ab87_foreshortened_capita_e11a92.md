## Introduction

Foreshortening can make one capital letter resemble another before blur, [film grain]({{ 'film-grain/' | relative_url }}) or digital [enhancement]({{ 'enhancement/' | relative_url }}) enters the problem. When a flat line of type is photographed obliquely, the dimension that recedes from the camera is compressed in the image. A tall capital may become unusually squat; a broad character may become narrow if the page turns away in the other direction. The strokes are still projections of the original letter, but their proportions are no longer those of the typeface that produced them.

That matters unusually strongly in the [Ramey memo]({{ 'ramey-memo/' | relative_url }}) photograph. The surviving image shows the sheet at an oblique and locally changing orientation rather than square to the camera, and the original Fort Worth Star-Telegram [negative]({{ 'negative/' | relative_url }}) is held by the University of Texas at Arlington.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AGen_Ramey_Roswell_memo_1947.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">File:Gen Ramey Roswell memo 1947.jpg</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Gen Ramey Roswell memo 1947.jpg - Wikimedia CommonsJuly 8, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: [July 8](&amp;#123;&amp;#123; &#x27;july-8/&#x27; &#124; relative_url &amp;#125;&amp;#125;), 1947</span></span></span> At the memo's tiny image scale, a geometrically compressed capital can therefore approach the shape of a different character before photographic degradation removes still. The practical consequence is important: apparent letter shape cannot safely be treated as original letter shape.

## How oblique viewing changes a capital's proportions

The simplest case is a genuinely flat piece of paper tilted relative to the camera. Imagine a rectangular capital printed on that plane. As the plane turns away, distances measured along the receding direction project into progressively smaller distances in the photograph. Computer-vision research treats this perspective distortion as a recognised obstacle to reading camera-captured documents and uses perspective rectification to recover an approximation of a front-facing view.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0262885605000119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Perspective rectification of document images using fuzzy set and morphological operations - ScienceDirectMay 1, 2005...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 1, 2005</span></span></span>

For letters, that transformation affects recognition because alphabetic identity depends partly on relationships between strokes rather than merely on the presence of ink. Experimental work on uppercase and lowercase Arial letters found that line terminations are particularly important visual features for distinguishing letters.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/j.1467-9280.2008.02218.x/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: psychologicalscience.org">[Association for Psychological Science]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">psychologicalscience.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Association for Psychological ScienceFeatures for Identification of Uppercase and Lowercase Letters - Psychological Science - APS...</span></span></span> Foreshortening changes the apparent spacing and geometry around precisely such features.

Consider a capital **V**. Viewed frontally, its two diagonals descend through a substantial vertical distance before meeting. Compress the vertical dimension and those diagonals become shallower while the junction moves visually closer to the top. David Rudiak, an advocate of a Roswell-related reading of the Ramey memo, has pointed to this exact type of problem: he argued that vertical perspective compression in the photograph could make a capital V look sufficiently squat for observers to perceive something resembling a lower-case r. He estimated compression of roughly 50 per cent in parts of his working image and consequently stretched those regions vertically during processing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2018/12/david-rudiaks-latest-on-ramey-memo.html?showComment=1546118226661" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: David Rudiak&#x27;s Latest on the Ramey MemoDecember 19, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 19, 2018</span></span></span>

That estimate should not be mistaken for an independently established measurement applying uniformly to the whole memo. It comes from one participant in the transcription dispute. The underlying mechanism, however, is conventional projective geometry: document-recognition studies independently demonstrate that perspective distortion changes character geometry enough to reduce recognition accuracy, and that rectification can improve it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0262885605000119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Perspective rectification of document images using fuzzy set and morphological operations - ScienceDirectMay 1, 2005...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 1, 2005</span></span></span>

The Ramey sheet makes the problem harder because it is not simply one flat plane at one angle. Photographs show a folded document, while reconstruction work has proposed substantial differences in orientation between portions of the sheet.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ramey-memo.com/the-missing-u/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ramey-memo.com">[Ramey Memo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ramey-memo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ramey Memo The missing U – Unlocking The Mystery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ramey Memo The missing U – Unlocking The Mystery</span></span></span> Research on camera-captured curled documents explains why this distinction matters: when a page is deformed, different local areas may have to be treated as separate approximately planar patches rather than corrected with one transformation for the entire page.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0262885606000904" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A partition approach for the restoration of camera images of planar and curled document - ScienceDirectAugust 1, 2006...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 1, 2006</span></span></span> More sophisticated systems reconstruct the three-dimensional deformation itself before attempting to flatten the text.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://publications.ri.cmu.edu/rectification-and-3d-reconstruction-of-curved-document-images" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: publications.ri.cmu.edu">[Robotics Institute Publications]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">publications.ri.cmu.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Robotics Institute PublicationsRectification and 3D Reconstruction of Curved Document Images - Robotics Institute Carnegie Mellon University...</span></span></span>

So there is no single meaningful statement that “the Ramey memo is compressed by X per cent”. A letter above a fold, one near the centre of the sheet and one beside the distorted margin can have different projection geometries. The relevant question is local: **how was this particular capital projected onto the negative?**

## Why tiny capitals become especially ambiguous

Perspective distortion would be comparatively manageable if every letter occupied hundreds of clean pixels. The Ramey problem is different because the document is only a small component of the original photograph. The available image is therefore already operating in a regime where fine distinctions between strokes are fragile. Rudiak's own discussion of competing processing attempts illustrates the point: he argued that lowering effective character resolution could smear an apparent S towards O-like geometry and objected to interpretations based on versions in which characters had been substantially reduced.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2018/12/david-rudiaks-latest-on-ramey-memo.html?showComment=1546118226661" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: David Rudiak&#x27;s Latest on the Ramey MemoDecember 19, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 19, 2018</span></span></span> Whatever one thinks of his proposed transcription, the example identifies a real image-analysis hazard.

Vision research provides a useful explanation. Letter recognition depends on spatial-frequency information — roughly, visual information at different scales. Fine components describe relatively small details, while coarser components preserve the broad skeleton of a character. Experiments with filtered letters show that recognition deteriorates once the spatial-frequency information needed to discriminate characters is removed. Uppercase alphabetic characters require sufficient spatial-frequency content for reliable identification, with increasingly complex symbols generally requiring finer information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29297056/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nih.gov.</span></span></span>

This creates a particularly troublesome sequence in an oblique historical photograph:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. **Perspective first changes the proportions.** A capital is projected shorter or narrower than its original form.
2. **The photographic system records that already-distorted form at small scale.** Fine separations and stroke endings may occupy very little image space.
3. **Focus, grain and reproduction losses further weaken small features.** The coarse silhouette survives better than subtle distinctions.
4. **Enlargement magnifies the surviving pattern, not the missing detail.** A larger ambiguous character remains an ambiguous character unless the source contained discriminating information.

</div>

The ordering matters. It is tempting to imagine blur acting on an otherwise normal letter and then ask which capital the blurred shape resembles. In the Ramey photograph, the more appropriate mental model is often a **foreshortened letter that was subsequently recorded imperfectly**. A classifier — whether a human observer or OCR system — is therefore being asked to identify a degraded projection rather than a degraded front-facing glyph.

Typography research offers a useful comparison. Reviews of reading at small print sizes note that tiny letters are recognised disproportionately through coarse features, which is one reason typefaces intended for small reproduction use stronger strokes and simpler, more open forms.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3428264/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed Central (PMC)]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on nih.gov.</span></span></span> The Ramey characters receive no such optimisation: whatever typewriter or communications typography produced the sheet was photographed incidentally, at distance and at an unfavourable orientation.

That combination helps explain why observers can sincerely disagree about capital identity. In the Ramey literature, disagreement has included interpretations in which marks that should belong to an uppercase text have been perceived as apparently lower-case or otherwise anomalous forms. A 2022 review, for example, recounts Russ Estes's observation that one disputed sequence seemed to contain mixed upper- and lower-case shapes and that a proposed initial V looked more like P to him.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journalofscientificexploration.org/index.php/jse/article/view/2635" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journalofscientificexploration.org">[Journal of Scientific Exploration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journalofscientificexploration.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on journalofscientificexploration.org.</span></span></span> Such disagreement is not proof that perspective caused every disputed reading, but it is exactly what one should expect when original typographic proportions are no longer reliably visible.

## A capital can cross a recognition boundary without changing identity

The most useful way to think about foreshortening is not that perspective literally “turns V into another letter”. The printed character remains V. What changes is the evidence available to identify it.

Suppose two capitals differ strongly when printed at their intended proportions but share several coarse strokes. Compression can reduce the visual weight of their distinguishing geometry. If subsequent blur then removes a terminal, closes a small gap or merges neighbouring strokes, their photographic representations can converge further. Human recognition research confirms that small local features help discriminate visually similar letters; when those features cease to be reliably represented, confusion increases.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1076/vimr.2.2.95.4427" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: tandfonline.com">[Taylor &amp; Francis Online]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">tandfonline.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on tandfonline.com.</span></span></span>

This gives the Ramey debate an important asymmetry. A character that looks implausible as an uppercase letter in the uncorrected photograph is not necessarily evidence that the underlying document contained mixed-case typography. Conversely, simply stretching an ambiguous mark until it resembles the capital expected by an analyst does not establish that identity either.

Both mistakes treat an uncertain transformation as though it were known.

The 2002 experiment by James Houran and Kevin Randle provides a separate warning about what happens once the image becomes ambiguous enough. They gave 176 participants versions of the memo under three different contextual conditions: Roswell, atomic-bomb testing, or no suggested subject. Many participants reported deciphering words, but their proposed readings tended to follow the contextual suggestions. A smaller number of words at common locations were perceived across conditions, leading the authors to argue that further study might still be informative under stronger methodological controls.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://digital.library.adelaide.edu.au/dspace/handle/2440/3334" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: digital.library.adelaide.edu.au">[Digital Library Adelaide]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">digital.library.adelaide.edu.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on edu.au.</span></span></span>

Foreshortening was not the variable tested in that experiment, so it would be wrong to cite the study as evidence that perspective produced particular disputed letters. Its relevance is narrower and more useful: **once physical degradation has made character evidence ambiguous, expectation can influence which interpretation wins**. Correcting geometry before judging letter identity is therefore not merely cosmetic. It is one way of reducing an avoidable source of ambiguity before linguistic expectations are brought to bear.

## When perspective correction genuinely helps

If a text region lies on an approximately flat plane and its geometry can be estimated from reliable features, perspective correction can be highly effective. A planar transformation known as a homography can map the oblique quadrilateral back towards a front-facing rectangle. Document-imaging studies have long used this approach because perspective distortion interferes with conventional OCR, while rectification can improve recognition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0262885605000119" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Perspective rectification of document images using fuzzy set and morphological operations - ScienceDirectMay 1, 2005...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 1, 2005</span></span></span>

Applied conservatively to the Ramey photograph, such correction can answer a useful question: **what would the recorded strokes look like if their local patch of paper were viewed closer to front-on?** It can restore more plausible height-to-width ratios, straighten baselines and make comparisons with candidate capital forms less misleading.

There is an especially strong case for correcting geometry before comparing a photographed character with a reference typeface. Comparing an unrectified, vertically compressed mark directly with a normal capital silently gives the normal reference an advantage: the two shapes are not represented under the same projection. Rectifying the observed text — or alternatively projecting the candidate reference letters through the estimated camera geometry — makes the comparison more meaningful.

Rudiak attempted the former strategy. He describes using morphing software to flatten the Ramey sheet, straighten lines, compensate for [folds]({{ 'folds/' | relative_url }}) and make characters more consistent in size and spacing. Crucially, he also acknowledged that this process introduces artefacts, especially around strongly distorted regions, folds and the warped left margin, and that poorly visible material requires judgement when constructing the warp model.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2018/12/david-rudiaks-latest-on-ramey-memo.html?showComment=1546118226661" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: David Rudiak&#x27;s Latest on the Ramey MemoDecember 19, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 19, 2018</span></span></span> That qualification is more important than any particular transcription produced afterwards.

Modern document-restoration research reaches the same general conclusion by more formal methods. Curved-page systems estimate local patches or reconstruct a three-dimensional surface because a single global correction is insufficient for genuinely deformed paper.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0262885606000904" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A partition approach for the restoration of camera images of planar and curled document - ScienceDirectAugust 1, 2006...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 1, 2006</span></span></span> The technology can estimate a plausible inverse transformation. It cannot guarantee that every tiny character has thereby become evidentially complete.

## What correction cannot put back

Perspective correction can reverse **geometry that was recorded**. It cannot recreate distinguishing letter information that never survived the photographic chain.

This is the central limit for ambiguous capitals in the Ramey memo. Suppose foreshortening reduced the vertical separation between two important stroke features until both fell into essentially the same blurred image structure. Stretching that region vertically afterwards will increase their separation in pixels, but those new pixels are interpolated from the recorded values. The operation does not travel back to 1947 and acquire the missing detail at a more favourable angle.

That is why successful rectification should not be judged by whether the result looks sharper or more convincingly typographic. The better test is whether **competing candidate letters become distinguishable using information demonstrably present in the source**.

Three outcomes are possible. First, rectification may expose a feature already present but geometrically compressed — for example, a diagonal or terminal whose position becomes easier to interpret once normal proportions are restored. That is genuine analytical value. Second, several reasonable correction models may continue to support the same letter identity; agreement across those models increases confidence. Third, small changes in the assumed paper surface may make the character swing between different plausible capitals. In that case the instability itself is evidence that the photograph does not securely determine the letter.

The last outcome is particularly important around folds. Research on dewarping deformed documents relies on estimating page shape from larger structures such as text lines, stroke statistics or local surface patches.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0262885606000904" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A partition approach for the restoration of camera images of planar and curled document - ScienceDirectAugust 1, 2006...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 1, 2006</span></span></span> But in the Ramey image, some of the very structures needed to constrain a correction are themselves poorly resolved. An analyst can therefore face a circular problem: the presumed positions of letters help define the warp, while the resulting warp influences what those letters appear to be.

## The safest comparison is a range, not one corrected image

For the Ramey memo, a single beautifully flattened reconstruction should therefore be treated as a hypothesis about geometry rather than as a recovered original document. The stronger method is to test ambiguous capitals across a range of plausible local corrections.

If a disputed mark is thought to be V, P, R or another capital, candidate glyphs can be subjected to the same estimated compression and degradation rather than relying solely on visual inspection of an enlarged reconstruction. Likewise, the source region can be rectified under slightly different plausible page angles. A robust identification should survive reasonable variation in those assumptions.

This approach separates two questions that are too easily collapsed:

**What does the mark resemble in this particular processed image?**

and

**Which original capital could plausibly have produced the recorded mark across the defensible range of viewing geometries?**

Only the second question properly incorporates foreshortening.

The distinction also fits the broader empirical record of the memo. Houran and Randle found both substantial interpretive variability and limited agreement at some locations, rather than either complete unreadability or universal transcription.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://digital.library.adelaide.edu.au/dspace/handle/2440/3334" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: digital.library.adelaide.edu.au">[Digital Library Adelaide]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">digital.library.adelaide.edu.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on edu.au.</span></span></span> A later review similarly concluded that attempts to decipher the photographed document had not produced definitive results capable of settling competing explanations of Roswell.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journalofscientificexploration.org/index.php/jse/article/view/2635" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journalofscientificexploration.org">[Journal of Scientific Exploration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journalofscientificexploration.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on journalofscientificexploration.org.</span></span></span> Foreshortening helps explain one physical route by which that ambiguity arises, but it does not justify choosing among disputed words by itself.

## The key distinction: distorted information versus missing information

Foreshortening is unusually important because, unlike simple blur, it can sometimes be mathematically reversed. That can create excessive confidence. If a capital looks twice as tall after rectification, it is easy to feel that twice as much information has been recovered. In reality, the transformation has mostly redistributed the information already recorded.

For relatively clear strokes, that redistribution can be extremely useful. It can restore meaningful proportions and prevent a compressed uppercase character from being compared unfairly with an undistorted reference letter. For tiny marks near folds or strongly oblique portions of the Ramey sheet, however, rectification eventually encounters the resolution limit. Once alternative capitals project into photographic patterns that are indistinguishable within grain, focus and sampling uncertainty, geometry alone cannot decide between them.

That is the point at which a fold becomes a genuine missing-information zone rather than merely an inconvenient distortion. Perspective can explain why a capital looks wrong; correction can show how it might have looked under a more frontal view. Neither fact proves which capital originally occupied the paper.

For disputed Ramey memo readings, the most defensible use of foreshortening analysis is therefore eliminative rather than creative. It can rule out naïve comparisons based on uncorrected letter proportions, identify regions where apparent lower-case or malformed shapes may simply be compressed capitals, and test whether a proposed reading survives plausible geometric corrections. Where several capitals remain compatible with the recorded strokes, the correct result is not a more aggressive stretch or enhancement. It is an unresolved capital.

    var template = String(cfg.url_template || 'https://www.ebay.com/sch/i.html?_nkw={query}');
    var url = template.replace('{query}', encodeQuery(query || '')).replace('{query_plain}', String(query || ''));
    var joiner = url.indexOf('?') >= 0 ? '&' : '?';
    var params = [];
    if (cfg.mkrid) params.push('mkrid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.mkrid));
    if (cfg.campaign_id) params.push('campid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.campaign_id));
    params.push('mkevt=1');
    params.push('mkcid=1');
    params.push('toolid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.tool_id || '10001'));
    if (reference) params.push('customid=' + encodeURIComponent(reference).slice(0, 256));
    return url + joiner + params.join('&');
  }
  function domainLabel(marketId) {
    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket] || {};
    return cfg.domain_label || 'eBay';
  }
  function flagClass(marketId) {
    return 'fr-ebay-market-flag fr-ebay-market-flag--' + String(marketId || '').toLowerCase().replace(/_/g, '-').replace(/[^a-z0-9-]+/g, '');
  }
  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
