## Introduction

David [Rudiak]({{ 'rudiak/' | relative_url }})’s proposed reading of the [Ramey memo]({{ 'ramey-memo/' | relative_url }}) is often reduced to one striking word: **“DISC”**. But the Roswell significance attributed to that word depends on substantially more than recognising four letters. Rudiak’s influential reconstruction places it in wording rendered in one published comparison as **“THE ‘DISC’ THEY will ship”**, and elsewhere in publicity for his work as **“in the ‘disc’ they will ship”**. The important claims — that something is *inside* a disc, that it will be *shipped*, and therefore that the disc is a container or vehicle rather than merely a descriptive label — come from those neighbouring words and their grammatical relationship.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>



<figure class="article-media-figure article-media-figure-illustration"><img src="{{ "/assets/images/roswell_UFO_ramey_me_6248d5_disc_reading_917df6_421dc7_rudiak_disc_sentence_f572e8-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Rudiak Sentence illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/roswell_UFO_ramey_me_6248d5_disc_reading_917df6_421dc7_rudiak_disc_sentence_f572e8-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/roswell_UFO_ramey_me_6248d5_disc_reading_917df6_421dc7_rudiak_disc_sentence_f572e8-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" width="1920" height="1080" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low"><figcaption class="article-media-caption"><span class="article-media-caption-label">Explanatory illustration 1</span></figcaption></figure>

That distinction is crucial because the best controlled study of the photograph did **not** independently validate this sentence. It found reproducible agreement on several other pieces of text, notably “Fort Worth TX”, “[story]({{ 'story/' | relative_url }})” and “weather balloons”, but not on “DISC” or Rudiak’s surrounding phrase. The appropriate evidential unit is therefore not “Rudiak read DISC”, but a layered reconstruction in which [uncertain letters]({{ 'uncertain-letters/' | relative_url }}), words, punctuation, syntax and historical context reinforce one another.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

First is the **character-level claim**: that a particular cluster of marks reads “DISC” or “DISK”. Second is the **phrase-level claim**: that nearby marks form something like “THE ‘DISC’ THEY WILL SHIP” or “IN THE ‘DISC’ THEY WILL SHIP”. Third is the **semantic claim**: that this wording means an object or material was located inside a recovered disc and was going to be transported elsewhere.

Those are progressively stronger propositions. Even if “DISC” were accepted, neither the preposition, the verb nor the relationship among the words follows automatically.

The comparison table published by James Houran and Kevin Randle in 2002 makes the problem unusually visible. On the relevant line, their table gives an earlier Rudiak version approximately as “5 PM THE ‘DISC’ they will ship …” and a later Rudiak version beginning “THE ‘DISC’ THEY will ship …”. Don Burleson’s transcription instead reads **“ON THE ‘DISK’ MUST HAVE SENT LOS ALAMOS…”**, while Tom Carey’s reads **“ON THE ‘DISK’ MUST THUS SAVE FOR THE ATOMIC LABORATORY”**. Another listed reading goes no further than **“DISK or DISC”**.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

That comparison matters more than the superficial agreement over “DISC/DISK” might suggest. The analysts were not simply debating whether the last letter was C or K. They disagreed over what came immediately before and after it, and therefore over what the line actually said.

The same table records an especially useful control: Neil Morris’s early transcription of the corresponding area looked more like **“ELSE”**, surrounded by largely unresolved material. That does not establish “ELSE” as the correct word. It shows instead that the visual information is weak enough for quite different letter sequences to emerge from the same region.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

## Which surrounding words create the Roswell meaning?

The distinction between **“DISC”** and **“in the disc they will ship”** is not cosmetic. Most of the sentence’s evidential force comes from the extra words.

Rudiak himself emphasised the fuller phrase. A 2002 newspaper report describing his methods quoted him as defending two key readings: “the victims of the wreck” and “in the ‘disc’ they will ship”. He argued that the latter implied something of value was **inside** the object and destined for air shipment. In that interpretation, “disc” ceases to be merely contemporary terminology for an unidentified flying object and becomes something with an interior containing material or occupants.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufologie.patrickgross.org/press/santafenewmexican22nov2002.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOS at close sight: the newspapers, David Rudiak&#x27;s work on Ramey memo, 2002...</span></span></span>

That is a much stronger Roswell claim than the four-letter reading alone could support. In July 1947, “disc” was already ordinary vocabulary in the flying-saucer story, including the Roswell Army Air Field announcement that it had recovered a “flying disc”. Consequently, finding the word somewhere in a document associated with the episode would be interesting but not self-interpreting. What gives Rudiak’s reconstruction its more dramatic meaning is the proposed grammar surrounding it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

Each component therefore needs its own evidential assessment:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **DISC/DISK** identifies the purported noun but remains disputed at character level.
* **IN** supplies the spatial relationship needed to put something inside the object. Without it, the interpretation changes markedly.
* **THE** makes “disc” appear to refer to a specific object already understood by sender and recipient.
* **Quotation marks** potentially suggest that “disc” was being used as a special or quoted designation, but the photograph contains ambiguous marks and punctuation must itself be established rather than assumed.
* **THEY** introduces an unidentified actor.
* **WILL SHIP** turns the fragment into an intended movement or transfer and supplies much of the argument that something associated with the disc was going elsewhere.

</div>

The semantic conclusion is therefore cumulative. It becomes compelling only if several uncertain readings are correct simultaneously.

A contemporary critique of Rudiak’s interpretation illustrates the grammatical sensitivity. It noted that changing **“in the disc they will ship”** to something resembling **“is the disc they will ship”** produces a fundamentally different proposition: instead of something being transported *inside* the disc, the disc itself becomes the thing being shipped. The critic also questioned the pronoun “they”, observing that a sender describing his own intended action might more naturally write “we”. These objections do not prove an alternative transcription, but they demonstrate why a few adjoining characters carry disproportionate interpretive weight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.astronomyufo.com/UFO/Ramey.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: astronomyufo.com">[Astronomy UFO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">astronomyufo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Astronomy UFOThe Ramey Document: Smoking gun or empty water pistol?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">August 1, 2003...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 1, 2003</span></span></span>

## Independent readings do not recover the sentence

The strongest reason to distinguish visible evidence from reconstruction comes from Houran and Randle’s controlled experiment. They assigned 176 participants to three conditions: one group was told that the image concerned Roswell and a possible extraterrestrial crash, another was told that it concerned secret atomic-bomb testing, and a blind group was simply told that it was an old document. Participants examined enlargements and high-resolution material derived from the [Ramey]({{ 'ramey/' | relative_url }}) photograph.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

The contextual cue changed what people thought they could read. The Roswell-primed and atomic-bomb-primed groups each reported roughly three times as many words as the blind group: averages of 4.6 and 4.8 respectively, against 1.6 in the blind condition. Roswell-primed participants produced Roswell-compatible readings such as “remains”, while the atomic group generated terms including “glasses”, “atomic” and “laboratory”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

Yet some genuine cross-group agreement emerged. The researchers reported common recognition of **“Fort Worth TX”**, **“story”** and **“weather balloons”** in matching locations. “Land” also attracted substantial agreement, although not across all three conditions in the same way. This is important because it demonstrates that the experiment was capable of detecting relatively stable visual information in the photograph.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

“DISC” was not among those independently reproduced terms. Neither was “they will ship”, nor the complete phrase that gives Rudiak’s reading its particular meaning.

That result does not prove that Rudiak’s phrase is wrong. The experimental participants were not specialist image analysts, and Houran and Randle explicitly accepted that portions of the memo might ultimately be decipherable. But it does mean that the fuller DISC sentence lacks the kind of independent convergence obtained for the clearest words elsewhere in the image. Their conclusion was that some text appeared genuinely legible while its broader meaning remained vulnerable to suggestion and weak agreement among interpreters.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

## Reconstruction enters before a complete sentence exists

Rudiak’s methodology was more systematic than simply looking at an enlargement and guessing. According to Houran and Randle, he proposed determining character positions from the document’s spacing, estimating word lengths and requiring candidate readings to obey plausible grammar, syntax and meaning. For badly formed words, he selected possible letters and used computer searches to find words fitting those constraints.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

There is nothing inherently illegitimate about using such constraints. Historians and textual scholars routinely use context when damaged documents are incomplete. The problem is **how much evidential independence remains once context helps choose the letters that are then used to establish the context**.

Houran and Randle identified precisely this risk. They considered Rudiak’s search for grammatically and historically plausible words reasonable in principle, but warned that their experiment showed how changing the supplied context changed what observers perceived. They argued that working backwards from an assumed context could therefore generate words that were not actually present.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

Rudiak also regarded knowledge of the 1947 Roswell story as useful in assembling the text. One example described in the paper concerns the apparent beginning “VA” in the expected address area: knowledge that General Hoyt Vandenberg was involved led Rudiak to test “Vandenberg”, which he then believed the enhanced image supported. Another involved interpreting the memo’s final lines against contemporary publicity concerning weather balloons and [radar targets]({{ 'radar-target/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

Again, contextual reconstruction can be informative. But it produces a different category of evidence from independently identifying characters without knowing what the document is expected to say. A reconstruction may become internally coherent precisely because candidate words have been selected for their ability to fit one another.

## Punctuation adds another interpretive layer

The quotation marks around **“DISC”** deserve particular caution because they can quietly influence how the sentence is understood. Rudiak regarded apparent quotation marks around “disk” and around other unusual words as evidence of stylistic consistency in the message. Houran and Randle regarded that as a reasonable possibility but warned that extracting the document’s general context from unverified unusual word usage risked circular interpretation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

Their experiment did find that participants perceived punctuation in the document, so punctuation-like marks are not simply an invention of one transcription. What it did not establish was that particular marks surrounding the disputed word were reliably quotation marks enclosing a securely identified “DISC”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

This distinction matters because printed [transcriptions]({{ 'transcriptions/' | relative_url }}) make uncertain photographic features look much firmer than they are. Once rendered cleanly as **THE “DISC” THEY WILL SHIP**, quotation marks, spaces, word boundaries and capital letters visually imply a finished sentence. The source photograph supplies none of that typographical certainty. A transcription necessarily converts blurred tonal marks into discrete characters and punctuation, and every such conversion can increase apparent confidence.

## Where reconstruction outruns visible character evidence

The fairest assessment is therefore not that Rudiak “invented a sentence”, nor that the photograph straightforwardly contains a readable statement about shipping something inside a disc. The evidence lies between those extremes.

There are real text-like structures on the photographed paper. Multiple investigators have recovered some words independently, and the controlled study found enough agreement to reject the idea that the entire document is simply unreadable noise. But the same research also showed that observers’ expectations influence how much text they believe they can decipher and what that text appears to say.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

For Rudiak’s DISC sentence specifically, confidence should decrease as the claim expands:

**Possible “DISC/DISK” → proposed neighbouring words → proposed grammar → reconstructed sentence → Roswell interpretation.**

The first stage is itself contested. The later stages add information that cannot be obtained from the four disputed letters alone. Most importantly, the claim that something was **inside** a disc depends on accepting “IN” and the syntax around it; the claim that it was destined for transport depends on accepting “THEY WILL SHIP”. Neither proposition survives merely by granting “DISC”.

This is why the Houran-Randle comparison table is more informative than quoting Rudiak’s finished sentence in isolation. It preserves the disagreement that polished transcriptions conceal: different investigators could obtain “DISC/DISK” in approximately the same area yet construct sharply different surrounding wording.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

## What can safely be said about Rudiak’s sentence?

Rudiak’s reading is best described as a **context-assisted reconstruction of a poorly resolved line**, not a verbatim sentence independently recoverable from the photograph. His method attempted to constrain guesses using spacing, word length, grammar, historical information and comparisons with other portions of the memo. Those techniques can help reconstruct damaged text, but in this case the underlying image is sufficiently ambiguous that contextual assumptions can also feed back into character identification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

The controlled evidence gives a useful benchmark. Words such as “Fort Worth”, “story” and “weather balloons” achieved cross-condition recognition; the disputed DISC sentence did not. Houran and Randle consequently recommended that future conclusions be based on standardised [enhancement]({{ 'enhancement/' | relative_url }}) and multiple independent, blind laboratories, so that agreement on exact words and positions could be measured without analysts first being primed with the Roswell interpretation. They explicitly cautioned that earlier analyses generally lacked such standardisation and blinding.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

The practical takeaway is that claims about the memo should preserve these evidential layers rather than collapsing them. Saying **“Rudiak transcribes a passage as referring to ‘DISC’ and shipping”** accurately describes his interpretation. Saying **“the memo says something was shipped inside the disc”** converts that interpretation into an established textual fact.

That gap is the central issue. The Roswell significance attributed to Rudiak’s DISC passage comes not merely from a disputed four-letter word but from a chain of reconstructed neighbouring words, punctuation and grammar. Until those elements can be recovered independently from the photographic evidence, the complete sentence remains a proposed reading whose meaning is considerably more certain on the printed transcription page than its individual components are in the original image.

    if (!cfg) return '#';
    var template = String(cfg.url_template || 'https://www.ebay.com/sch/i.html?_nkw={query}');
    var url = template.replace('{query}', encodeQuery(query || '')).replace('{query_plain}', String(query || ''));
    var joiner = url.indexOf('?') >= 0 ? '&' : '?';
    var params = [];
    if (cfg.mkrid) params.push('mkrid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.mkrid));
    if (cfg.campaign_id) params.push('campid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.campaign_id));
    params.push('mkevt=1');
    params.push('mkcid=1');
    params.push('toolid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.tool_id || '10001'));
    if (reference) params.push('customid=' + encodeURIComponent(reference).slice(0, 256));
    return url + joiner + params.join('&');
  }
  function domainLabel(marketId) {
    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket] || {};
    return cfg.domain_label || 'eBay';
  }
  function flagClass(marketId) {
    return 'fr-ebay-market-flag fr-ebay-market-flag--' + String(marketId || '').toLowerCase().replace(/_/g, '-').replace(/[^a-z0-9-]+/g, '');
  }
  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
