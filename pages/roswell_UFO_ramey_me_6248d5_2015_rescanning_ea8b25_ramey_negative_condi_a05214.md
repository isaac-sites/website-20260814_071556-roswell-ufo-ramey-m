## Introduction

The 2015 inspection of the original photographic [negative]({{ 'negative/' | relative_url }}) behind the [Ramey memo]({{ 'ramey-memo/' | relative_url }}) produced an important negative finding: the film was **not badly deteriorated in the part containing the memo**. Investigators found ordinary signs of an ageing, historically used newspaper negative — minor chemical staining, dust and scratches — but reported no significant degradation that had destroyed the memo image. They also judged the original exposure adequate, found no evident camera or subject movement, and concluded that the memo fell within the photographer's zone of focus.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journalofscientificexploration.org/index.php/jse/article/download/2635/1631" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journalofscientificexploration.org">[Journal of Scientific Exploration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journalofscientificexploration.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Journal of Scientific Exploration Anomalistics</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Journal of Scientific ExplorationAnomalisticsAugust 21, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 21, 2022</span></span></span>

## Dust, stains and scratches were present — but not decisive

The April 2015 work at the University of Texas at Arlington (UTA) involved direct examination of the original Fort Worth Star-Telegram negative rather than another attempt to enhance a reproduction. The negatives are preserved in UTA Libraries' Special Collections as part of the newspaper's photographic archive. UTA has confirmed that the original 8 July 1947 negatives are held there and that it subsequently worked with Kevin Randle, David [Rudiak]({{ 'rudiak/' | relative_url }}), Simon Schollum and Jerry Morelock on the [Ramey]({{ 'ramey/' | relative_url }})-memo research project.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/spcouta/36080122212" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: flickr.com">[Flickr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">flickr.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Roswell UFO Incident, July 1947 &#124;...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 1947</span></span></span>

This direct access made it possible to separate two questions that can easily be confused: whether the negative had acquired defects during its long life, and whether those defects were severe enough to explain the memo's illegibility.

The answer to the first question was yes. The published account incorporating Simon Schollum's 2015 technical report records **minor chemical stains, dust particles and scratches**, describing them as consistent with the negative's age and history of handling. Randle's contemporary account similarly acknowledged scratches and some accumulated dirt from handling and previous examination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journalofscientificexploration.org/index.php/jse/article/download/2635/1631" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journalofscientificexploration.org">[Journal of Scientific Exploration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journalofscientificexploration.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Journal of Scientific Exploration Anomalistics</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Journal of Scientific ExplorationAnomalisticsAugust 21, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 21, 2022</span></span></span>

The second question produced a different answer. Schollum reported that the densities in the photographic emulsion appeared well fixed and that there was no significant damage or degradation specifically affecting the memo area. In other words, visible ageing existed, but the inspection did not find evidence that seven decades of deterioration had erased the crucial information researchers were seeking.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journalofscientificexploration.org/index.php/jse/article/download/2635/1631" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journalofscientificexploration.org">[Journal of Scientific Exploration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journalofscientificexploration.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Journal of Scientific Exploration Anomalistics</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Journal of Scientific ExplorationAnomalisticsAugust 21, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 21, 2022</span></span></span>

Some physical defects were also consistent with the negative's original working life. The report noted that newspaper photographers operated under tight deadlines that could entail less-than-ideal processing and drying before pictures were printed. Damage compatible with those practices was visible on the negatives. That observation puts the blemishes in context: not every mark necessarily resulted from decades in archival storage, and the existence of marks did not by itself establish serious loss of image information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journalofscientificexploration.org/index.php/jse/article/download/2635/1631" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journalofscientificexploration.org">[Journal of Scientific Exploration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journalofscientificexploration.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Journal of Scientific Exploration Anomalistics</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Journal of Scientific ExplorationAnomalisticsAugust 21, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 21, 2022</span></span></span>

This distinction is particularly important because earlier researchers had already complained about dirt and debris on the source material. A 2002 study by James Houran and Kevin Randle noted Stanton Friedman's concern that years of handling had allowed dirt and debris to accumulate on the negatives. The 2015 direct inspection therefore addressed a genuine pre-existing concern rather than inventing a new explanation after the scans failed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://noufors.com/Documents/Books%2C%20Manuals%20and%20Published%20Papers/Specialty%20UFO%20Publications/Journal%20of%20Scientific%20Exploration/jse_16_1_houran.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: noufors.com">[Noufors]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">noufors.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Noufors&#x27;&#x27;A Message in a Bottle:&quot; Confounds in Deciphering the Ramey Memo from the Roswell UFO CaseMay 23, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 23, 2026</span></span></span>

## Why focus and exposure were judged adequate

Ageing was only one possible way photographic information could have been lost. Even a perfectly preserved negative would offer little hope if the original 1947 photograph had been badly exposed, blurred by movement or focused on the wrong plane. The 2015 examination specifically considered those possibilities.

Schollum's findings were favourable on all three points. The flash exposure was judged adequate. Investigators found no evident subject movement or camera shake. Critical portions of the scene were described as well focused, with the memo itself falling within the focus zone established by the photographer's lens aperture.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journalofscientificexploration.org/index.php/jse/article/download/2635/1631" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journalofscientificexploration.org">[Journal of Scientific Exploration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journalofscientificexploration.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Journal of Scientific Exploration Anomalistics</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Journal of Scientific ExplorationAnomalisticsAugust 21, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 21, 2022</span></span></span>

That conclusion should not be overstated. Saying that the memo was **within the focus zone** does not mean that its tiny type was recorded with enough resolution to be read character by character. Focus and resolution are different constraints. A small object can be correctly focused yet occupy so little film area that its finest details never become distinguishable.

The 2015 measurements illustrate precisely that problem. In the published account, a digitisation of the complete 4-by-5-inch frame measured about 3,663 by 2,743 pixels, while the memo's message occupied only about 148.5 pixels in width in that particular representation. Investigators therefore used a Nikon SMZ1500 microscope and other [imaging methods]({{ 'imaging-methods/' | relative_url }}) to examine the memo region more closely.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journalofscientificexploration.org/index.php/jse/article/download/2635/1631" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journalofscientificexploration.org">[Journal of Scientific Exploration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journalofscientificexploration.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Journal of Scientific Exploration Anomalistics</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Journal of Scientific ExplorationAnomalisticsAugust 21, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 21, 2022</span></span></span>

The physical condition assessment consequently changed the diagnosis. The problem was not principally that a once-sharp, information-rich memo image had subsequently been ruined. The report instead characterised the negatives as relatively good in condition, well exposed, processed and professionally preserved, before identifying **scale** as the main obstacle to reading the text.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journalofscientificexploration.org/index.php/jse/article/download/2635/1631" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journalofscientificexploration.org">[Journal of Scientific Exploration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journalofscientificexploration.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Journal of Scientific Exploration Anomalistics</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Journal of Scientific ExplorationAnomalisticsAugust 21, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 21, 2022</span></span></span>

## What the condition findings ruled out

The 2015 inspection did not prove that the Ramey memo could be deciphered, nor did it validate any disputed transcription. What it did was eliminate or substantially weaken several mundane explanations for why researchers had failed to read it reliably.

**Severe archival deterioration was not supported.** The memo area showed no significant damage or degradation according to Schollum's inspection. Ordinary age-related defects were present, but not at a level judged sufficient to account for the central readability problem.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journalofscientificexploration.org/index.php/jse/article/download/2635/1631" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journalofscientificexploration.org">[Journal of Scientific Exploration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journalofscientificexploration.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Journal of Scientific Exploration Anomalistics</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Journal of Scientific ExplorationAnomalisticsAugust 21, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 21, 2022</span></span></span>

**Gross exposure failure was not supported.** The flash exposure was assessed as adequate. There was therefore no indication that the memo had disappeared into a fundamentally unusable over- or under-exposed frame.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journalofscientificexploration.org/index.php/jse/article/download/2635/1631" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journalofscientificexploration.org">[Journal of Scientific Exploration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journalofscientificexploration.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Journal of Scientific Exploration Anomalistics</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Journal of Scientific ExplorationAnomalisticsAugust 21, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 21, 2022</span></span></span>

**Motion blur was not identified as the culprit.** No evident camera or subject shake was reported. This matters because motion blur could otherwise smear letter strokes before the image ever reached the negative, creating an irreversible loss that no later scanning resolution could repair.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journalofscientificexploration.org/index.php/jse/article/download/2635/1631" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journalofscientificexploration.org">[Journal of Scientific Exploration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journalofscientificexploration.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Journal of Scientific Exploration Anomalistics</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Journal of Scientific ExplorationAnomalisticsAugust 21, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 21, 2022</span></span></span>

**Simple misfocus was not supported either.** The memo was judged to lie within the photograph's focus zone. Again, that did not make its characters readable, but it removed the straightforward explanation that the photographer had simply focused elsewhere and left the document optically blurred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journalofscientificexploration.org/index.php/jse/article/download/2635/1631" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journalofscientificexploration.org">[Journal of Scientific Exploration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journalofscientificexploration.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Journal of Scientific Exploration Anomalistics</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Journal of Scientific ExplorationAnomalisticsAugust 21, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 21, 2022</span></span></span>

These findings also help explain why merely producing a cleaner scan could not guarantee a breakthrough. The researchers did obtain improved digital material. Contemporary reporting by Randle says dozens of scans were made under differing conditions, and the eventual collection amounted to roughly 27 gigabytes. Yet he acknowledged later in 2015 that the scans, although improved over earlier material, had not produced the hoped-for clarity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/09/ramey-memo-update.html?showComment=1443576324320" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: Ramey Memo Update</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: Ramey Memo Update</span></span></span>

## A preserved negative can still contain too little detail

The counter-intuitive result of the 2015 inspection is that **good preservation did not imply recoverable writing**. Once serious deterioration, bad exposure, motion and gross misfocus had been pushed down the list of explanations, the physical scale of the memo became more important.

Photographic film records an image through microscopic grains in its emulsion. If a typed character occupies only a tiny area of the negative, its strokes can approach the same scale as the grain structure used to record them. At that point, higher-resolution scanning can reveal the film itself with increasing fidelity without necessarily recovering additional letter information. The later published account of Schollum's work explicitly identified the relationship between character dimensions and the size and distribution of the film's silver-halide grains as the principal determinant of whether individual letter forms could be recognised.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journalofscientificexploration.org/index.php/jse/article/download/2635/1631" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journalofscientificexploration.org">[Journal of Scientific Exploration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journalofscientificexploration.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Journal of Scientific Exploration Anomalistics</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Journal of Scientific ExplorationAnomalisticsAugust 21, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 21, 2022</span></span></span>

That is why the film-condition finding is central to understanding the **failed breakthrough of 2015**. The negative had scratches, dirt and chemical blemishes, but investigators did not discover a ruined artefact whose original information had simply decayed away. Nor did they find a badly exposed or obviously blurred photograph that explained everything.

They found something more frustrating: a reasonably preserved photographic record in which the object of interest had been captured adequately but **too small for the surviving film information to resolve much of its type unambiguously**. The [2015 rescanning]({{ '2015-scans/' | relative_url }}) could therefore improve access to what the negative actually contained, but it could not manufacture letter detail that the 1947 photograph may never have recorded distinctly enough in the first place.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journalofscientificexploration.org/index.php/jse/article/download/2635/1631" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journalofscientificexploration.org">[Journal of Scientific Exploration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journalofscientificexploration.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Journal of Scientific Exploration Anomalistics</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Journal of Scientific ExplorationAnomalisticsAugust 21, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 21, 2022</span></span></span>

That interpretation also fits the outcome reported by the researchers themselves. The new scans were better, but they did not yield the clear, consensual reading they had hoped modern imaging would provide. UTA's own later description continued to characterise the words in Ramey's hand as obscured, while noting continuing attempts to decipher them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/11/the-ramey-memo-scans.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: The Ramey Memo Scans</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: The Ramey Memo Scans</span></span></span>

The most defensible conclusion from the 2015 film inspection is therefore narrow but useful: **the Ramey negative was not too damaged, badly exposed or obviously out of focus to explain the memo's unreadability on those grounds alone**. The inspection shifted the limiting factor away from preservation and towards the amount of textual detail physically recorded in the original image — a much harder limitation for subsequent enhancement to overcome.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journalofscientificexploration.org/index.php/jse/article/download/2635/1631" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journalofscientificexploration.org">[Journal of Scientific Exploration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journalofscientificexploration.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Journal of Scientific Exploration Anomalistics</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Journal of Scientific ExplorationAnomalisticsAugust 21, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 21, 2022</span></span></span>
