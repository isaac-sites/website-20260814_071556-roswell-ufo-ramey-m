## Introduction

J. [Bond Johnson]({{ 'johnson/' | relative_url }})’s changing recollection of what Brigadier General Roger Ramey said in Fort Worth is a small but important credibility problem in the Roswell record. In Johnson’s March 1989 interview with researcher Kevin Randle, he remembered being briefed while photographing Ramey that the supposed flying disc was actually a crashed weather balloon. The contemporary newspaper record points in the same direction: reports said Ramey identified the material as a weather device, with weather officer Irving [Newton]({{ 'newton/' | relative_url }}) confirming the identification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2009/10/ufos-ramey-memo-j-bond-johnson-and-me.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[blogspot.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: UFOs, the Ramey Memo, J. Bond Johnson and MeOctober 9, 2009...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 9, 2009</span></span></span>

Later, however, Johnson rejected his earlier recollection and portrayed [Ramey]({{ 'ramey/' | relative_url }}) as uncertain — even remembering a dismissive response equivalent to “How the hell should I know?” A 1997 radio appearance presented essentially that later version.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2009/10/ufos-ramey-memo-j-bond-johnson-and-me.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: UFOs, the Ramey Memo, J. Bond Johnson and MeOctober 9, 2009...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 9, 2009</span></span></span>

The issue is not whether the weather-balloon explanation was ultimately correct. It is narrower: **what did Johnson actually hear from Ramey, and how much [confidence]({{ 'confidence/' | relative_url }}) should be placed in a recollection that changed substantially decades after the event?**

## What Johnson told Kevin Randle in 1989

When Randle interviewed Johnson in March 1989, Johnson gave a relatively straightforward account of his visit to Ramey’s office. He recalled being dispatched from the *Fort Worth Star-Telegram* after news arrived that material connected with the Roswell flying-disc report was being brought to Fort Worth. He drove to the airfield with his Speed Graphic camera, entered Ramey’s office and photographed the debris spread across the floor.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2009/10/ufos-ramey-memo-j-bond-johnson-and-me.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: UFOs, the Ramey Memo, J. Bond Johnson and MeOctober 9, 2009...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 9, 2009</span></span></span>

The significant part is Johnson’s description of what he understood while he was there. In the recorded recollection, he said that when he posed Ramey with the debris he was briefed that it was not the flying disc first reported but a weather balloon that had crashed. Randle's later reproduction of the interview preserves this wording, and a subsequent scholarly treatment of the Roswell testimony quotes the same passage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2009/10/ufos-ramey-memo-j-bond-johnson-and-me.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: UFOs, the Ramey Memo, J. Bond Johnson and MeOctober 9, 2009...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 9, 2009</span></span></span>

That statement does not necessarily prove that **Ramey personally** delivered every part of the briefing. Johnson later acknowledged that the public information officer might have briefed him. That distinction matters when reconstructing the precise conversation. But it still places the weather-balloon explanation inside Johnson's remembered visit rather than as information he first encountered long afterwards. Randle's chronology also records Johnson saying that he spent perhaps 20 minutes at the office before leaving.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2013/01/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: January 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 2013</span></span></span>

This early recollection therefore creates a problem for the stronger later claim that Ramey himself remained baffled during Johnson's photography. The two memories are not merely different emphases. One describes Johnson being briefed that the debris was a weather balloon while he was there; the other moves the weather-balloon explanation later and substitutes uncertainty at the crucial encounter with Ramey.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2009/10/ufos-ramey-memo-j-bond-johnson-and-me.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: UFOs, the Ramey Memo, J. Bond Johnson and MeOctober 9, 2009...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 9, 2009</span></span></span>

The evidential distinction should nevertheless be kept precise. Randle is both the interviewer who preserved Johnson's early statement and a Roswell researcher with his own interpretation of the case. His conclusion about Johnson's reliability is therefore an argument, not independent proof. What makes the discrepancy important is that the taped statement can be compared with evidence created in 1947 rather than judged solely through Randle's interpretation.

## What the contemporary newspaper account reported

The newspaper record strongly favours the proposition that Ramey was publicly presenting a weather-device identification in Fort Worth, rather than openly telling reporters that he had no idea what the material was.

The *Fort Worth Star-Telegram* report of 9 July described the New Mexico find as having been identified at Fort Worth Army Air Field the previous night as a “weather balloon-kite”. More specifically, it reported that Ramey and his chief of staff, Colonel Thomas Dubose, identified the material as a weather device used by the Army Air Forces. Newton, the Fort Worth weather forecaster summoned to inspect it, then identified the object as a Rawin radar target carried by a balloon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://priory-of-sion.com/biblios/links/fort.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: priory-of-sion.com">[priory-of-sion.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">priory-of-sion.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">New Mexico Rancher&#x27;s &#x27;Flying Disk&#x27; Proves To Be Weather Balloon-Kite</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Mexico Rancher&#x27;s &#x27;Flying Disk&#x27; Proves To Be Weather Balloon-Kite</span></span></span>

Another version of the contemporary account stated the sequence even more directly: after looking at the material, Ramey declared that it was a weather balloon and the weather officer verified his judgement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/rw/p/amarillodailynews9jul1947.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOS at close sight: Roswell 1947 - 1947 Press, Roswell debris explained, The Amarillo Daily News, July 9, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 9, 1947</span></span></span>

That is important because it gives the dispute a contemporary anchor. It does **not** establish that the military identification was truthful, nor does it settle arguments over whether the photographed material was the material recovered near Roswell. Those are separate Roswell controversies. What it establishes for this narrower question is that Ramey's recorded public position at the time was one of identification, not bafflement.

Newton's later account fits that sequence in an interesting way. Randle's chronology says Newton remembered being summoned to Ramey's office and being told beforehand that the Roswell officers thought they had found a flying saucer but that “the general thinks it’s a weather balloon” and wanted Newton to examine it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2013/01/the-roswell-time-line-july-8-1947.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: The Roswell Time Line</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: The Roswell Time Line - July 8, 1947January 19, 2013</span></span></span> Although Newton's own recollections also developed inconsistencies over the decades, this particular point independently resembles the 1947 press narrative: Ramey had reached, or was presenting, a balloon explanation before Newton supplied the specialist identification.

A modern *Star-Telegram* retrospective likewise describes Ramey as showing the debris to Johnson and claiming it was only a weather balloon. It notes that the newspaper's headline presented the Roswell “disk” as identified as a weather balloon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.star-telegram.com/opinion/bud-kennedy/article289713499.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: star-telegram.com">[Fort Worth Star-Telegram]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">star-telegram.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fort Worth Star-Telegram‘Roswell Incident’ UFO crash in 1947 stirred Fort Worth &#124; Fort Worth Star-Telegram...</span></span></span> This modern account is not as valuable as the 1947 reporting for reconstructing the event, but it confirms how the newspaper's surviving institutional record understands its original coverage.

There is therefore an unusually useful hierarchy of evidence here. Johnson's 1989 recollection agrees broadly with the contemporary reporting; his later recollection conflicts with both. That does not automatically make the later version false, because contemporary newspaper stories can contain errors and official statements can be misleading. It does, however, mean that accepting the later version requires explaining why Johnson's earlier recorded memory and the near-contemporary written record both point the other way.

## How the 1997 version changed the scene

Johnson did not simply become less certain with age. His later account changed the dramatic meaning of his encounter with Ramey.

When Randle subsequently confronted Johnson with the 1989 recording, Johnson disputed what he had previously said. In the later exchange reproduced by Randle, Johnson remembered asking Ramey what the material was and recalled Ramey shrugging and answering, in effect, “How the hell should I know?” Randle responded by reading Johnson's earlier weather-balloon statement back to him. Johnson maintained that the earlier version could not have been right, although he acknowledged that he had not made his own recording of the original conversation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2009/10/ufos-ramey-memo-j-bond-johnson-and-me.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: UFOs, the Ramey Memo, J. Bond Johnson and MeOctober 9, 2009...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 9, 2009</span></span></span>

The revised version also shifted the timing. Johnson now suggested that there had been no balloon “cover [story]({{ 'story/' | relative_url }})” when he returned to the newspaper and that Ramey's weather-balloon explanation emerged later. That directly clashes with his earlier statement that he had already been briefed about the weather balloon while posing Ramey with the debris.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2009/10/ufos-ramey-memo-j-bond-johnson-and-me.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: UFOs, the Ramey Memo, J. Bond Johnson and MeOctober 9, 2009...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 9, 2009</span></span></span>

By July 1997, this changed memory had become part of the public Roswell narrative. On *Coast to Coast AM*, host Art Bell introduced Johnson by saying that he had handled and arranged the debris and had asked Ramey what it was, with Ramey supposedly replying that they did not know. Jaime Shandera then repeated essentially the same chronology immediately before Johnson joined the programme.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fight.fudgie.org/search/show/ab/episode/19970702_Wed" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fight.fudgie.org">[Fight]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fight.fudgie.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Fight Art Bell</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Art Bell - Coast to Coast AM with Art Bell - Jamie Shandera - Roswell Crash - Transcript...</span></span></span>

That broadcast is particularly useful because it shows how the later recollection functioned rhetorically. In the 1989 version, Johnson enters an office where an explanation is already being supplied: the object is being presented as a weather balloon. In the 1997 presentation, Johnson arrives before that certainty exists and encounters a commanding general who supposedly cannot identify the material in front of him. The second scene naturally makes the later balloon announcement look more like a subsequently devised explanation.

Yet it is precisely the more dramatic scene that has the weaker chronological support.

## Did Ramey sound certain or baffled to Johnson?

The safest conclusion is narrower than either side of the larger Roswell argument might prefer. **The surviving evidence supports saying that Johnson originally remembered being given the weather-balloon explanation during his visit to Ramey's office; years later, he remembered Ramey as uncertain and moved the balloon explanation later in the sequence.**

Three considerations favour giving greater weight to the earlier version.

First, the 1989 statement was already made roughly 42 years after the event, but it was still earlier than Johnson's revised accounts. With retrospective testimony, an account recorded closer to the event is generally preferable when later versions become more elaborate or contradictory, unless independent evidence supports the revision.

Second, Johnson's original recollection matches the basic contemporary documentary record. The 1947 *Star-Telegram* coverage described Ramey as identifying the debris as a weather device and Newton as positively identifying the radar target. Another contemporary formulation explicitly said Ramey called it a weather balloon after his first look.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://priory-of-sion.com/biblios/links/fort.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: priory-of-sion.com">[priory-of-sion.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">priory-of-sion.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">New Mexico Rancher&#x27;s &#x27;Flying Disk&#x27; Proves To Be Weather Balloon-Kite</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Mexico Rancher&#x27;s &#x27;Flying Disk&#x27; Proves To Be Weather Balloon-Kite</span></span></span>

Third, the contradiction is unusually direct because Randle had recorded Johnson. This is not simply a dispute between two researchers remembering what a witness once told them. When Johnson later denied that he would have said he was briefed about a weather balloon during the visit, Randle could quote the earlier interview back to him. The published transcript preserves the exchange.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2009/10/ufos-ramey-memo-j-bond-johnson-and-me.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: UFOs, the Ramey Memo, J. Bond Johnson and MeOctober 9, 2009...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 9, 2009</span></span></span>

None of this proves that Ramey's balloon explanation accurately described whatever had originally been recovered in New Mexico. A witness can accurately remember being told something that was itself false. The credibility question here is therefore independent of the ultimate Roswell question. Even someone who believes the balloon explanation was a cover story can recognise that the available evidence indicates Johnson heard, or at least remembered hearing, that explanation earlier than his later accounts allowed.

That distinction is especially important for the Ramey memo debate. Johnson is sometimes valuable because he was physically present and photographed the document in Ramey's hand. But physical presence does not make every recollection equally reliable. Where Johnson's later narrative supplies information invisible in the photograph — what Ramey said, when he said it and whether he appeared mystified — the conflict between Johnson's own versions becomes a reason for caution.

## What the contradiction does — and does not — establish

Johnson's changing story should not be used as a shortcut to deciding the entire Roswell case. It neither proves that the photographed wreckage was genuinely the Roswell material nor proves that it was substituted material. It also does not establish who first devised the weather-balloon explanation.

What it does establish is a limitation on Johnson as a retrospective narrator. His memories evolved on a point central to the interpretation of the scene. The earlier Johnson remembered being briefed about a weather balloon while photographing Ramey; the later Johnson remembered Ramey unable to identify the debris and placed the balloon explanation afterwards. The 1947 newspaper record is considerably more compatible with the first version.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2009/10/ufos-ramey-memo-j-bond-johnson-and-me.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: UFOs, the Ramey Memo, J. Bond Johnson and MeOctober 9, 2009...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 9, 2009</span></span></span>

That makes Johnson's testimony most useful when separated into two categories. His presence in Ramey's office and role as photographer are independently anchored by the surviving photographs and newspaper record. His decades-later reconstruction of dialogue and sequence is much less secure. The distinction is visible even in archival descriptions of the surviving imagery: the photograph of Ramey holding the document is preserved as Johnson's work associated with the *Star-Telegram*'s July 1947 coverage, independently of whatever Johnson later remembered about the conversation surrounding it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AGeneral_Ramey_with_Roswell_Memo.png" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">File:General Ramey with Roswell Memo.png</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">File:General Ramey with Roswell Memo.png</span></span></span>

For readers assessing Johnson's recollections versus the photograph, that is the central lesson. The photograph is a surviving 1947 artefact. The weather-balloon reporting is a surviving 1947 documentary record. Johnson's memories are testimony recorded more than four decades later and subsequently revised. Where those layers disagree, the later recollection that makes Ramey appear baffled deserves less evidential weight than the earlier recorded statement and the contemporary account.
