## Introduction

That sequence is important because it separates two questions that are often blurred together. The photograph containing the sheet is a genuine product of the 1947 Fort Worth episode; whether researchers can reliably read the tiny photographed text, or establish who created the document, is much less certain. Johnson’s later recollections about where the paper came from actually complicate that second question rather than settling it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=e162b50a3885a28c826cb31c3deca69abaaba9ee&amp;repid=rep1&amp;type=pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: citeseerx.ist.psu.edu">[CiteSeerX]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">citeseerx.ist.psu.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Journal of Scientific Exploration, Vol. 16, No. 1, pp. 45–66, 2002October 19, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 19, 2025</span></span></span>

## How the six Fort Worth frames fit together

The memo photograph was not an isolated image that appeared decades after Roswell became famous. Johnson was dispatched as part of an ordinary newspaper assignment, used a 4-by-5-inch Speed Graphic camera, photographed the material in [Ramey]({{ 'ramey/' | relative_url }})’s office and returned to the newsroom. The *Star-Telegram*’s retrospective account says he shot six frames of Ramey and other officers with the material. Johnson himself recalled being sent because he had his camera available when his editor received the developing [story]({{ 'story/' | relative_url }}) over the news wire.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.star-telegram.com/opinion/bud-kennedy/article289713499.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: star-telegram.com">[Fort Worth Star-Telegram]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">star-telegram.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fort Worth Star-Telegram‘Roswell Incident’ UFO crash in 1947 stirred Fort Worth &#124; Fort Worth Star-TelegramJuly 5, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 5, 2024</span></span></span>

The surviving Fort Worth photographs show several arrangements of people and debris rather than one carefully composed scene. Ramey appears with the material; Colonel Thomas DuBose appears with him in other views; Major Jesse Marcel is photographed handling pieces of debris. That repeated setting is evidentially useful. Walls, curtains, furniture, uniforms and the material on the floor provide contextual relationships between frames that would have to be explained if the memo-bearing photograph were alleged to be a later fabrication.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.star-telegram.com/opinion/bud-kennedy/article289713499.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: star-telegram.com">[Fort Worth Star-Telegram]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">star-telegram.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fort Worth Star-Telegram‘Roswell Incident’ UFO crash in 1947 stirred Fort Worth &#124; Fort Worth Star-TelegramJuly 5, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 5, 2024</span></span></span>

There are nevertheless wrinkles in reconstructing precisely who exposed every frame. Later researchers disputed whether Johnson personally took the two photographs of Marcel. Kevin Randle, who interviewed Johnson and subsequently investigated the negatives, argued that the photographs’ archival grouping, photographic characteristics and contemporary chronology favoured Johnson as the photographer, while acknowledging that Johnson’s later accounts became inconsistent. One reported reconstruction based on sunlight and shadows had placed the Marcel images earlier, but Randle disputed the resulting attribution.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/11/the-timing-of-roswell-photographs.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on blogspot.com.</span></span></span>

That disagreement is worth retaining rather than smoothing away. It shows the difference between **sequence provenance** and a perfect reconstruction of every exposure. The evidence does not require Johnson’s decades-later memory to be correct about the order or authorship of every frame. What matters for [memo authenticity]({{ 'authenticity/' | relative_url }}) is that the Ramey photographs belong to a contemporary *Star-Telegram* photographic record whose existence can be traced independently of later UFO research.

UTA’s catalogue provides the strongest institutional anchor. Its Special Collections description identifies the relevant Roswell photograph as *Fort Worth Star-Telegram Collection* item “AR406-6 07/08/1947 #2026, scan 10000684”, states that Johnson created the original negatives on 8 July 1947 and says those negatives are held by UTA. The archive itself also describes the sheet in Ramey’s hand as a memo that researchers have repeatedly studied but that “has yet to be definitively deciphered”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/spcouta/36080122212" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: flickr.com">[Flickr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">flickr.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Roswell UFO Incident, July 1947 &#124;...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 1947</span></span></span>

## What the assignment record establishes

Johnson’s original recollection of how he received the assignment is unusually valuable because parts of it can be checked against records outside his memory. In a recorded 1989 interview reproduced by Roswell researcher Kevin Randle, Johnson said he returned to the *Star-Telegram* newsroom late on 8 July when city editor Cullum Greene asked whether he had his camera. Johnson said an Associated Press — or possibly another wire-service — alert had reported that material from Roswell was being flown to Ramey’s headquarters. Greene sent him to photograph it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/11/j-bond-johnsons-statement-on-his-visit.html?showComment=1447183915509" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on blogspot.com.</span></span></span>

The modern *Star-Telegram* account broadly matches that core story: Johnson happened to be in the newsroom, Greene sent him to the airfield, and Johnson got the job because the regular photographers were unavailable and he possessed a Speed Graphic camera.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.star-telegram.com/opinion/bud-kennedy/article289713499.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: star-telegram.com">[Fort Worth Star-Telegram]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">star-telegram.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fort Worth Star-Telegram‘Roswell Incident’ UFO crash in 1947 stirred Fort Worth &#124; Fort Worth Star-TelegramJuly 5, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 5, 2024</span></span></span>

More importantly, the photographs escaped the newsroom’s internal chain almost immediately. Randle reports recovering documentation accompanying the photographs that identified the date as 8 July 1947, the photographer as J. Bond Johnson and a transmission time of 11:59 p.m. A scholarly examination by James Houran and Randle likewise reported that a copy traced through the Bettmann Photo Archives carried information identifying Johnson as photographer and recording an 11:59 p.m. transmission.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/11/j-bond-johnsons-statement-on-his-visit.html?showComment=1447183915509" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on blogspot.com.</span></span></span>

Contemporary publication supplies another independent chronological anchor. A Ramey photograph appeared with the *Star-Telegram*’s 9 July coverage, “One Disk Tale Exploded by Army as Others Fly”. A surviving reproduction identifies Johnson and that newspaper issue as its source.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AGeneral_Ramey_with_Roswell_Memo.png" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Commons File:General Ramey with Roswell Memo.png</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:General Ramey with Roswell Memo.png - Wikimedia CommonsJuly 9, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 9, 1947</span></span></span>

Taken together, those strands make a modern insertion hypothesis extremely difficult to sustain. To argue that the memo-bearing photographic evidence was manufactured much later, one would have to account not merely for a digital file but for the *Star-Telegram* collection, the original-format negatives, contemporary or near-contemporary photographic transmission records and publication during the Roswell news cycle. The reasonable conclusion is therefore narrow but strong: **Ramey really was photographed in July 1947 holding the text-bearing sheet that became known as the Ramey memo.**<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/spcouta/36080122212" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: flickr.com">[Flickr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">flickr.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Roswell UFO Incident, July 1947 &#124;...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 1947</span></span></span>

## Johnson's memory complicates the memo's origin

The strongest challenge concerning Johnson does not seriously undermine the photograph’s date. It undermines attempts to use his later testimony to prove **where the paper came from**.

Johnson at one stage entertained the possibility that he had carried a news-wire “flash” to the airfield and handed it to Ramey. If so, the photographed sheet might have been civilian wire copy connected with the developing Roswell story rather than an internal military communication. In a later interview, however, Johnson characterised that account as an early speculation and said he had been mistaken.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/11/j-bond-johnsons-statement-on-his-visit.html?showComment=1447183915509" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on blogspot.com.</span></span></span>

This became consequential once researchers began claiming that enlarged versions contained words such as “victims”. A civilian wire-service document and an internal military message have radically different evidential implications, even if both concern Roswell. Houran and Randle therefore identified Johnson’s shifting account as a significant confound: the photograph may possess strong provenance while the provenance of the **paper within the photograph** remains unresolved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=e162b50a3885a28c826cb31c3deca69abaaba9ee&amp;repid=rep1&amp;type=pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: citeseerx.ist.psu.edu">[CiteSeerX]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">citeseerx.ist.psu.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Journal of Scientific Exploration, Vol. 16, No. 1, pp. 45–66, 2002October 19, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 19, 2025</span></span></span>

That distinction also explains why Johnson should not be treated as a single all-or-nothing witness. His later memories can be tested selectively.

The parts corroborated by external evidence — his employment by the newspaper, the assignment, his presence at Ramey’s office, the photographic equipment and the existence of contemporary photographs — carry considerably more weight than recollections about a small piece of paper reconstructed decades afterwards. Randle, despite being deeply involved in Roswell research, ultimately argued for precisely this approach: accept the portions supported by documentation and treat Johnson’s changing later narratives with caution.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/11/the-timing-of-roswell-photographs.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on blogspot.com.</span></span></span>

The *Star-Telegram*’s own recent treatment is similarly restrained about the larger claim. It identifies Johnson as the photographer and the telegram in Ramey’s hand as an object researchers continue to study, while quoting Randle acknowledging that there is no consensus on proposed readings such as “victims of the wreck”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.star-telegram.com/opinion/bud-kennedy/article289713499.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: star-telegram.com">[Fort Worth Star-Telegram]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">star-telegram.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fort Worth Star-Telegram‘Roswell Incident’ UFO crash in 1947 stirred Fort Worth &#124; Fort Worth Star-TelegramJuly 5, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 5, 2024</span></span></span>

## Why a sequence is harder to fake than one image

The significance of the six-frame assignment is cumulative. A lone photograph can raise questions about cropping, retouching, dating or misidentification. A sequence embedded in a newspaper archive provides more opportunities for cross-checking.

In Johnson’s case, those checks include the same office environment across photographs, recurring debris, identifiable officers, the photographic format he described using, archival negatives, newspaper ownership of the material and contemporary dissemination. Randle reports that the surviving Ramey [negative]({{ 'negative/' | relative_url }}) includes its uncropped frame and edge markings and that UTA, which received the negatives from the *Star-Telegram*, identified them as originals. Investigators were permitted to examine the negative on a light table and under magnification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2016/05/the-ramey-memo-negative.html?showComment=1462514194505" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: The Ramey Memo Negative</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: The Ramey Memo NegativeMay 5, 2016...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 5, 2016</span></span></span>

That does not make every surviving image equally informative. One reported “missing” Ramey view was known through its newspaper reproduction while its negative was unavailable, illustrating that the surviving photographic record is not perfectly complete.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/11/?m=0" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: November 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 2015</span></span></span> But incompleteness is different from evidence of fabrication. The surviving negatives and published material still form an interconnected historical record rather than a single unexplained artefact.

The memo photograph also contains limitations that are consistent with an ordinary press exposure rather than a document-photography exercise. The paper occupies only a small part of the 4-by-5 frame; it is angled, folded and partly affected by the limitations of the original exposure. Later high-resolution scanning can extract the information actually recorded on the film, but it cannot recreate letter detail that never resolved on the negative. A 2015 effort using the UTA negative and photographic and forensic expertise produced improved scans but, according to Randle, only marginal improvement in the decisive areas.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/09/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: September 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 2015</span></span></span>

This is why photographic authenticity and textual decipherment must remain separate. The very characteristics that make the photograph historically credible do not make its tiny text conveniently readable.

## What the sequence proves — and what it does not

For the authenticity question, Johnson’s Fort Worth sequence supports several conclusions with different levels of confidence.

**The strongest conclusion is chronological.** The memo-bearing image belongs to the July 1947 Roswell news episode. UTA holds original Johnson negatives from 8 July, the *Star-Telegram* published imagery from the assignment immediately afterwards, and surviving transmission information independently places Johnson’s photography in that contemporary news cycle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/spcouta/36080122212" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: flickr.com">[flickr.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">flickr.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Roswell UFO Incident, July 1947 &#124;...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 1947</span></span></span>

**The next conclusion is physical.** Ramey was holding an actual text-bearing sheet while being photographed with the Roswell material. Whatever that sheet ultimately proves to contain, it was not simply text digitally superimposed upon a modern reproduction. The document is an element of the original photographic scene.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/spcouta/36080122212" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: flickr.com">[Flickr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">flickr.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Roswell UFO Incident, July 1947 &#124;...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 1947</span></span></span>

**The weaker conclusion concerns provenance inside the scene.** The photographs do not establish who typed the sheet, who sent it, whether it was military or civilian, whether it was addressed to or written by Ramey, or whether Johnson handed it to him. Johnson’s changing recollections make those questions less secure, not more.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=e162b50a3885a28c826cb31c3deca69abaaba9ee&amp;repid=rep1&amp;type=pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: citeseerx.ist.psu.edu">[CiteSeerX]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">citeseerx.ist.psu.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Journal of Scientific Exploration, Vol. 16, No. 1, pp. 45–66, 2002October 19, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 19, 2025</span></span></span>

**The weakest claims are specific controversial readings.** Researchers have proposed substantially different transcriptions, and experimental work discussed by Houran and Randle found that expectations and contextual information could influence what people perceived in the degraded lettering. Their broader work therefore cautions against treating recognisable-looking fragments as equivalent to a securely recovered document.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

That hierarchy is the central value of Johnson’s photo sequence to the Ramey memo debate. It moves the argument about **whether the photographed sheet existed in 1947** onto unusually solid ground, while simultaneously showing why authenticity of the photograph cannot be converted automatically into authenticity of any proposed transcription.

The sequence is consequently stronger evidence for the memo’s **photographic provenance** than for the memo’s **meaning**. Johnson’s camera preserved a genuine, contemporary clue. It did not preserve enough detail — or enough reliable information about the sheet’s immediate origin — to make that clue self-explanatory.
