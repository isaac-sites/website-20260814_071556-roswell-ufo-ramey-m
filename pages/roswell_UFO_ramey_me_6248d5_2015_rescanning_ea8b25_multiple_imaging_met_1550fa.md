## Introduction

The 2015 archival rescanning project improved the [Ramey memo]({{ 'ramey-memo/' | relative_url }}) images without solving the central problem: the photographed writing remained too small, relative to the grain structure of the original film, for many character shapes to become uniquely identifiable. Researchers at the University of Texas at Arlington (UTA) used microscopy, digital re-photography, bracketed exposures, microfiche equipment and high-dynamic-range (HDR) processing. Those methods could expose tonal differences, suppress distracting background density and produce cleaner enlargements. They could not recreate letter-form information that the 1947 photograph had never recorded clearly enough in the first place.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journalofscientificexploration.org/index.php/jse/article/download/2635/1631" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journalofscientificexploration.org">[Journal of Scientific Exploration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journalofscientificexploration.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Journal of Scientific Exploration Anomalistics</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Journal of Scientific ExplorationAnomalisticsAugust 21, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 21, 2022</span></span></span>

## Microscopy and digital re-photography at UTA

The strongest feature of the 2015 project was that it returned to the original photographic [negative]({{ 'negative/' | relative_url }}) preserved in UTA's Fort Worth Star-Telegram collection rather than repeatedly processing an enlargement or compressed digital copy. UTA's catalogue identifies the relevant material as a black-and-white negative from 8 July 1947, and the university continues to preserve the [Ramey]({{ 'ramey/' | relative_url }}) photograph in its Special Collections.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mavmatrix.uta.edu/specialcollections_startelegram1940s/35502/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mavmatrix.uta.edu">[MavMatrix]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mavmatrix.uta.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mav Matrix General Ramey holding enlargement of telegram</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">General Ramey holding enlargement of telegramJuly 8, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

According to the published version of Simon Schollum's 2015 technical report, the team deliberately approached the negative through several recording systems. A Nikon SMZ1500 biological microscope was used to examine and digitally record the memo area. The negative was also recorded through UTA Special Collections' digital microfiche system. A Canon digital camera with macro and micro lenses provided another set of images, while Schollum additionally used a film camera with macro and micro optics and ultra-high-resolution Kodak recording film.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journalofscientificexploration.org/index.php/jse/article/download/2635/1631" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journalofscientificexploration.org">[Journal of Scientific Exploration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journalofscientificexploration.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Journal of Scientific Exploration Anomalistics</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Journal of Scientific ExplorationAnomalisticsAugust 21, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 21, 2022</span></span></span>

Using several systems was sensible because each addressed a somewhat different potential weakness. Microscopy could reveal the physical density pattern at high magnification. RAW digital photography preserved more of the camera's captured data for later processing. Different lenses and recording media provided alternative representations of the same tiny region. The microfiche system offered another independent route for examining the negative.

But these were multiple measurements of one underlying photographic record, not multiple independent photographs of the original sheet of paper. That limitation is crucial. Every method ultimately encountered the same finite pattern of developed photographic material laid down when the picture was taken in 1947. Modern equipment could sample that pattern more thoroughly; it could not go behind it and observe the original typed characters directly.

The 2015 examination actually strengthened this interpretation because it found relatively little evidence that deterioration was the dominant obstacle. The negative showed the expected minor consequences of age and handling, including dust, scratches and chemical staining, but the memo area was described as adequately exposed, in the camera's focus zone and without significant degradation sufficient to explain the reading problem. Schollum consequently identified scale as the principal difficulty.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journalofscientificexploration.org/index.php/jse/article/download/2635/1631" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journalofscientificexploration.org">[Journal of Scientific Exploration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journalofscientificexploration.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Journal of Scientific Exploration Anomalistics</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Journal of Scientific ExplorationAnomalisticsAugust 21, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 21, 2022</span></span></span>

That is an important result even though it is a negative one. If poor focus, camera shake or severe archival damage had been responsible, improved scanning might plausibly have recovered substantially clearer characters. Instead, the investigation found that the surviving negative itself was reasonably sound. The [ambiguity]({{ 'ambiguity/' | relative_url }}) therefore lay much closer to the original information limit of the photograph.

## What bracketed exposures and HDR were meant to improve

David [Rudiak]({{ 'rudiak/' | relative_url }}) and UTA staff made recordings at bracketed exposures — several captures of the same subject at different exposure settings — and retained both RAW and enhanced versions. Schollum's account says HDR techniques were then used to exploit the range of tones contained in the negative.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362854511_A_Grounded_Theory_Update_on_the_Roswell_UFO_Incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate(PDF) A Grounded Theory Update on the Roswell UFO Incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Research Gate(PDF) A Grounded Theory Update on the Roswell UFO Incident</span></span></span>

This was potentially useful because the memo was not simply a question of making everything darker or lighter. The desired signal consisted of very small density variations representing type against the photographed paper, embedded within the tonal and granular structure of the negative. HDR and image stacking allowed investigators to manipulate those relationships rather than depend upon one exposure or one global contrast setting.

The stated processing objectives were particularly revealing. The researchers tried to reduce interference from film grain where it crossed character forms, distinguish the tone of the paper from that of the writing, and separate or strengthen the tonal values attributed to the characters.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362854511_A_Grounded_Theory_Update_on_the_Roswell_UFO_Incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate(PDF) A Grounded Theory Update on the Roswell UFO Incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Research Gate(PDF) A Grounded Theory Update on the Roswell UFO Incident</span></span></span> These operations could make faint structures easier for the eye to follow.

Bracketed exposure also reduced the risk that a single digital capture would conceal potentially useful negative density. One exposure might render a dense region more effectively while another preserved distinctions elsewhere. Combining or comparing them could therefore provide a more complete digital representation of what survived on the negative.

What HDR could not do was change the spatial information originally captured. Dynamic range concerns the ability to distinguish tonal levels; resolving two neighbouring fine details is a different problem. NIST's general definition of measurement resolution makes the underlying principle clear: increasing the number of displayed digits does not itself increase an instrument's ability to distinguish features that its measurement cannot resolve.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/mpc/section4/mpc451.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: itl.nist.gov">[NIST]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">itl.nist.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">2.4.5.1. Resolution</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">2.4.5.1. Resolution...</span></span></span> In the Ramey memo case, creating a richer digital file was therefore not equivalent to obtaining a new photograph in which the letters occupied more of the original film.

This is why the sheer quantity of 2015 data can be misleading. Randle reported individual files approaching 250 MB and later said the complete collection of scans made with different techniques, lighting and filters amounted to roughly 27 GB. Yet he also acknowledged that the scans had not produced the hoped-for clarity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/10/digital-image-of-ramey-memo.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: Digital Image of the Ramey Memo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: Digital Image of the Ramey MemoOctober 29, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 29, 2015</span></span></span> A large digital dataset can represent a tiny analogue feature extremely thoroughly without that feature containing enough information for a secure transcription.

## Why cleaner character shapes remained ambiguous

Schollum's measurements put the problem into unusually concrete terms. In the digital representation discussed in his report, the complete 4×5-inch negative measured about 3,663 by 2,743 pixels, while the message itself occupied only about 148.5 pixels in width. He argued that readability therefore depended heavily on the size of the font relative to the size and distribution of the film's silver-halide structure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362854511_A_Grounded_Theory_Update_on_the_Roswell_UFO_Incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate(PDF) A Grounded Theory Update on the Roswell UFO Incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Research Gate(PDF) A Grounded Theory Update on the Roswell UFO Incident</span></span></span>

[Photographic grain]({{ 'grain-ceiling/' | relative_url }}) is not merely dirt laid over an otherwise perfect image. Traditional film records an image through photosensitive silver-halide material, whose distribution produces the granular structure visible under sufficient enlargement. Modern explanations of analogue film likewise note that grain becomes increasingly conspicuous when photographic material is enlarged and that grain characteristics are connected to the resolving properties of film.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.adobe.com/uk/creativecloud/video/discover/film-grain.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: adobe.com">[Adobe]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">adobe.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Film grain effect &#124; What is film grain &#124; Adobe</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Film grain effect &#124; What is film grain &#124; Adobe...</span></span></span>

That makes the memo fundamentally different from a normally sized but low-contrast document. With a faint modern scan of a page, increasing contrast may reveal complete strokes that were present but difficult to see. With the Ramey photograph, portions of a tiny typed character can coincide with the scale at which the film itself records density variations. A dark patch might represent part of a letter stroke, grain associated with that stroke, neighbouring strokes blended together, or some combination of those effects.

Processing can make such a patch more conspicuous without determining which interpretation is correct. Sharpening may produce a stronger apparent edge. Contrast separation may make one possible stroke stand out. Grain suppression may produce a smoother-looking character. None of those transformations proves that the resulting shape corresponds uniquely to an original letter.

The 2015 work therefore encountered an information problem rather than merely an enhancement problem. Once two plausible characters generate sufficiently similar surviving density patterns, different processing choices can favour different visual readings. A cleaner image can consequently look more legible while remaining evidentially ambiguous.

## More images did not mean independent confirmation

The use of microscopy, conventional re-photography, microfiche recording, bracketed exposures and HDR might initially sound like several independent tests pointing towards a final answer. In practice, their independence was limited because all were derived from the same negative.

If a particular stroke were clearly reproduced by several methods, that would provide useful confidence that the feature belonged to the negative rather than being an artefact introduced by one scanner or processing operation. But it would not necessarily establish what letter produced that stroke. Repeatedly observing an ambiguous source feature confirms the feature; it does not automatically resolve its linguistic identity.

This distinction helps explain why opening the scans to additional readers did not quickly settle matters. In October 2015, Randle wrote that the analysis had produced no universal conclusions and that copies had been supplied to other researchers, including sceptical participants, in the hope that somebody might find a successful method. The following month he publicly described the scans as better than their predecessors but still short of the necessary clarity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/10/digital-image-of-ramey-memo.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: Digital Image of the Ramey Memo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: Digital Image of the Ramey MemoOctober 29, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 29, 2015</span></span></span>

There was already experimental reason to be cautious about treating apparent words as self-validating. A 2002 study by James Houran and Randle had given participants high-resolution Ramey memo material under different contextual suggestions. Its purpose was to test whether expectations could influence attempts to decipher an intrinsically ambiguous stimulus. The researchers found no dependable complete reading emerging from the material, reinforcing the concern that recognisable-looking fragments could be shaped by interpretation as well as image information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

The implication for the [2015 scans]({{ '2015-scans/' | relative_url }}) is not that every proposed letter was imaginary. Some strokes and partial character forms are genuinely visible. The problem is deciding where observation ends and reconstruction begins. When the surviving visual evidence permits several plausible completions, recognising a word is no longer equivalent to measuring one.

## The failed breakthrough clarified the real limit

The most useful result of the 2015 programme was therefore diagnostic. It tested several straightforward explanations for the memo's persistent illegibility and substantially narrowed the problem.

The negative was not found to be hopelessly deteriorated. The investigators did not rely on one low-quality scan. They used microscopy and multiple recording systems. They varied exposure. They retained RAW material. They exploited HDR and image stacks to separate tonal information and reduce distracting grain. The resulting scans were regarded by participants as improvements. Yet the character sequences still did not converge on an agreed transcription.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journalofscientificexploration.org/index.php/jse/article/download/2635/1631" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journalofscientificexploration.org">[Journal of Scientific Exploration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journalofscientificexploration.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Journal of Scientific Exploration Anomalistics</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Journal of Scientific ExplorationAnomalisticsAugust 21, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 21, 2022</span></span></span>

That outcome points to a harder ceiling: the memo occupied too little of the original photograph for every character to have been recorded with the distinctive detail needed to discriminate reliably between competing letters. The technology used in 2015 became increasingly good at representing the surviving negative, but representation and recovery are not the same thing.

UTA's later treatment of the problem reflects that unresolved status. In 2017, the university described the words in Ramey's hand as obscured and publicised a $10,000 reward being offered for a definitive decipherment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.uta.edu/utamagazine/spring-2017/stories/collected.php" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uta.edu">[UTA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uta.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Ramey Memo &#124; UTA Magazine</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Ramey Memo &#124; UTA Magazine...</span></span></span> The institutional description is significant because it came after the 2015 rescanning campaign rather than before it.

The lesson of the failed breakthrough is consequently more precise than saying that the photograph was simply “too blurry”. The original negative contains real tonal structure, and modern imaging can expose that structure with impressive fidelity. But where a typed character was projected onto only a minute area of photographic emulsion, its distinctive strokes could become inseparable from the analogue recording structure itself. Microscopy makes those structures larger. HDR makes their tonal differences easier to inspect. Bracketed exposures make the digital capture more complete. None guarantees that an ambiguous pattern will collapse into one uniquely supported letter.

That is why multiple imaging methods could all work as intended and still fail to settle the Ramey memo. They improved access to the surviving information; they did not demonstrate that enough information had survived to support a definitive reading.
