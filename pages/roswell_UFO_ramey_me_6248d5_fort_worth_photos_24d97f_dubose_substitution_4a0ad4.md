## Introduction

Thomas J. DuBose is often cited as one of the strongest witnesses for the claim that the debris photographed in Brigadier General Roger [Ramey]({{ 'ramey/' | relative_url }})’s Fort Worth office was a deliberate substitute for whatever had arrived from Roswell. The attraction is understandable: DuBose was not a distant observer but Ramey’s chief of staff, and in a signed 1991 affidavit he said that the material photographed in the office was a weather balloon and that the weather-balloon explanation was a “cover [story]({{ 'story/' | relative_url }})” intended to divert the press.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2007/05/dubose-affidavit.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: The Du Bose Affidavit</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: The DuBose AffidavitMay 12, 2007...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 12, 2007</span></span></span>

There is, however, a major complication. In another 1991 interview, DuBose was asked explicitly whether the Roswell debris had been switched for a weather balloon. He emphatically said it had **not** been switched and identified the photographed material as what Major [Jesse Marcel]({{ 'marcel/' | relative_url }}) had brought from Roswell.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/rw/d/muj1991shandera.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOS at close sight: Roswell 1947, investigations in the 1990&#x27;s, Jaime H. That contradiction is central. DuBose provides unusually direct evidence that some form of cover story existed at Fort Worth, but his surviving statements do not straightforwardly establish the particular [substitution]({{ 'substitution/' | relative_url }}) scenario often attributed to him.

## DuBose was inside Ramey’s headquarters

In July 1947, DuBose was a colonel serving as chief of staff to Major General Roger Ramey, commander of the [Eighth Air Force]({{ 'command-control/' | relative_url }}) at Fort Worth Army Air Field. He later retired as a brigadier general. His position matters because the Fort Worth episode occurred inside the command in which he worked, rather than being something he learnt about second-hand years later. Biographical material records his West Point and senior Air Force background, while the surviving photographs place him with Ramey and the debris itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/rw/w/thomasdubose.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOS at close sight: Roswell 1947, the witnesses, Thomas J. DuBose therefore occupied a particularly important evidential position. The question was not simply what he thought the debris resembled. He could potentially speak to **how the material reached Fort Worth, what orders were received, what happened to it and what the public was subsequently told**.

His 1991 affidavit gave precisely such an account. DuBose said Major General Clements McMullen, then deputy commander of Strategic Air Command, telephoned him after the Roswell story became public. According to DuBose, he then directed Roswell commander Colonel William Blanchard to send the recovered material to Fort Worth in a sealed container.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2007/05/dubose-affidavit.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: The Du Bose Affidavit</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: The DuBose AffidavitMay 12, 2007...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 12, 2007</span></span></span>

DuBose further claimed that after the Roswell aircraft arrived, Fort Worth base commander Colonel Al Clark was instructed to take possession of material and transport it towards Washington, with McMullen intending it eventually to reach General Benjamin Chidlaw at Air Materiel Command at [Wright Field]({{ 'wright-field/' | relative_url }}). DuBose characterised the operation as conducted under very tight secrecy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2007/05/dubose-affidavit.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: The Du Bose Affidavit</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: The DuBose AffidavitMay 12, 2007...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 12, 2007</span></span></span>

These claims were made roughly 44 years after the event, so they cannot simply be treated as a contemporaneous record. Nevertheless, they explain why DuBose became so important to the substitution argument: his later testimony appeared to put a senior headquarters officer directly inside both the debris-handling chain and the management of the press story.

## What his affidavit actually says

The crucial passage in DuBose’s affidavit is narrower than it is sometimes presented. He stated:

> “The material shown in the photographs taken in Maj. Gen. Ramey’s office was a weather balloon.”

He immediately added that the weather-balloon explanation was a cover story designed to divert press attention.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2007/05/dubose-affidavit.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: The Du Bose Affidavit</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: The DuBose AffidavitMay 12, 2007...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 12, 2007</span></span></span>

Read in isolation, those two statements appear to support a substitution interpretation. If material arrived from Roswell under secrecy, while the object photographed for reporters was merely a weather balloon deployed as a cover story, an obvious inference is that two different sets of material existed.

That inference has consequently become attached to DuBose in later Roswell literature: genuine wreckage arrives, it is moved onwards under tight security, and ordinary balloon debris is displayed for journalists in Ramey’s office. Under this interpretation, the mundane appearance of the Fort Worth photographs does not disprove an unusual Roswell recovery because the photographs show the prop used to end press interest, not the recovered material itself. DuBose’s affidavit is frequently reproduced precisely because its wording appears compatible with that scenario.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://majesticdocuments.com/investigations/witnesses/first-hand-witnesses/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: majesticdocuments.com">[Majestic Documents]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">majesticdocuments.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Majestic Documents First Hand Witnesses &#124; Majestic Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Majestic Documents First Hand Witnesses &#124; Majestic Documents</span></span></span>

But there is an important distinction between **saying the balloon explanation was a cover story** and **saying the photographed debris had been substituted**. DuBose’s affidavit never actually uses the language of a switch. Nor does it describe someone removing one pile of wreckage and replacing it with another before reporters entered the office. That additional step is an interpretation of his account rather than something expressly stated in the affidavit.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.astronomyufo.com/UFO/FtWorth.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: astronomyufo.com">[Astronomy UFO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">astronomyufo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Astronomy UFOA deflating experience</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Astronomy UFOA deflating experience</span></span></span>

That distinction becomes decisive when DuBose’s other testimony is considered.

## Asked directly about a switch, DuBose denied it

In interviews published by UFO researcher Jaime Shandera in the January 1991 *MUFON UFO Journal*, DuBose was confronted with essentially the exact question at issue. Shandera referred to researchers Kevin Randle and Donald Schmitt claiming that the material in Ramey’s office had been switched and replaced by a weather balloon. DuBose rejected the proposition emphatically: “That material was never switched!”<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/rw/d/muj1991shandera.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOS at close sight: Roswell 1947, investigations in the 1990&#x27;s, Jaime H. Shandera then removed any possible [ambiguity]({{ 'ambiguity/' | relative_url }}) by asking whether DuBose meant that the material in Ramey’s office was the actual debris brought from Roswell. DuBose answered that this was “absolutely right”. When asked whether Ramey or somebody else might have arranged a switch without his knowledge, DuBose again insisted that he had been present, had responsibility for the material and would have known.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/rw/d/muj1991shandera.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOS at close sight: Roswell 1947, investigations in the 1990&#x27;s, Jaime H. This is the most important qualification to the claim that “DuBose confirmed the switch”. He did not merely fail to mention a substitution on one occasion; when researchers put that specific hypothesis to him, he explicitly denied it.

Shandera also showed DuBose photographs of the Fort Worth debris. According to the published account, DuBose identified what he saw as the material Marcel had brought into Fort Worth from Roswell.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.astronomyufo.com/UFO/FtWorth.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: astronomyufo.com">[Astronomy UFO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">astronomyufo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Astronomy UFOA deflating experience</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Astronomy UFOA deflating experience</span></span></span>

Both propositions cannot comfortably support the conventional “real debris removed, balloon substituted” reconstruction at the same time.

## What “cover story” could mean


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

The ambiguity turns largely on what DuBose meant when he called the balloon explanation a cover story. That phrase sounds decisive but does not identify what was being concealed.

</div>

One interpretation is the classic substitution scenario: genuinely extraordinary material came from Roswell, was secretly sent onwards, and a balloon was placed on Ramey’s floor for the press. DuBose’s affidavit, particularly its sequence of secret shipment followed by the reference to photographed balloon material, can readily be read that way.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2007/05/dubose-affidavit.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: The Du Bose Affidavit</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: The DuBose AffidavitMay 12, 2007...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 12, 2007</span></span></span>

The difficulty is that DuBose’s direct denial of a switch works against precisely this reading. If the photographed debris really was what arrived from Roswell, then “cover story” must have referred to something other than swapping physical evidence.

A second possibility is that the material was balloon-related but its **purpose or origin** was being concealed. This became particularly relevant after the US Air Force’s 1990s Roswell investigation concluded that the recovered material most probably came from Project Mogul, a classified effort involving balloon trains and acoustic equipment intended to help detect Soviet nuclear tests. The Air Force’s 1995 report argued that the components themselves included ordinary balloons and radar targets even though their employment formed part of a sensitive programme.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/GOVPUB-D301-PURL-LPS71655" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govinfo.gov">[GovInfo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govinfo.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GOVPUB D301 PURL LPS71655</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Roswell Report: Fact Versus Fiction in the New Mexico Desert - GOVPUB-D301-PURL-LPS71655 &#124; Content Details &#124; GovInfoJanuary 1...</span></span></span>

Under that interpretation, a statement that “weather balloon” was a cover story does not necessarily imply that the debris on the floor was fake. The cover could instead consist of giving an innocuous explanation for material associated with a classified programme. The Air Force report is an official retrospective explanation rather than independent proof of what DuBose personally meant in 1991, but it demonstrates why the words “cover story” do not logically require an extraterrestrial object or a physical substitution.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/GOVPUB-D301-PURL-LPS71655" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: govinfo.gov">[GovInfo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">govinfo.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GOVPUB D301 PURL LPS71655</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Roswell Report: Fact Versus Fiction in the New Mexico Desert - GOVPUB-D301-PURL-LPS71655 &#124; Content Details &#124; GovInfoJanuary 1...</span></span></span>

There is also a third possibility: DuBose’s memories had become internally inconsistent during the four decades between Roswell and his interviews. That possibility cannot be dismissed merely because he was a senior officer. His testimony is valuable because of his position, but rank does not eliminate the normal limitations of long-delayed recollection.

## The photographs make the contradiction consequential

The DuBose dispute matters particularly because the Fort Worth photographs are physical historical records. They unquestionably show Ramey, DuBose, Marcel and weather officer Irving Newton with material that resembles balloon and radar-reflector wreckage. The 1995 Air Force investigation included technical material on the ML-307 radar reflector and Newton’s later statement identifying the wreckage as balloon and radar-target equipment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Roswell_report_fact_versus_fiction_i.html?id=qrsIzxnsAjgC" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: books.google.com">[Google Books]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">books.google.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Books The Roswell report: fact versus fiction in the New Mexico desert</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Books The Roswell report: fact versus fiction in the New Mexico desert</span></span></span>

For a substitution theory, therefore, DuBose could have supplied a powerful answer to an otherwise awkward photograph: **yes, the pictures show balloon wreckage because balloon wreckage was deliberately substituted**.

But that is stronger than his evidence permits. His affidavit supports the proposition that the balloon explanation was deliberately used to divert journalists, yet his Shandera interview directly rejects the physical substitution needed to separate the photographed debris from the Roswell material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2007/05/dubose-affidavit.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: The Du Bose Affidavit</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: The DuBose AffidavitMay 12, 2007...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 12, 2007</span></span></span>

This distinction also prevents two different questions from being collapsed into one:

**Was the public explanation misleading?** DuBose later said yes.

**Did officers replace the Roswell wreckage with unrelated balloon material before the photographs were taken?** DuBose also went on record saying no.

Those are not equivalent claims.

## DuBose strengthens a cover-story case more than a substitution case

The safest conclusion is therefore narrower than either side of the Roswell debate sometimes allows. DuBose is significant evidence that a senior officer inside Ramey’s headquarters later remembered the Fort Worth press explanation as intentionally deceptive. His affidavit is unusually explicit on that point, and his position makes the recollection difficult simply to ignore.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/rw/w/thomasdubose.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOS at close sight: Roswell 1947, the witnesses, Thomas J. Dubose...</span></span></span>

What his testimony does **not** securely establish is that the debris visible in the famous Fort Worth photographs was brought in separately and substituted for the material Marcel transported from Roswell. The most directly relevant DuBose interview says exactly the opposite.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/rw/d/muj1991shandera.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOS at close sight: Roswell 1947, investigations in the 1990&#x27;s, Jaime H. Shandera DuBose interview, 1991January 1, 1991...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 1991</span></span></span>

Nor does DuBose’s affidavit identify the concealed object as extraterrestrial. Its wording concerns an “object” recovered outside Roswell, secret handling of material, a weather balloon in the photographs and a cover story. Later descriptions sometimes convert those statements into a much more specific narrative than the affidavit itself contains.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/rw/w/thomasdubose.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOS at close sight: Roswell 1947, the witnesses, Thomas J. Dubose...</span></span></span>

That leaves DuBose as a genuinely difficult witness rather than a clean victory for either interpretation. For critics of the original 1947 weather-balloon announcement, his “cover story” statement is important testimony from inside Ramey’s command. For proponents of the specific Fort Worth substitution theory, however, his explicit insistence that the debris was **never switched** is a substantial evidential problem.

Within the larger dispute over the Fort Worth photographs, the distinction is crucial. DuBose gives reason to question whether the public explanation told the whole story. He does not, on the surviving record, provide reliable confirmation that the photographed debris itself was a substitute.
