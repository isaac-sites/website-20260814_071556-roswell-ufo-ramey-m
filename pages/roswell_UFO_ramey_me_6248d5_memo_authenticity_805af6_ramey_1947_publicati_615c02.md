## Introduction

Yes. The strongest evidence that the [Ramey]({{ 'ramey/' | relative_url }}) photograph itself existed in 1947 is not a later UFO book, enlargement or digital scan: it is the contemporary newspaper record. A photograph of Brigadier General Roger M. Ramey from the Fort Worth sequence appeared in the *Fort Worth Star-Telegram* on 9 July 1947, immediately after the Army publicly reclassified the Roswell debris as balloon-related material. The surviving newspaper reproduction is attributed to reporter-photographer J. [Bond Johnson]({{ 'johnson/' | relative_url }}), while the University of Texas at Arlington (UTA) preserves negatives from the same photographic assignment in the newspaper's archive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AGeneral_Ramey_with_Roswell_Memo.png" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[wikimedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:General Ramey with Roswell Memo.png4 Oct 2024 — &quot;One Disk Tale Exploded by Army as Others Fly.&quot; Fort Worth Star-Tel...</span></span></span>

That combination sharply [limits]({{ 'limits/' | relative_url }}) one particular [authenticity]({{ 'authenticity/' | relative_url }}) claim. Whatever the sheet in Ramey's hand actually says, the underlying photographic scene cannot reasonably be dismissed as an image manufactured decades after Roswell became famous. Contemporary publication places the photograph in the public record in July 1947. It does **not**, however, authenticate modern attempts to decipher the photographed text, nor does it establish who composed the document or what its complete message was.

## The July 1947 newspaper appearance

The key contemporary item is the *Fort Worth Star-Telegram*'s coverage of 9 July 1947. A surviving reproduction of the Ramey photograph identifies its source as J. Bond Johnson's front-page article, “One Disk Tale Exploded by Army as Others Fly”, in the newspaper's evening edition. The reproduction is therefore not merely labelled retrospectively as a “1947 Roswell photograph”; it is tied to a named newspaper, edition, article, photographer and publication date.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AGeneral_Ramey_with_Roswell_Memo.png" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:General Ramey with Roswell Memo.png4 Oct 2024 — &quot;One Disk Tale Exploded by Army as Others Fly.&quot; Fort Worth Star-Tel...</span></span></span>

This occurred within roughly a day of the events depicted. Johnson had been sent to Fort Worth Army Air Field on 8 July, where he photographed Ramey and other officers with the material brought from Roswell. The *Star-Telegram*'s own retrospective account says Johnson was 21, had a Speed Graphic camera available when the assignment arose, and made six frames of Ramey and other officers with the debris. UTA's archival catalogue independently dates the relevant photographic material to 8 July 1947 and identifies it as part of the Fort Worth *Star-Telegram* Collection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.star-telegram.com/opinion/bud-kennedy/article289713499.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: star-telegram.com">[star-telegram.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">star-telegram.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fort Worth Star-Telegram‘Roswell Incident’ UFO crash in 1947 stirred Fort Worth &#124; Fort Worth Star-TelegramJuly 5, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 5, 2024</span></span></span>

The publication chronology matters because the Roswell story was still breaking. The Army's Roswell announcement of a recovered “flying disc” had produced nationwide attention on 8 July; the Fort Worth explanation that followed identified the material as balloon and [radar-target debris]({{ 'radar-target/' | relative_url }}). The Ramey photographs belong directly to that contemporary press cycle rather than to the much later revival of Roswell as a UFO controversy. The US Air Force's historical review likewise treats Ramey's Fort Worth press presentation as part of the events of 1947, irrespective of the Air Force's separate conclusions about what the recovered material represented.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Portals/16/documents/AFD-101201-038.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dafhistory.af.mil">[DAF History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dafhistory.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">AFD 101201 038</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DAF HistoryThe Roswell Report... 1947 TO THE PRESENT. There is also evidence that the photograph travelled beyond the *Star-Telegram*'s own production chain. A 2002 paper in the *Journal of Scientific Exploration* reported that a copy obtained from the Bettmann Photo Archives carried information crediting Johnson and dating a soundphoto-wire transmission to the night of 8 July 1947. Researcher Kevin Randle separately described encountering Bettmann copies bearing an International News Photos soundphoto credit to Johnson. The precise transmission time has been disputed in specialist accounts, so it should not be used to claim minute-by-minute certainty. The important point is narrower: an independently circulating press-photo copy was associated with Johnson and the July 1947 news cycle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=e162b50a3885a28c826cb31c3deca69abaaba9ee&amp;repid=rep1&amp;type=pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: citeseerx.ist.psu.edu">[CiteSeerX]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">citeseerx.ist.psu.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Journal of Scientific Exploration, Vol. 16, No. 1, pp. 45–66, 2002October 19, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 19, 2025</span></span></span>

## What contemporary publication proves

For photographic provenance, newspaper publication provides something fundamentally different from a witness remembering the event decades later. A recollection can change; a physical newspaper printed immediately after an event fixes the existence of reproduced visual information at or before its publication date.

In this case, the 9 July appearance establishes a useful terminus: the photographic image had necessarily been created by then. A hypothetical claim that somebody fabricated the entire Ramey photograph during the later Roswell controversy would have to explain how substantially the same scene was already reproduced in a newspaper in July 1947. That makes a decades-later fabrication of the **underlying photograph** incompatible with the surviving publication record.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AGeneral_Ramey_with_Roswell_Memo.png" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:General Ramey with Roswell Memo.png4 Oct 2024 — &quot;One Disk Tale Exploded by Army as Others Fly.&quot; Fort Worth Star-Tel...</span></span></span>

The newspaper evidence becomes stronger when considered alongside the archive. UTA says that it holds photographs and negatives from the incident as part of the Fort Worth *Star-Telegram* Collection. Its catalogue dates the Ramey material to 8 July 1947, identifies the original format as black-and-white negatives and describes an enlargement of the telegram being held by Ramey. Another UTA description identifies the source as collection AR406-6 07/08/1947 #2026 and states that Johnson's original negatives from 8 July are preserved by Special Collections.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mavmatrix.uta.edu/specialcollections_startelegram1940s/35502/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mavmatrix.uta.edu">[uta.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mavmatrix.uta.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mav Matrix General Ramey holding enlargement of telegram</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mav Matrix General Ramey holding enlargement of telegram</span></span></span>

Those are complementary forms of provenance rather than redundant ones. The archive connects today's scans and enlargements to physical photographic holdings inherited from a newspaper collection; contemporary publication demonstrates that a Ramey image was already being used journalistically when the Roswell story happened. The combination is much stronger evidence for the age and historical existence of the photographic sequence than a modern digital file's metadata would ever be.

It is also significant that the scene is not known solely through one mysterious crop showing a piece of paper. The *Star-Telegram* archive contains photographs of Ramey, Colonel Thomas DuBose and Major Jesse Marcel from the Fort Worth assignment. UTA's catalogue, for example, separately records Ramey and DuBose examining the material and Marcel holding debris, all dated 8 July. The newspaper's modern account describes Johnson taking six frames. This wider sequence makes the memo-bearing view part of an ordinary press-photography assignment rather than an isolated artefact with no surrounding photographic context.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.star-telegram.com/opinion/bud-kennedy/article289713499.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: star-telegram.com">[star-telegram.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">star-telegram.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fort Worth Star-Telegram‘Roswell Incident’ UFO crash in 1947 stirred Fort Worth &#124; Fort Worth Star-TelegramJuly 5, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 5, 2024</span></span></span>

The most defensible conclusion is consequently quite specific: **the photographic episode in which Ramey was pictured with the Roswell material and a text-bearing sheet is genuinely rooted in the July 1947 press record**. One does not have to accept any particular interpretation of Roswell to reach that conclusion.

## What publication still cannot prove about the memo

Contemporary publication authenticates the existence and approximate date of a photograph much more readily than it authenticates every proposition later derived from that photograph. This distinction is particularly important with the [Ramey memo]({{ 'ramey-memo/' | relative_url }}) because the controversial evidence is not simply the picture of Ramey; it is the tiny, obliquely photographed writing on the folded sheet in his hand.

UTA itself uses appropriately cautious language. Its description says that the memo has been closely scrutinised but “has yet to be definitively deciphered”. The institution's archive therefore supports the photograph's provenance without endorsing one of the competing transcriptions of its text.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/spcouta/36080122212" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: flickr.com">[Flickr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">flickr.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Roswell UFO Incident, July 1947 &#124;...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 1947</span></span></span>

The technical literature gives good reason for maintaining that separation. The *Journal of Scientific Exploration* paper on attempts to read the Ramey memo noted that researchers had produced interpretations containing potentially dramatic terms, but also highlighted major gaps and ambiguities in the proposed readings. The fact that the source photograph is historically genuine does not recover image detail that the negative failed to capture clearly enough in the first place. Enhancement can make existing patterns easier to inspect; it cannot reliably reconstruct characters whose visual information is insufficient or ambiguous.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=e162b50a3885a28c826cb31c3deca69abaaba9ee&amp;repid=rep1&amp;type=pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: citeseerx.ist.psu.edu">[CiteSeerX]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">citeseerx.ist.psu.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Journal of Scientific Exploration, Vol. 16, No. 1, pp. 45–66, 2002October 19, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 19, 2025</span></span></span>

Contemporary newspaper publication therefore cannot, by itself, establish:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* that a proposed word such as “victims” has been read correctly;
* that the paper concerned recovered occupants or extraordinary wreckage;
* that it was an official telegram rather than another type of message or document;
* who wrote, dictated, transmitted or received it;
* whether every visible line concerned Roswell;
* or which of the competing modern transcriptions, if any, most closely reproduces its wording.

</div>

That evidential boundary resolves an important confusion in discussions of Ramey-memo authenticity. “The photograph is authentic” and “this transcription of the memo is authentic” are two separate claims requiring different evidence. The first has unusually strong historical support because of the 1947 newspaper and archival record. The second remains an image-analysis problem.

## Why the 1947 publication matters

The newspaper appearance is most useful as a control on the range of plausible explanations. It removes a broad class of late-fabrication theories without deciding the larger Roswell dispute. There is no need to rely on Johnson's later memories of precisely what happened in Ramey's office to establish that the photographic assignment occurred: the contemporary newspaper product and surviving *Star-Telegram* negatives provide evidence independent of those recollections. This is especially valuable because later accounts of details surrounding Johnson's assignment have themselves generated disagreements among Roswell researchers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2009/10/ufos-ramey-memo-j-bond-johnson-and-me.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on blogspot.com.</span></span></span>

The evidential chain can therefore be stated conservatively. Johnson photographed Ramey during the Fort Worth Roswell episode on 8 July 1947; the *Star-Telegram* published a Ramey photograph in its coverage on 9 July; photographic material from the assignment survives in the newspaper's collection at UTA; and the photographed sheet is visible in that historically anchored sequence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AGeneral_Ramey_with_Roswell_Memo.png" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[wikimedia.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:General Ramey with Roswell Memo.png4 Oct 2024 — &quot;One Disk Tale Exploded by Army as Others Fly.&quot; Fort Worth Star-Tel...</span></span></span>

For the Ramey memo controversy, that is an important but deliberately limited finding. The question **“Did the Ramey photo exist in 1947?”** has exceptionally strong documentary support: contemporary publication says yes. The much harder question — **“What exactly does the paper in Ramey's hand say?”** — is not answered by the newspaper's date stamp. Historical provenance secures the photograph as a genuine 1947 object of study; it does not turn uncertain pixels into certain words.
