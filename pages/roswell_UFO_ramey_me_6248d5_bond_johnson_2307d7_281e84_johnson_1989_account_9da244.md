## Introduction

J. [Bond Johnson]({{ 'johnson/' | relative_url }})’s March 1989 recollection is important because it captures his Roswell story before some of its more dramatic features appeared. More than four decades after he photographed Brigadier General Roger [Ramey]({{ 'ramey/' | relative_url }}) and the debris at Fort Worth Army Air Field, Johnson remembered a fairly ordinary newspaper assignment: his editor saw a wire alert, discovered Johnson had his camera, and sent him to photograph whatever had arrived from Roswell. In Ramey’s office, Johnson said, he was told that the supposed flying disc was actually a weather balloon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/11/j-bond-johnsons-statement-on-his-visit.html?showComment=1447183915509" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on blogspot.com.</span></span></span>



<figure class="article-media-figure article-media-figure-illustration"><img src="{{ "/assets/images/roswell_UFO_ramey_me_6248d5_bond_johnson_2307d7_281e84_johnson_1989_account_9da244-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="1989 Account illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/roswell_UFO_ramey_me_6248d5_bond_johnson_2307d7_281e84_johnson_1989_account_9da244-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/roswell_UFO_ramey_me_6248d5_bond_johnson_2307d7_281e84_johnson_1989_account_9da244-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" width="1920" height="1080" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low"><figcaption class="article-media-caption"><span class="article-media-caption-label">Explanatory illustration 1</span></figcaption></figure>

That version is not perfect. Johnson’s memory of how much film he carried is difficult to reconcile with the surviving photographic record, and uncertainty remains over precisely who briefed him. But its central sequence fits surprisingly well with evidence from July 1947: the Roswell [story]({{ 'story/' | relative_url }}) was already moving rapidly over the wires; Ramey publicly identified the debris as a balloon; a weather officer confirmed that identification; and Johnson photographed the scene for the *Fort Worth Star-Telegram*.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/11/j-bond-johnsons-statement-on-his-visit.html?showComment=1447183915509" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on blogspot.com.</span></span></span>

For evaluating Johnson as a witness — and especially for assessing claims about the paper in Ramey’s hand — that distinction matters. His earliest recorded account is strongest where contemporary records independently support it and weakest where later recollections added details that the 1947 record does not require.

## How Johnson said the assignment began

Johnson told researcher Kevin Randle on 24 March 1989 that he had returned to the *Fort Worth Star-Telegram* newsroom late on Tuesday, 8 July 1947. He described himself as a reporter and backup photographer who kept a recently acquired 4-by-5-inch Speed Graphic camera in his car because his work included night and police assignments. His city editor asked whether he had the camera and then sent him to Ramey’s office to obtain a photograph.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/11/j-bond-johnsons-statement-on-his-visit.html?showComment=1447183915509" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on blogspot.com.</span></span></span>

The key point is how Johnson remembered the newspaper learning about the event. He did not describe a personal invitation from Ramey or a specially arranged military press conference. Instead, he recalled an alert arriving over a news wire — he thought it was Associated Press, although he allowed that it might have been another service — reporting that material connected with the Roswell story was being flown to Fort Worth. His editor reacted to that breaking news and dispatched him.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/11/j-bond-johnsons-statement-on-his-visit.html?showComment=1447183915509" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on blogspot.com.</span></span></span>

That is consistent with the speed at which the Roswell story was circulating on 8 July. Contemporary reporting shows that the Army announcement from Roswell had become a major wire-service story before Fort Worth supplied the explanation. An updated Associated Press report subsequently announced that the celebrated Roswell object had been identified at Fort Worth as a weather balloon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/rw/p/rdr9jul1947.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOS at close sight: The Roswell 1947 incident in the Press, Roswell Daily Record, July 9, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 9, 1947</span></span></span>

A modern *Star-Telegram* retrospective gives essentially the same newsroom framework. It identifies city editor Cullum Greene as the person who sent the 21-year-old Johnson to the airfield after news arrived about the Roswell material. Johnson was available and had the Speed Graphic camera needed to cover the story.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.star-telegram.com/opinion/bud-kennedy/article289713499.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: star-telegram.com">[Fort Worth Star-Telegram]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">star-telegram.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fort Worth Star-Telegram‘Roswell Incident’ UFO crash in 1947 stirred Fort Worth &#124; Fort Worth Star-TelegramJuly 5, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 5, 2024</span></span></span>

The agreement is significant because it makes the mundane explanation for Johnson’s presence stronger than later scenarios in which the military actively summoned him. The evidence does not require Ramey to have invited the newspaper. The Roswell announcement itself supplied ample reason for a Fort Worth editor to send a reporter to the headquarters of the general handling the story.

## Where the 1989 account matches the 1947 record

Once Johnson reached the base, his 1989 narrative continued in a form broadly compatible with the contemporary reporting. He remembered entering Ramey’s large office and photographing the general with debris spread across the floor. His description was distinctly unromantic: he called the material smelly rubbish and remembered the debris as incongruous in Ramey’s comparatively plush office.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/11/j-bond-johnsons-statement-on-his-visit.html?showComment=1447183915509" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on blogspot.com.</span></span></span>

The surviving photograph independently establishes the essential scene. Ramey was photographed with the debris and the document that later became known as the [Ramey memo]({{ 'ramey-memo/' | relative_url }}). The image is attributed to Johnson and the *Fort Worth Star-Telegram*, while the newspaper’s surviving photographic collection is held by the University of Texas at Arlington.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AGeneral_Ramey_with_Roswell_Memo.png" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Commons File:General Ramey with Roswell Memo.png</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:General Ramey with Roswell Memo.png - Wikimedia CommonsJuly 9, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 9, 1947</span></span></span>

More important is what Johnson said he was told. In the 1989 interview, he recalled that while he was there he was briefed that the object was not the flying disc originally reported but a crashed weather balloon. He later allowed that the briefing might have come from a public information officer rather than Ramey personally.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/11/j-bond-johnsons-statement-on-his-visit.html?showComment=1447183915509" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on blogspot.com.</span></span></span>

That uncertainty about the speaker matters less than the content, because the content matches the public record from 1947. The contemporary story attributed to Johnson concluded that Ramey identified the material as a weather balloon and that a weather officer confirmed the identification. Contemporary Associated Press coverage likewise reported that Fort Worth weather officer Irving Newton had identified the material as a weather balloon and Rawin radar target.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/11/j-bond-johnsons-statement-on-his-visit.html?showComment=1447183915509" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on blogspot.com.</span></span></span>

The National Archives' account of the surviving 1947 reporting similarly notes that Ramey initially described the debris as flimsy, foil-like material and that the story quickly shifted to the weather-balloon identification. Whatever judgement one makes about whether that identification was correct — the later Air Force investigation argued that the Roswell debris was associated with the classified Project Mogul balloon programme — there is little documentary support for portraying the Fort Worth scene as one in which no explanation had yet been offered.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://text-message.blogs.archives.gov/2017/07/05/see-something-say-something-ufo-reporting-requirements-office-of-military-government-for-bavaria-germany-may-1948/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: text-message.blogs.archives.gov">[The Text Message]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">text-message.blogs.archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Text Message“See Something, Say Something”: UFO Reporting Requirements, Office of Military Government for Bavaria, Germany May 1948 –...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: may 1948</span></span></span>

That is the central point of agreement between Johnson’s 1989 memory and the contemporary record. His early recollection did not depict Ramey as mystified. It depicted Johnson arriving while the original flying-disc story was being replaced by the balloon explanation.

## The photograph exposes a weakness in Johnson’s memory

Not everything in the 1989 interview fits comfortably with the surviving evidence. Johnson recalled taking only one film holder containing two sheets of 4-by-5 film. Yet substantially more than two photographs from the Fort Worth session survive or are documented, and the *Star-Telegram* now describes Johnson as having shot six frames of Ramey and other officers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/11/j-bond-johnsons-statement-on-his-visit.html?showComment=1447183915509" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on blogspot.com.</span></span></span>

This discrepancy is useful precisely because it is mundane. It demonstrates the normal limitations of a witness trying to reconstruct a fast-moving assignment 42 years later. Johnson could remember the type of camera, his editor’s instruction, the debris in Ramey’s office and the newspaper’s handling of the photographs while being wrong about something as concrete as the quantity of film he carried.

Randle also recorded that Johnson later disputed taking the photographs of Major [Jesse Marcel]({{ 'marcel/' | relative_url }}), although the photographic sequence and Johnson’s earlier discussion of the images raised problems for that denial. Randle’s subsequent review of the surviving Ramey photographs concluded that four photographs of Ramey were identifiable, while the broader *Star-Telegram* account places six Johnson frames in the Fort Worth sequence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/11/?m=0" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: November 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 2015</span></span></span>

The sensible historical lesson is not that every Johnson memory must therefore be discarded. It is that his testimony should be weighted according to corroboration. His presence, camera work and basic assignment are independently secure. A precise decades-old recollection about how many sheets of film were in his camera bag is much less secure.

## The 1947 story is an unusually important benchmark

There is another reason to treat the contemporary newspaper account as the baseline. During Randle’s early contact with Johnson, Johnson identified the 9 July *Star-Telegram* story as his own work. Randle later reported that Johnson subsequently denied having written it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/11/j-bond-johnsons-statement-on-his-visit.html?showComment=1447183915509" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on blogspot.com.</span></span></span>

Authorship questions do not make every sentence of a newspaper report infallible. Newspaper stories can combine a reporter’s observations with wire copy, editorial rewriting and information from other reporters. But the article was produced within hours of the event, whereas Johnson’s interview came 42 years later. It therefore provides a contemporaneous check against later memory even if the precise division of authorship cannot be reconstructed.

That check becomes particularly valuable on the question of what Ramey supposedly thought. The 1947 report says that after examining the material Ramey regarded it as a weather balloon and that the weather officer verified that judgement. The broader Associated Press account told essentially the same story: Newton examined the wreckage at Fort Worth and identified a balloon and radar target.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/11/j-bond-johnsons-statement-on-his-visit.html?showComment=1447183915509" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on blogspot.com.</span></span></span>

Johnson’s 1989 testimony fits this chronology rather well because he explicitly remembered being briefed on the balloon explanation before leaving the office. A later recollection in which Ramey appeared baffled would therefore have to overcome not merely an impersonal newspaper clipping but Johnson’s own earlier recorded memory.

## Which later details departed from the earlier version

By the later 1990s, Johnson had become much more closely involved with researchers interested in the Roswell photographs and the Ramey memo. A 1997 *Coast to Coast AM* programme involving Johnson and Jaime Shandera still retained the basic newsroom origin of the assignment: the city editor saw a wire report about the Roswell material going to Ramey and sent Johnson because he had a camera.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fight.fudgie.org/search/show/ab/episode/19970702_Wed" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fight.fudgie.org">[Fight]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fight.fudgie.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Fight Art Bell</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Art Bell - Coast to Coast AM with Art Bell - Jamie Shandera - Roswell Crash - TranscriptJuly 2, 1997...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 2, 1997</span></span></span>

Other elements, however, became more dramatic. The programme presented Ramey as baffled by the debris rather than as a general participating in an already developing balloon identification. That is difficult to square with Johnson’s 1989 statement that he was briefed at the scene that the supposed disc was a weather balloon, and with the contemporary reporting attributing that identification to Ramey and Newton.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/11/j-bond-johnsons-statement-on-his-visit.html?showComment=1447183915509" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on blogspot.com.</span></span></span>

The provenance of the document in Ramey’s hand became still more unstable. At one stage Johnson speculated that he himself might have brought the paper into the office — specifically, a copy of the news-wire flash his editor had given him — and handed it to Ramey. In a later interview he explicitly withdrew that idea, calling his earlier speculation erroneous.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/11/j-bond-johnsons-statement-on-his-visit.html?showComment=1447183915509" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on blogspot.com.</span></span></span>

That reversal has direct consequences for Ramey-memo arguments. If Johnson had supplied the sheet, it could simply have been a civilian wire-service document and its presence in Ramey’s hand would reveal little about internal military communications. If he did not supply it, it could have originated within Ramey’s headquarters — but the photograph alone cannot establish whether it was a military message, draft press statement, routine communication or something else. As Randle himself has acknowledged when discussing these alternatives, the document’s provenance cannot be settled merely by assuming one of Johnson’s changing recollections is correct.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/10/ramey-memo-and-victims-of-wreck.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: Ramey Memo and Victims of the Wreck</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: Ramey Memo and Victims of the WreckOctober 27, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 27, 2015</span></span></span>

The important distinction is between observation and retrospective inference. Johnson unquestionably photographed Ramey holding a document. His later attempts to remember or infer where that document came from are a different category of evidence.

## What the 1989 account is actually good for

The 1989 interview remains valuable, but mostly for the parts that can be checked against independent evidence. It strengthens a relatively straightforward reconstruction of Johnson’s role: breaking news reached the *Star-Telegram*; his editor sent him because he had a camera; Johnson went to Fort Worth Army Air Field; he photographed Ramey and the debris; and by the time of his visit he understood that the Army was identifying the supposed flying disc as a weather balloon. Contemporary reporting and the surviving photographs support that framework.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/11/j-bond-johnsons-statement-on-his-visit.html?showComment=1447183915509" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[blogspot.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on blogspot.com.</span></span></span>

It is less reliable as a source for small logistical details. His recollection of carrying only two sheets of film sits awkwardly beside the larger surviving photographic sequence. Later uncertainty over which photographs he took reinforces the point that even an eyewitness can misremember the mechanics of an assignment decades afterwards.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/11/?m=0" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: November 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 2015</span></span></span>

And it is weaker still when used to establish facts that became important only after researchers began scrutinising the photographs — particularly the origin of the paper in Ramey’s hand. Johnson’s acknowledgement that his claim about supplying the AP flash was speculation is especially important. It removes what might otherwise appear to be direct eyewitness evidence for the memo’s provenance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/11/j-bond-johnsons-statement-on-his-visit.html?showComment=1447183915509" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on blogspot.com.</span></span></span>

For the Ramey memo debate, therefore, Johnson’s 1989 account is most useful as a boundary on later storytelling. It shows what he remembered relatively early in the modern Roswell investigation: an editor-driven newspaper assignment, debris displayed in Ramey’s office and a balloon explanation already being communicated. Where later accounts became more elaborate — Ramey appearing baffled, Johnson’s role in the photographic sequence changing, or the document acquiring a more definite provenance — those additions deserve less weight unless they can be independently corroborated.

The surviving 1947 record does not answer every Roswell question, and it certainly does not make the blurred text of the Ramey memo readable. What it does provide is a fixed reference point. When a participant’s memories change over time, the photograph, the contemporary newspaper coverage and the earliest recorded recollection should carry more weight than details that entered the story decades later.
