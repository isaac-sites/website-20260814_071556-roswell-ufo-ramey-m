## Introduction

David Rudiak’s [confidence]({{ 'confidence/' | relative_url }}) coding matters because it exposes a distinction that can disappear when his [Ramey memo]({{ 'ramey-memo/' | relative_url }}) reconstruction is quoted as ordinary prose. In published comparison tables, his transcription mixed upper- and lower-case letters: capitals represented characters treated as comparatively secure, while lower-case letters represented less certain readings or best guesses. Thus a string such as “VictIMs of tHE weECK” was not originally a claim that every letter in “VICTIMS OF THE WRECK” was equally visible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>



<figure class="article-media-figure article-media-figure-illustration"><img src="{{ "/assets/images/roswell_UFO_ramey_me_6248d5_rudiak_reconstructio_1fcf0f_rudiak_confidence_co_12cb8a-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Confidence Coding illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/roswell_UFO_ramey_me_6248d5_rudiak_reconstructio_1fcf0f_rudiak_confidence_co_12cb8a-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/roswell_UFO_ramey_me_6248d5_rudiak_reconstructio_1fcf0f_rudiak_confidence_co_12cb8a-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" width="1920" height="1080" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low"><figcaption class="article-media-caption"><span class="article-media-caption-label">Explanatory illustration 1</span></figcaption></figure>

That distinction goes to the heart of [Rudiak]({{ 'rudiak/' | relative_url }})’s evidentiary claim. His reconstruction attempts to move from fragmentary visual information to complete words and then to coherent sentences. Confidence coding lets readers see where that movement occurs. It is therefore simultaneously a strength of his presentation — because uncertainty is disclosed — and a reason for caution: a plausible completed sentence can look much more secure than the individual characters from which it was assembled.

## What the mixed capitals were meant to show

The mixed typography was not supposed to reproduce the appearance of the original document. The photographed message itself appears to have been typed in capitals, consistent with the technology under discussion. Rather, mixed case functioned as an editorial confidence notation. The 2002 study by James Houran and Kevin Randle explains the convention explicitly: capital letters or all-capital words marked readings an interpreter believed accurate, while lower-case letters indicated a best guess. Asterisks and dashes were also used in related [transcriptions]({{ 'transcriptions/' | relative_url }}) to distinguish undeciphered characters from areas where little more than a smudge could be discerned.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

The convention originated in the wider collaborative effort to decipher the memo rather than being unique to Rudiak. Neil Morris had already used capitals for characters he considered secure, lower case for tentative identifications, an asterisk for an undeciphered letter and a dash for a largely unreadable position. Rudiak subsequently used the same basic distinction between stronger and weaker readings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

That makes the typography unusually informative. Consider the early Rudiak transcription preserved in the literature:

> “VictIMs of tHE weECK you fOrWArdEd TO…”

The capitals visually advertise an uneven evidence base. “TO”, for example, is presented much more confidently than several letters in the surrounding words. The same transcription gives “EaM At FORT WORTH, TEX.” on the next line, with “FORT WORTH, TEX.” markedly more secure than the incomplete word preceding it. On another line, Rudiak rendered “THE ‘DISC’ they will ship”, again distinguishing the proposed strong reading from the weaker continuation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

The notation therefore prevents one important misunderstanding. Rudiak was not saying that the photograph supplies a uniformly crisp sentence which merely needs to be enlarged. His own transcription records substantial differences in confidence from character to character.

## From visible letters to completed words

The harder question is what happens after those confidence levels have been assigned. Rudiak’s method did not stop at identifying isolated letter shapes. According to Houran and Randle’s account of discussions with him, he proposed determining likely character positions from the spacing of clearer text, establishing word lengths, and requiring candidate sentences to satisfy English grammar, syntax and meaning. For poorly formed words, he could select likely letters and search for words fitting those positions and lengths.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

Rudiak publicly described the logic through a crossword analogy. A few letters, the apparent word length and surrounding language narrow the possibilities; contextual information can then eliminate candidates that make little sense. In discussing “VICTIMS”, for example, he argued that alternative dictionary matches fitting proposed letter positions produced words that were implausible in the sentence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufologie.patrickgross.org/press/santafenewmexican22nov2002.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOS at close sight: the newspapers, David Rudiak&#x27;s work on Ramey memo, 2002November 23, 2002...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 23, 2002</span></span></span>

That is a legitimate form of hypothesis generation, but it means there are two different kinds of information in the resulting reconstruction. One comes directly from proposed visual features in the photograph. The other comes from constraints imposed by spelling, grammar, word length and presumed context. The mixed-capital transcription is valuable precisely because it provides at least a rough indication of where those categories cease to be equivalent.

This becomes especially important when an uncertain letter is pivotal. Critics of the “victims” reading have proposed alternatives including “remains” and, later, “viewing”. John Kirby’s independent attempt produced a substantially different second line — one referring to “remains of the material” — even though he agreed with the much less dramatic “At Fort Worth, Tex.” on the following line.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

In other words, agreement is not distributed evenly across the memo. A reader can accept that some characters or phrases are recoverable without accepting every completed word in Rudiak’s reconstruction.

## Why complete sentences raise the evidential stakes

Confidence coding becomes most important when Rudiak’s reconstruction is converted from mixed-case working text into polished quotations. Compare the evidentiary impression created by “VictIMs of tHE weECK” with the clean phrase “THE VICTIMS OF THE WRECK”. The latter looks like a direct transcription. The former tells the reader that some of the characters carrying the meaning were treated as less secure.

The same issue becomes larger when multiple reconstructed words are linked into a narrative. Rudiak’s later reading develops passages about a ranch, victims of a wreck, material forwarded to Fort Worth, a “disc”, aircraft movements and weather balloons. Yet the earlier confidence-coded version contains [uncertain letters]({{ 'uncertain-letters/' | relative_url }}), alternative readings in brackets and large unresolved gaps between comparatively secure fragments. Kevin Randle’s 2022 review reproduces that earlier version and contrasts it with the substantially different readings proposed by other investigators.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362854511_A_Grounded_Theory_Update_on_the_Roswell_UFO_Incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate(PDF) A Grounded Theory Update on the Roswell UFO Incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Research Gate(PDF) A Grounded Theory Update on the Roswell UFO Incident</span></span></span>

This does not mean that a word assembled partly from uncertain characters is necessarily wrong. Context really can recover degraded text: anyone reading a damaged inscription or an incomplete crossword uses constraints supplied by neighbouring letters and language. The problem is evidential independence. If the reconstructed sentence is then used to prove the very historical context that helped select its uncertain letters, the reasoning risks becoming circular.

Houran and Randle identified this problem explicitly. They regarded searching for candidate words as reasonable, but warned that working backwards from an assumed context could produce words that were not actually present. They also reported that prior exposure to UFO and Roswell information was associated with the number of words participants thought they could decipher. Their proposed remedy was independent, blind examination rather than allowing interpreters to begin with a Roswell narrative.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

Their experiment makes the confidence issue more concrete. Participants given different contextual information produced some context-specific readings, whereas a small group of comparatively stable words appeared across conditions. A later review of the experiment identifies “Fort Worth TEX”, “[Story]({{ 'story/' | relative_url }})” and “Balloons” among the recurring readings, while participants primed with an atomic-testing story reported terms such as “Atomic” and “Laboratory”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journalofscientificexploration.org/index.php/jse/article/view/2713/1663" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journalofscientificexploration.org">[Journal of Scientific Exploration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journalofscientificexploration.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Journal of Scientific ExplorationJournal of Scientific ExplorationFebruary 18, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 18, 2026</span></span></span>

The important point is not that this experiment automatically disproves Rudiak’s transcription. It is that the experiment demonstrates why uncertain characters should not silently inherit the apparent certainty of the sentence into which they have been fitted.

## The confidence gap is largest where the claim is strongest

Rudiak’s reconstruction is evidentially interesting precisely because some comparatively readable material appears compatible with the known circumstances of July 1947. “AT FORT WORTH” receives unusually broad agreement in the comparison assembled by Houran and Randle. “WEATHER BALLOONS” also appears as an area of agreement among several interpreters.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

Those phrases, however, do not by themselves establish the extraordinary interpretation. Fort Worth is where Ramey was photographed, and references to balloons are entirely compatible with the publicly reported Roswell story. The reconstruction becomes far more consequential when the memo is read as referring to “victims” and a “disc”, and still more consequential when tentative fragments are connected into a coherent description of a recovery and subsequent public-relations response.

That creates a useful way of reading Rudiak’s work: distinguish **character confidence**, **word confidence** and **narrative confidence**. The first asks whether a particular mark really resembles a particular letter. The second asks whether enough letters and spacing justify a proposed word. The third asks whether the resulting words genuinely support the larger reconstructed account. Success at one level does not automatically establish the next.

The published comparison table illustrates the problem particularly well. Several investigators could agree on “AT FORT WORTH” while disagreeing sharply elsewhere. On the critical second line, Kirby supplied “REMAINS OF THE MATERIAL” where other investigators supplied “VICTIMS OF THE WRECK”. On line four, proposed readings diverged even more substantially despite some interpreters identifying “DISC” or “DISK”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

This variation is more informative than simply asking whether somebody can see a particular word. A robust decipherment should ideally survive changes of interpreter, [enhancement]({{ 'enhancement/' | relative_url }}) procedure and prior expectations. Houran and Randle accordingly recommended standardised enhancement and at least three independent, blind specialist laboratories, with agreement between them used to estimate reliability.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

## Which parts were really secure?

The fairest assessment is therefore neither that Rudiak simply invented a continuous message nor that his completed reconstruction can be treated as a conventional transcription. His confidence coding shows that he recognised different degrees of uncertainty and made some of that uncertainty visible to readers. That is an important methodological virtue.

But the same notation limits what can safely be claimed from the reconstruction. A lower-case letter designated as a “best guess” remains a hypothesis even when grammar makes the completed word attractive. Several such hypotheses can reinforce one another until a smooth sentence emerges, without every constituent character becoming independently better resolved.

That is why reproducing Rudiak’s mixed-case versions alongside any normalised transcription is important. Readers should be able to distinguish something like “VictIMs of tHE weECK” from an unqualified presentation of “VICTIMS OF THE WRECK”. The first preserves information about the reconstruction process; the second suppresses it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362854511_A_Grounded_Theory_Update_on_the_Roswell_UFO_Incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate(PDF) A Grounded Theory Update on the Roswell UFO Incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Research Gate(PDF) A Grounded Theory Update on the Roswell UFO Incident</span></span></span>

The continuing lack of consensus reinforces that caution. Randle’s later review concludes that investigators still disagree over what the message says and how its discrepancies should be resolved. Even sophisticated examination has produced substantially different readings of precisely the passages carrying the greatest Roswell significance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362854511_A_Grounded_Theory_Update_on_the_Roswell_UFO_Incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate(PDF) A Grounded Theory Update on the Roswell UFO Incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Research Gate(PDF) A Grounded Theory Update on the Roswell UFO Incident</span></span></span>

Rudiak’s confidence coding therefore matters for more than typography. It is effectively an uncertainty map of his reconstruction. It shows where the photograph was thought to supply relatively strong character evidence and where linguistic or historical inference had to do more of the work. For judging the Ramey memo, preserving that map is essential: the evidentiary question is not merely whether Rudiak could construct a coherent Roswell-related message, but how much of that message can be recovered independently from the photograph before the reconstruction itself supplies the missing context.
