## Introduction

The disputed line in the [Ramey memo]({{ 'ramey-memo/' | relative_url }}) cannot presently be read with enough [confidence]({{ 'confidence/' | relative_url }}) to establish that it says “victims of the wreck”. Several Roswell investigators have reconstructed something close to that phrase, but another examiner read the same region as referring to “remains of the material”, while still others questioned whether the first disputed word begins with a V at all. The difference is crucial: “victims” suggests casualties, whereas “remains” can simply mean recovered debris.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://noufors.com/Documents/Books%2C%20Manuals%20and%20Published%20Papers/Specialty%20UFO%20Publications/Journal%20of%20Scientific%20Exploration/jse_16_1_houran.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: noufors.com">[Noufors]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">noufors.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Noufors&#x27;&#x27;A Message in a Bottle:&quot; Confounds in Deciphering the Ramey Memo from the Roswell UFO CaseMay 23, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 23, 2026</span></span></span>

This is therefore more than a disagreement over one fuzzy letter. The competing readings demonstrate the central difficulty of the [Ramey]({{ 'ramey/' | relative_url }}) memo: investigators can agree on comparatively recognisable fragments elsewhere yet construct sharply different sentences from its most consequential line. Experimental work has also found that expectations can influence what readers extract from the degraded photograph. Later high-resolution work improved the image without producing a definitive transcription.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=e162b50a3885a28c826cb31c3deca69abaaba9ee&amp;repid=rep1&amp;type=pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: citeseerx.ist.psu.edu">[CiteSeerX]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">citeseerx.ist.psu.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Journal of Scientific Exploration, Vol. 16, No. 1, pp. 45–66, 2002October 19, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 19, 2025</span></span></span>

## How investigators reconstructed the disputed line

The Ramey memo survives not as an original document available for ordinary reading but as tiny text visible on a sheet held by Brigadier General Roger Ramey in a 1947 photograph. Researchers therefore work from photographic reproductions and scans, enlarging and processing an image in which the characters occupy very little usable information. Even recent researchers pursuing improved scans describe angle, [film grain]({{ 'film-grain/' | relative_url }}), damage and the limited original resolution as fundamental constraints.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ramey-memo.com/press-conference/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ramey-memo.com">[Ramey Memo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ramey-memo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ramey Memo THE PRESS CONFERENCE –</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ramey MemoTHE PRESS CONFERENCE – JULY 8, 1947 – UnlockingTheMystery...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

That distinction matters because digital enhancement is not equivalent to recovering information that the photograph never recorded clearly. Contrast adjustments, filtering and enlargement can make existing patterns easier to inspect, but they cannot guarantee that a fuzzy cluster corresponds uniquely to one letter. This is exactly where the rival [transcriptions]({{ 'transcriptions/' | relative_url }}) developed.

Neil Morris approached the memo line by line and explicitly distinguished between characters he regarded as relatively certain, tentative guesses and unreadable marks. His second-line reconstruction included “VICTIMS OF THE WReck”. Don Burleson, who said he had spent about a year analysing the document with image-enhancement software, independently produced a substantially different overall message but likewise interpreted the key portion as “THE VICTIMS OF THE WREKCD”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

David Rudiak developed perhaps the best-known pro-“victims” reconstruction. In versions published around 2000, his second line included the sequence “THE VICTIMS OF THE WRECK YOU FORWARDED”. The agreement on those particular words between Morris, Burleson and Rudiak is one reason the phrase became prominent in Roswell literature. Yet their surrounding text does not exhibit comparable agreement. Side-by-side transcriptions show different words, gaps and sentence structures before and after the supposed phrase.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://rense.com/general5/ramey.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: rense.com">[rense.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">rense.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">New Roswell Evidence Deciphered In Gen. Ramey&#x27;s Memo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">October 31, 2000...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 31, 2000</span></span></span>

That is an important qualification. Three investigators recognising “victims” sounds stronger when the phrase is considered in isolation than when their complete reconstructions are compared. Their disagreement elsewhere shows that the underlying image permits substantial interpretative latitude.

## Victims versus remains, word by word

The strongest challenge to “victims” is not simply that the memo is blurry. It is that another investigator produced a coherent alternative from the same part of the image.

John Kirby, described in the Houran-Randle study as a computer-industry researcher who examined the message with relevant equipment, reported being unable to see much confidently. He did accept “At Fort Worth, Tex.” on the following line, but reconstructed the critical second line as “are the remains of the material you commanded we fly”. Houran and Randle explicitly noted that substituting “remains” for “victims” changes the nature of the message.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

The two readings point in very different directions:

* **“Victims”** makes the grammatical object people or other casualties. Combined with “wreck”, it naturally invites the inference that beings were injured or killed.
* **“Remains”** can refer to objects. In the Roswell setting, “remains of the material” would naturally describe recovered wreckage or debris without implying bodies.
* **The surrounding words matter.** “Victims of the wreck” sounds compelling only if several neighbouring uncertain shapes are also correctly reconstructed as “of the wreck”. Kirby instead grouped those shapes into a different sentence.
* **The first letter is itself disputed.** Russ Estes, who examined a large print with optical and video equipment, thought the initial character looked more like a P than a V. He also questioned shapes elsewhere in the proposed word, including the final character.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362854511_A_Grounded_Theory_Update_on_the_Roswell_UFO_Incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate(PDF) A Grounded Theory Update on the Roswell UFO Incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Research Gate(PDF) A Grounded Theory Update on the Roswell UFO Incident</span></span></span>

The debate has generated still more possibilities. Kevin Randle later discussed correspondence suggesting that the disputed word might be something other than “victims”, including a proposed “viewing”. Rudiak's counterargument was partly structural: he maintained that “victims” fitted the apparent number and positions of characters and that relatively few plausible V-words suited the surrounding context.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2016/04/viewing-vs-victims-of-wreck.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: Viewing vs Victims of the Wreck</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: Viewing vs Victims of the WreckApril 30, 2016...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 30, 2016</span></span></span>

That argument illustrates both the strength and weakness of contextual reconstruction. Word length, syntax and expected vocabulary are legitimate clues when reading damaged text. But once context is used to select among indistinct letter shapes, the result is no longer purely a visual identification. A candidate phrase can begin helping the reader decide what the ambiguous marks ought to be.

## Why different readers can see convincing words

Houran and Randle tested this problem experimentally in their 2002 study. They divided 176 participants into three groups. One group was told that the photographed document concerned Roswell and UFOs, another that it concerned secret atomic-bomb testing, and a third received no suggested context. Participants were then asked to decipher the degraded document.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://noufors.com/Documents/Books%2C%20Manuals%20and%20Published%20Papers/Specialty%20UFO%20Publications/Journal%20of%20Scientific%20Exploration/jse_16_1_houran.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: noufors.com">[Noufors]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">noufors.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Noufors&#x27;&#x27;A Message in a Bottle:&quot; Confounds in Deciphering the Ramey Memo from the Roswell UFO CaseMay 23, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 23, 2026</span></span></span>

The resulting transcriptions showed an [expectation]({{ 'expectation/' | relative_url }}) effect. Participants frequently reported words consistent with the scenario they had been given. The UFO-conditioned group, for example, produced terms including “UFO” and “crash”; the atomic-test condition generated words such as “atomic” and “laboratory”. Yet some fragments appeared across conditions, particularly “Fort Worth TX”, suggesting that certain areas of the photograph may genuinely contain more recoverable information than others.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=e162b50a3885a28c826cb31c3deca69abaaba9ee&amp;repid=rep1&amp;type=pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: citeseerx.ist.psu.edu">[CiteSeerX]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">citeseerx.ist.psu.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Journal of Scientific Exploration, Vol. 16, No. 1, pp. 45–66, 2002October 19, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 19, 2025</span></span></span>

That result is particularly relevant to the “victims” dispute. It does not prove that the word is absent. Instead, it demonstrates why repeated recognition by informed Roswell investigators cannot by itself establish that the word is present. Investigators who already know that they are examining a document connected with the Roswell controversy possess far more contextual information than the experiment's participants. Once “victims of the wreck” becomes a known candidate transcription, completely independent reading becomes especially difficult.

The study therefore creates an important distinction between **agreement on a proposed reading** and **independent recovery of a word from the image**. If several readers inspect an ambiguous pattern after learning that others see V-I-C-T-I-M-S, their subsequent agreement has less evidential force than several genuinely blind transcriptions arriving at the same sequence.

## Better scans have not settled the wording

One might expect improved imaging to decide between “victims” and “remains”. So far, it has not.

Randle's 2022 review describes attempts by specialists using several image-processing techniques. One examination applied Fourier-transform methods intended to improve the signal-to-noise relationship but found that photographic noise occurred at frequencies comparable with the text itself. Another, by a City University of Hong Kong computer-engineering specialist, applied techniques including homomorphic filtering, wavelet algorithms and deblurring but obtained no clearly interpretable text.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362854511_A_Grounded_Theory_Update_on_the_Roswell_UFO_Incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate(PDF) A Grounded Theory Update on the Roswell UFO Incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Research Gate(PDF) A Grounded Theory Update on the Roswell UFO Incident</span></span></span>

A later effort went back to the photographic negative rather than merely reprocessing familiar reproductions. In 2020, Gene Cooper of GIGAmarco made a series of high-resolution scans using specialised equipment. The intention was to characterise and reduce interference introduced by grain, scratches, dust and decades of handling while extracting as much information as possible from the negative.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362854511_A_Grounded_Theory_Update_on_the_Roswell_UFO_Incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate(PDF) A Grounded Theory Update on the Roswell UFO Incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Research Gate(PDF) A Grounded Theory Update on the Roswell UFO Incident</span></span></span>

The resulting image was somewhat clearer, but not enough to resolve the disputed wording. The published assessment concluded that no further increase in visual readability could be obtained through that particular process and that further progress would probably require algorithms capable of distinguishing type characters from the background. Randle consequently judged that the memo still could not be read with reasonable certainty.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journalofscientificexploration.org/index.php/jse/article/view/2635" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journalofscientificexploration.org">[Journal of Scientific Exploration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journalofscientificexploration.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on journalofscientificexploration.org.</span></span></span>

This result is significant because it reverses a common assumption about the dispute. The problem is not simply that nobody has yet applied sufficiently sophisticated enhancement. Substantial enhancement efforts have been made. The limiting factor appears to be how little character information survives in the source photograph.

## What the disagreement means for Roswell claims


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

If “victims of the wreck” could be established independently and reproducibly, it would be an unusually important piece of Roswell evidence. A contemporary document referring to “victims” associated with a “wreck” would demand an explanation, although even that wording alone would not establish that the victims were extraterrestrial. This is why proponents such as Rudiak have treated the reading as central to the memo's importance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.listennotes.com/podcasts/a-different/a-different-perspective-with-kKUxI1TAWtN/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: listennotes.com">[Listen Notes]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">listennotes.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Listen Notes A Different Perspective with Kevin Randle Interviews</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Listen Notes A Different Perspective with Kevin Randle Interviews</span></span></span>

</div>

But the evidential status of the line is weaker than that formulation suggests. The surviving image supports several incompatible reconstructions; investigators disagree about the supposedly decisive letters; controlled testing demonstrates that contextual suggestion can shape readings; and improved scans have still not produced a reproducible transcription. Randle's 2022 reassessment — despite remaining open to an extraordinary interpretation of Roswell — explicitly concludes that attempts to decipher the memo have not produced definitive results.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journalofscientificexploration.org/index.php/jse/article/view/2635" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journalofscientificexploration.org">[Journal of Scientific Exploration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journalofscientificexploration.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on journalofscientificexploration.org.</span></span></span>

The most useful way to describe the line, therefore, is not “the memo says victims of the wreck”, nor the equally categorical “the memo says remains”. Both exceed what the photograph presently establishes. **“Victims of the wreck” and “remains of the material” are competing reconstructions of degraded text.** The former has been independently favoured by several Roswell investigators and carries dramatic implications; the latter demonstrates that a materially different, non-casualty reading can be constructed from the same image.

That unresolved contrast is the real evidential lesson of the line. The Ramey memo may contain information about what happened at Roswell, and some portions appear more consistently recognisable than others. But at precisely the point where the transcription would become most consequential — the difference between recovered material and recovered victims — the photographic evidence does not currently provide enough information to choose confidently between the competing readings.
