## Introduction

The [Ramey memo]({{ 'ramey-memo/' | relative_url }}) is most useful for understanding how the Army Air Forces managed a sudden and confusing public information crisis in July 1947. The photograph of Brigadier General Roger M. [Ramey]({{ 'ramey/' | relative_url }}) holding the document at Fort Worth Army Air Field does not provide a readable record of every private decision made during the Roswell response, but it does place written communication at the centre of the military’s effort to explain a rapidly developing news story.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AGen_Ramey_Roswell_memo_1947.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">File:Gen Ramey Roswell memo 1947.jpg</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Gen Ramey Roswell memo 1947.jpg - Wikimedia CommonsJuly 8, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: [July 8](&amp;#123;&amp;#123; &#x27;july-8/&#x27; &#124; relative_url &amp;#125;&amp;#125;), 1947</span></span></span>



<figure class="article-media-figure article-media-figure-illustration"><img src="{{ "/assets/images/roswell_UFO_ramey_me_6248d5_what_memo_proves_277897_military_communicati_1aa0ba-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Military Communication illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/roswell_UFO_ramey_me_6248d5_what_memo_proves_277897_military_communicati_1aa0ba-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/roswell_UFO_ramey_me_6248d5_what_memo_proves_277897_military_communicati_1aa0ba-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" width="1920" height="1080" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low"><figcaption class="article-media-caption"><span class="article-media-caption-label">Explanatory illustration 1</span></figcaption></figure>

Within hours of the Roswell Army Air Field announcement that a “flying disc” had been recovered, senior officers moved from an initial statement to a different public explanation involving a weather balloon. The Ramey memo photograph captures the stage where officials were coordinating that transition: handling debris, briefing reporters, and presenting an official interpretation of events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ramey-memo.com/press-conference/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ramey-memo.com">[Ramey Memo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ramey-memo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ramey Memo THE PRESS CONFERENCE –</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ramey MemoTHE PRESS CONFERENCE – JULY 8, 1947 – UnlockingTheMystery...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span> The document therefore matters less as a supposed hidden message and more as evidence of the communication machinery operating during one of the most famous military public relations moments of the twentieth century.

## Inside the Army's Roswell Response Process

### Officials managing a sudden news event

The Roswell response unfolded under unusual pressure. The original statement from Roswell Army Air Field was distributed beyond the local area through news channels, creating immediate national attention. The Army Air Forces then had to manage questions from journalists while senior commanders reviewed the material that had arrived from New Mexico.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ramey-memo.com/press-conference/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ramey-memo.com">[Ramey Memo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ramey-memo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ramey Memo THE PRESS CONFERENCE –</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ramey MemoTHE PRESS CONFERENCE – JULY 8, 1947 – UnlockingTheMystery...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

The Ramey photograph shows this process at the level of senior command. General Ramey, commander of the [Eighth Air Force]({{ 'command-control/' | relative_url }}), was photographed with debris identified by the military as coming from a weather balloon-related device. Colonel Thomas DuBose and Major [Jesse Marcel]({{ 'marcel/' | relative_url }}) were also associated with the briefing process, showing that communication was not handled by a single individual but by a chain of officers involved in intelligence, command and public affairs roles.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ramey-memo.com/press-conference/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ramey-memo.com">[Ramey Memo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ramey-memo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ramey Memo THE PRESS CONFERENCE –</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ramey MemoTHE PRESS CONFERENCE – JULY 8, 1947 – UnlockingTheMystery...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

This sequence reveals a familiar military communication pattern during sensitive incidents:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--decision" markdown="1">

* **Initial field reporting:** Local personnel reported what they believed they had recovered.
* **Command review:** Higher-ranking officers assessed the information and controlled the next stage of communication.
* **Public explanation:** Officials presented an interpretation designed to answer immediate media questions.

</div>

The Ramey memo belongs to the second and third stages. It is evidence of an organised response process, even though the exact wording of the paper remains uncertain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AGen_Ramey_Roswell_memo_1947.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">File:Gen Ramey Roswell memo 1947.jpg</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Gen Ramey Roswell memo 1947.jpg - Wikimedia CommonsJuly 8, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: [July 8](&amp;#123;&amp;#123; &#x27;july-8/&#x27; &#124; relative_url &amp;#125;&amp;#125;), 1947</span></span></span>

The significance is that the memo photograph shows the military responding as an institution. The image is not simply a record of an object being displayed; it documents a moment when officers were preparing and delivering an explanation during a rapidly changing public situation.

## What the memo reveals about internal communication

The strongest evidence from the Ramey memo is not the disputed text itself but the circumstances surrounding it. The paper was photographed in Ramey’s hand during the Fort Worth press event, establishing that written communication was part of the briefing environment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AGen_Ramey_Roswell_memo_1947.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">File:Gen Ramey Roswell memo 1947.jpg</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Gen Ramey Roswell memo 1947.jpg - Wikimedia CommonsJuly 8, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: [July 8](&amp;#123;&amp;#123; &#x27;july-8/&#x27; &#124; relative_url &amp;#125;&amp;#125;), 1947</span></span></span>

Researchers have attempted to enhance the photograph and reconstruct the document’s wording, with some arguing that certain phrases can be identified. However, the image quality, angle and limited resolution prevent a universally accepted reading. The debate has continued because different analysts have interpreted the same blurred marks in different ways.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Close-up-of-the-memo-section-of-the-photograph-of-Brigadier-General-Roger-M-Ramey_fig2_228706129" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

This limitation is important when examining military communication. A document can prove that information was being passed between officials without necessarily revealing the full content of that communication. The Ramey memo therefore provides a window into the existence and timing of official coordination, but it cannot independently reconstruct every instruction, conversation or decision behind the response.

The communication clues that can be established are broader:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Written briefing material existed:** Ramey was photographed holding a document during the response.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AGen_Ramey_Roswell_memo_1947.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">File:Gen Ramey Roswell memo 1947.jpg</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Gen Ramey Roswell memo 1947.jpg - Wikimedia CommonsJuly 8, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: [July 8](&amp;#123;&amp;#123; &#x27;july-8/&#x27; &#124; relative_url &amp;#125;&amp;#125;), 1947</span></span></span>
* **Multiple levels of command were involved:** The movement of information from Roswell to Fort Worth indicates coordination between field personnel and senior officers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://muller.lbl.gov/teaching/physics10/Roswell/USMogulReport.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muller.lbl.gov">[muller.lbl.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muller.lbl.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Mogul</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Mogul</span></span></span>
* **The military prioritised a public explanation:** The debris display and press engagement show an organised attempt to shape public understanding of the event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ramey-memo.com/press-conference/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ramey-memo.com">[Ramey Memo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ramey-memo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ramey Memo THE PRESS CONFERENCE –</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ramey MemoTHE PRESS CONFERENCE – JULY 8, 1947 – UnlockingTheMystery...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

These points make the memo valuable even without a confirmed transcription. It shows communication in action rather than providing a complete record of what officials privately believed.

## Why the memo cannot fully reconstruct private decisions

The main challenge in using the Ramey memo as historical evidence is separating what the photograph proves from what researchers infer. The photograph securely connects Ramey, a document and the Roswell response, but the document’s unreadable sections limit how far conclusions can go.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3AGen_Ramey_Roswell_memo_1947.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">File:Gen Ramey Roswell memo 1947.jpg</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Gen Ramey Roswell memo 1947.jpg - Wikimedia CommonsJuly 8, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: [July 8](&amp;#123;&amp;#123; &#x27;july-8/&#x27; &#124; relative_url &amp;#125;&amp;#125;), 1947</span></span></span>

Some interpretations of the memo have argued that hidden phrases could contradict the military’s public explanation. Those claims depend on disputed readings of unclear text rather than a clearly preserved document. Academic discussion of the memo has highlighted the problem of interpreting ambiguous visual information, where expectations about the case can influence how unclear marks are understood.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/figure/Close-up-of-the-memo-section-of-the-photograph-of-Brigadier-General-Roger-M-Ramey_fig2_228706129" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

The broader documentary record also complicates attempts to use the memo as a complete explanation of the Roswell response. Later Air Force investigations concluded that the recovered material was consistent with a balloon-borne research project connected to [Project Mogul]({{ 'project-mogul/' | relative_url }}) and found no documentary evidence of extraterrestrial materials or a hidden recovery programme.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.af.mil/The-Roswell-Report/About-Us/AF-75th-Anniversary/About-Us/AF-75th-Anniversary/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: af.mil">[Air Force]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Air Force The Roswell Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Air Force The Roswell Report</span></span></span>

For the communication history of Roswell, the more cautious conclusion is therefore the most useful one: the Ramey memo shows officials managing information during a high-profile military incident, but it does not provide a complete transcript of their internal discussions. It is strongest as evidence of the response mechanism itself — a chain of reports, command review and public messaging — rather than as a standalone proof of any particular explanation of the event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/military/air-force/ufos" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[archives.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project BLUE BOOK</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unidentified Flying Objects &#124; National ArchivesJune 25, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2024</span></span></span>
