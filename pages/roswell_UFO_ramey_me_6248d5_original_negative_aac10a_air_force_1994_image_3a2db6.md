## Introduction

The most important fact about the US Air Force's 1994 examination of the [Ramey]({{ 'ramey/' | relative_url }}) photographs is that investigators were not limited to the degraded newspaper reproductions or small digital copies familiar to later internet users. According to the Air Force's own Roswell investigation, researchers obtained original, first-generation photographic prints from the University of Texas at Arlington (UTA), subsequently obtained first-generation negatives, and submitted the relevant imagery for digitisation and specialist interpretation. The result, reported on 20 July 1994, was [negative]({{ 'negative/' | relative_url }}): the imagery was still considered insufficient to resolve the fine details investigators wanted to see.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/decade-1990s-roswell-report-fact-vs-fiction-1994-dtic-ada326148" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">decade 1990s roswell report fact vs fiction 1994 dtic ada326148</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyRoswell Report: Fact vs Fiction, 1994, U.S. Air Force Headquarters (Office of Air Force History) · 1994 · UFO Transparenc...</span></span></span>

That episode is particularly important to the debate over the [Ramey memo]({{ 'ramey-memo/' | relative_url }}) because it tests a simple explanation for its persistent unreadability: that researchers have merely been working from bad copies. The 1994 investigation reached much closer to the photographic source, yet still could not extract a dependable reading. It therefore points to a harder limitation — not merely later copying, but the amount and quality of information recorded in the 1947 photograph itself.

## What photographic material investigators obtained from UTA

The relevant passage appears in the Air Force investigation prepared in response to the General Accounting Office inquiry into Roswell. Investigators reported obtaining from UTA's archives a set of what they explicitly described as original, or "first generation", prints of the Fort Worth Star-Telegram photographs showing Brigadier General Roger Ramey and Major [Jesse Marcel]({{ 'marcel/' | relative_url }}) with the debris. They subsequently obtained a set of first-generation negatives as well.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/decade-1990s-roswell-report-fact-vs-fiction-1994-dtic-ada326148" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">decade 1990s roswell report fact vs fiction 1994 dtic ada326148</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyRoswell Report: Fact vs Fiction, 1994, U.S. Air Force Headquarters (Office of Air Force History) · 1994 · UFO Transparenc...</span></span></span>

This [provenance]({{ 'provenance/' | relative_url }}) is what makes the 1994 episode more useful than an ordinary attempt to enhance a published Ramey photograph. The investigators were working with archival photographic material very near the beginning of the reproduction chain. The surviving negative associated with the Ramey image is held in UTA's Fort Worth Star-Telegram collection; later researchers have likewise described examining the large-format negative itself rather than relying exclusively on newspaper or web reproductions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2016/05/the-ramey-memo-negative.html?showComment=1462514194505" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: The Ramey Memo Negative</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: The Ramey Memo NegativeMay 5, 2016...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 5, 2016</span></span></span>

The Air Force was interested in two unusually fine details in the photographs. One was the writing on the sheet of paper visible in Ramey's hand — the object now normally called the Ramey memo. The other was whether the photographed wreckage showed the patterned or "flowered" tape that witnesses and later Roswell accounts associated with supposedly unusual markings. The investigation therefore required considerably more than recognising people or large objects in the room: it depended on recovering very small structures inside the photographic frame.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.users.globalnet.co.uk/~hex/ufo/text/pages026-050/page33.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: users.globalnet.co.uk">[GlobalNet]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">users.globalnet.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Global Netpage33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">page33 - Air Force Roswell Report...</span></span></span>

The memo presented the more consequential documentary possibility. The Air Force report says investigators hoped its text might provide information useful in locating further records relating to Roswell. In other words, the 1994 exercise was not described merely as an attempt to make an interesting photograph look clearer. Reading the paper could potentially have generated a new documentary lead.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/decade-1990s-roswell-report-fact-vs-fiction-1994-dtic-ada326148" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">decade 1990s roswell report fact vs fiction 1994 dtic ada326148</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyRoswell Report: Fact vs Fiction, 1994, U.S. Air Force Headquarters (Office of Air Force History) · 1994 · UFO Transparenc...</span></span></span>

## What the national-level analysis reported in July 1994

The Air Force sent the photograph containing the visible writing to an unnamed "national-level organization" for digitisation followed by photographic interpretation and analysis. The same organisation was asked to examine the digitised photographs for the disputed markings on the wreckage. The report does not identify the organisation, describe its scanner, give the digital sampling resolution, reproduce a technical laboratory report, or explain its image-processing procedures. Those omissions limit how precisely the test can be evaluated today.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.users.globalnet.co.uk/~hex/ufo/text/pages026-050/page33.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: users.globalnet.co.uk">[GlobalNet]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">users.globalnet.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Global Netpage33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">page33 - Air Force Roswell Report...</span></span></span>

What the Air Force did record is the result. On **20 July 1994**, the organisation reported that, even after digitisation, the photographs were of insufficient quality to visualise either of the requested details. Thus the exercise produced neither a readable memo nor a photographic identification of the disputed tape markings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/decade-1990s-roswell-report-fact-vs-fiction-1994-dtic-ada326148" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">decade 1990s roswell report fact vs fiction 1994 dtic ada326148</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyRoswell Report: Fact vs Fiction, 1994, U.S. Air Force Headquarters (Office of Air Force History) · 1994 · UFO Transparenc...</span></span></span>

There is an important qualification. "Insufficient quality" did not mean that the photographs contained no recoverable quantitative information at all. The Air Force says the same analysis could derive measurements from the sticks visible among the debris once investigators established, through an interview with the original photographer, what camera he had used. Those measurements were considered compatible with the wooden components of the radar target being investigated by the Air Force.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://crashdown.com/news/2000/01/the-real-roswell-incident-part-nine/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: crashdown.com">[Crashdown]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">crashdown.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Real “Roswell Incident” Part Nine – Crashdown.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Real “Roswell Incident” Part Nine – Crashdown.comJanuary 11, 2000...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 11, 2000</span></span></span>

That contrast is revealing. Large, geometrically defined objects in the photograph could support measurement, while the far finer information needed to distinguish individual characters on Ramey's paper could not. The 1994 result therefore should not be paraphrased as "the photograph was useless". It was useful at one scale and inadequate at another.

Nor did the Air Force report establish that no character anywhere on the sheet could possibly be recognised. Its conclusion was narrower: the photographic analysis could not visualise the details sought well enough for the investigative purpose. That distinction matters because later claims that isolated words or letter-like shapes can be perceived are not necessarily incompatible with the 1994 result. The stronger claim — that the complete disputed text can be reliably transcribed — requires much more information than occasionally recognising a plausible character or word.

## Why the result challenges the poor-web-copy explanation

The 1994 test creates a useful evidential checkpoint. If the memo were unreadable only because modern observers normally encountered low-resolution newspaper reproductions, compressed JPEGs or copies several generations removed from the source, moving back to UTA's first-generation photographic material should have removed much of that disadvantage. Yet the Air Force reported that specialist digitisation still failed to resolve the requested fine detail.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/decade-1990s-roswell-report-fact-vs-fiction-1994-dtic-ada326148" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">decade 1990s roswell report fact vs fiction 1994 dtic ada326148</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyRoswell Report: Fact vs Fiction, 1994, U.S. Air Force Headquarters (Office of Air Force History) · 1994 · UFO Transparenc...</span></span></span>

That does **not** prove that every subsequent attempt must fail. Scanning equipment, digital storage and image-processing methods have improved considerably since 1994, and later investigators have returned to the UTA material using newer equipment. In 2015, for example, a team arranged new scans of the negative at UTA, producing digital files reportedly approaching 250 MB. Kevin Randle, who participated in later Ramey-memo research, wrote that the hoped-for immediate breakthrough did not occur and that the resulting analyses had not produced universal agreement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/10/digital-image-of-ramey-memo.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: Digital Image of the Ramey Memo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: Digital Image of the Ramey MemoOctober 29, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 29, 2015</span></span></span>

More recent work illustrates just how small the target is. A 2022 *Journal of Scientific Exploration* paper discussing renewed examination of the negative reported a full-frame digital image roughly 3,663 by 2,743 pixels in which the message extended only about 148.5 pixels in one dimension; later work therefore included microscopic imaging of the negative itself. The same paper concluded that attempts to decipher the memo had still not produced definitive results.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.journalofscientificexploration.org/index.php/jse/article/download/2635/1631" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journalofscientificexploration.org">[Journal of Scientific Exploration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journalofscientificexploration.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Journal of Scientific Exploration Anomalistics</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Journal of Scientific ExplorationAnomalisticsAugust 21, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 21, 2022</span></span></span>

Those later efforts strengthen the significance of the 1994 failure without making it conclusive. Better access and larger files can reduce losses introduced during copying or scanning, but they cannot automatically create character information that the camera lens, [film grain]({{ 'film-grain/' | relative_url }}), focus, viewing angle and distance failed to distinguish in 1947. The key question is therefore not simply how many pixels a modern scan contains. It is whether distinguishable optical information corresponding to the individual typewritten characters exists in the underlying film.

## A failed reading is different from a proven transcription

Later researchers have nevertheless proposed readings of the Ramey memo, sometimes claiming to identify words with major implications for the Roswell story. Such claims are precisely where the 1994 result becomes an important control: a transcription needs to explain why supposedly clear textual information escaped a specialist examination working from early-generation archival material. That does not make a later reading impossible, but it raises the evidential bar.

An experimental study published by James Houran and Kevin Randle in 2002 illustrates the problem from another direction. Participants were shown the ambiguous memo imagery under different contextual suggestions — Roswell, atomic-bomb testing or no stated context. Many reported seeing words, but their interpretations tended to reflect the suggestions they had received. Some character sequences appeared more consistently across groups, so the authors did not dismiss further analysis as pointless; instead, they argued that future attempts required stronger methodological controls.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=e162b50a3885a28c826cb31c3deca69abaaba9ee&amp;repid=rep1&amp;type=pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: citeseerx.ist.psu.edu">[CiteSeerX]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">citeseerx.ist.psu.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on psu.edu.</span></span></span>

That finding helps distinguish two questions that are easily blurred together. One is whether **some** textual structure survives in the photograph. The other is whether enough survives to choose reliably among competing words and sentences. The 1994 Air Force analysis bears primarily on the second problem. It showed that access to first-generation photographic material plus digitisation did not automatically turn the memo into an ordinary readable document.

Later technical investigations have likewise reported difficulty obtaining clearly interpretable text. A subsequent review summarised analyses using methods including deconvolution, Fourier processing, filtering, wavelets and deblurring; several specialists reported either that noise remained a major obstacle or that no clearly interpretable text resulted.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.scribd.com/document/667605767/UFO" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scribd.com">[Scribd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scribd.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Update on the Roswell UFO Incident &#124; PDF &#124; Unidentified Flying Object &#124; Science</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Update on the Roswell UFO Incident &#124; PDF &#124; Unidentified Flying Object &#124; Science</span></span></span> This does not settle every disputed letter, but it makes the persistent lack of consensus harder to attribute solely to unsophisticated software or poor internet reproductions.

## What the 1994 test actually establishes

The July 1994 result is strongest when treated as a limited empirical finding rather than as a verdict on Roswell. It does not establish what Ramey's paper said. It does not prove that no future technique could extract additional information. And because the Air Force did not identify the analysing organisation or publish detailed scanning parameters and laboratory documentation, the test cannot now be reproduced exactly from the description in the report.

What it does establish is more specific and highly relevant to the original-negative-versus-digital-copy question. By 1994, an official investigation had obtained first-generation Ramey photographic material from UTA and had deliberately attempted to recover the memo's text through digitisation and specialist image interpretation. The attempt still failed to resolve the requested detail.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/decade-1990s-roswell-report-fact-vs-fiction-1994-dtic-ada326148" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">decade 1990s roswell report fact vs fiction 1994 dtic ada326148</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyRoswell Report: Fact vs Fiction, 1994, U.S. Air Force Headquarters (Office of Air Force History) · 1994 · UFO Transparenc...</span></span></span>

That is why the episode remains a valuable boundary condition in assessing later Ramey-memo claims. A poor web image certainly can conceal information, and returning to an original negative is preferable to analysing a heavily reproduced copy. But the 1994 test demonstrates that reproduction generation is not the whole problem. Later high-resolution work has continued to encounter ambiguity, while controlled research has shown how easily expectations can influence readings of the indistinct characters.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/10/digital-image-of-ramey-memo.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: Digital Image of the Ramey Memo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: Digital Image of the Ramey MemoOctober 29, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 29, 2015</span></span></span>

The cautious conclusion is therefore neither that the memo contains no recoverable text nor that modern enhancement can reveal whatever investigators wish to find. It is that the surviving first-generation photographic evidence itself appears to impose a severe information limit. The 1994 Air Force examination matters because it encountered that limit close to the archival source — decades before today's disputed transcriptions became widely circulated online.
