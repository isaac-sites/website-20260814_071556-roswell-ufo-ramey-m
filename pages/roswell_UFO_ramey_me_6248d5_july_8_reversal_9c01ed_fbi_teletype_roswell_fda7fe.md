## Introduction

The FBI teletype sent from Dallas at 6:17 p.m. on 8 July 1947 is one of the most useful surviving documents for understanding the hours in which the Roswell story changed from a recovered “flying disc” to a balloon. Its importance is not that the FBI independently identified the wreckage, nor that it proves an extraterrestrial explanation. The Bureau had not examined the material. Rather, the message records what [Eighth Air Force]({{ 'command-control/' | relative_url }}) headquarters at Fort Worth was telling the FBI while the explanation was still developing. The recovered object, the FBI was told, “resembles a high altitude weather balloon with a radar reflector”, yet a telephone conversation between Fort Worth and [Wright Field]({{ 'wright-field/' | relative_url }}) “had not borne out this belief”; the material was supposedly being sent to Wright Field for examination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.fbi.gov/video-repository/inside-the-fbi-ufos-102921.mp4/view" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fbi.gov">[FBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Inside the FBI Podcast: UFOs — FBI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Inside the FBI Podcast: UFOs — FBI...</span></span></span>

That combination is the key. Contemporary public reporting was moving towards a categorical Rawin [radar-target]({{ 'radar-target/' | relative_url }}) identification, while the internal FBI communication retained conditional language and an unresolved reference to Wright Field. The teletype therefore does not settle what crashed near Roswell. What it does unusually well is preserve a snapshot of the explanation **during the transition itself**.

## What Eighth Air Force told the FBI

The document is headed “FBI Dallas”, dated 8 July 1947 and timed at 6:17 p.m. It was addressed urgently to the FBI Director and the Special Agent in Charge at Cincinnati. The FBI's own archive now describes it as a Dallas Field Office teletype concerning a “flying disc” resembling a high-altitude weather balloon recovered near Roswell. The Government Accountability Office — then called the General Accounting Office (GAO) — subsequently authenticated the message through the FBI during its congressionally requested Roswell records investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://vault.fbi.gov/search?SearchableText=roswell" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vault.fbi.gov">[FBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vault.fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">— Federal Bureau of Investigation</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FBI — Federal Bureau of Investigation...</span></span></span>

The information did not originate with an FBI examination. According to the teletype, Eighth Air Force headquarters had telephoned the Dallas FBI office. The message reported that an object “purporting to be a flying disc” had been recovered near Roswell that day. It then supplied a surprisingly concrete description: the “disc” was hexagonal and suspended by cable from a balloon said to be approximately twenty feet in diameter.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.fbi.gov/video-repository/inside-the-fbi-ufos-102921.mp4/view" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fbi.gov">[FBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Inside the FBI Podcast: UFOs — FBI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Inside the FBI Podcast: UFOs — FBI...</span></span></span>

This detail is important because it places the balloon-and-reflector interpretation firmly inside the documentary record on 8 July itself. The idea was not invented decades later when the Air Force investigated Roswell again. The FBI record explicitly says that the object resembled a high-altitude weather balloon with a radar reflector. In other words, whatever later disputes arose over Project Mogul, substitution of debris or [witness testimony]({{ 'witness-testimony/' | relative_url }}), the basic balloon/radar-target interpretation was already being communicated within the federal government on the evening of the original event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.fbi.gov/video-repository/inside-the-fbi-ufos-102921.mp4/view" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fbi.gov">[FBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Inside the FBI Podcast: UFOs — FBI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Inside the FBI Podcast: UFOs — FBI...</span></span></span>

That also limits what can reasonably be claimed for the teletype as evidence of something exotic. Its own physical description contains a balloon, a cable and a hexagonal object compared with a radar reflector. The FBI did not record alien bodies, unusual propulsion, extraordinary metals or an extraterrestrial origin. Nor did it describe the Bureau as disputing the Army Air Forces' account.

Yet the message did not simply say, “It was a weather balloon.” Its wording was more complicated.

## Why “resembles” and “not borne out” matter

The most consequential sentence says that the recovered object **resembled** a high-altitude weather balloon with a radar reflector, “but” that a telephone conversation between Eighth Air Force headquarters and Wright Field had not borne out that belief.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.fbi.gov/video-repository/inside-the-fbi-ufos-102921.mp4/view" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fbi.gov">[FBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Inside the FBI Podcast: UFOs — FBI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Inside the FBI Podcast: UFOs — FBI...</span></span></span>

Two parts deserve to be kept together.

First, “resembles” expresses comparison rather than final identification. It is evidence that Fort Worth had a balloon hypothesis, and evidently a fairly specific one, but the surviving sentence does not frame that hypothesis as an accomplished technical determination.

Second comes the qualification about Wright Field. This has sometimes been interpreted in UFO literature as evidence that Wright Field had ruled out a weather balloon. That is stronger than the document warrants. The message says only that a telephone conversation had “not borne out this belief”. It does not reproduce the conversation, identify what Wright Field personnel actually said, establish that Wright Field had inspected the debris, or state that Wright Field concluded the object was something extraordinary. Indeed, the next sentence says the material was being transported to Wright Field “for examination”, strongly suggesting that whatever happened during the telephone call was not itself a physical examination of the Roswell wreckage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.fbi.gov/video-repository/inside-the-fbi-ufos-102921.mp4/view" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fbi.gov">[FBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Inside the FBI Podcast: UFOs — FBI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Inside the FBI Podcast: UFOs — FBI...</span></span></span>

But the opposite reading — that the balloon explanation was already wholly finalised — is also difficult to reconcile with the literal text. A belief that “resembles” a particular type of apparatus, has not yet been borne out through a telephone exchange with the technical centre, and is followed by plans for examination is plainly less categorical than a final identification.

The careful conclusion is therefore narrower than either pole of the Roswell debate. At the moment represented by the teletype, **Eighth Air Force was giving the FBI a balloon-and-radar-reflector explanation, but the information transmitted to the Bureau still contained an explicit qualification about its confirmation**.

## The 6:17 p.m. message versus the public explanation

The evidential value becomes clearer when the teletype is compared with contemporary press reporting from the same evening.

A surviving account from the *Dallas Morning News*, reproduced by the historical research project Project 1947, reported that Major E. M. Kirton, intelligence officer at Fort Worth Army Air Field, spoke to the newspaper at 5:30 p.m. Kirton described the object as a “Rawin high altitude sounding device”, explained that such equipment was attached to a balloon, and said it contained tinfoil-like material. More importantly, his public language was categorical: “The identification at Fort Worth is final”, he said, adding that it would therefore not be necessary to forward the object to Wright Field as originally planned.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/roswell/rosearch.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947 - The Search For Records On Roswell...</span></span></span>

That account sits awkwardly beside the 6:17 p.m. FBI teletype. Less than an hour separates the reported 5:30 statement from the FBI timestamp. Yet the two surviving records preserve materially different descriptions of the status of the case.

To the newspaper, the identification was **final**, and the Wright Field trip was **unnecessary**.

In the FBI teletype, the object **resembled** balloon-and-reflector equipment, the Wright Field telephone conversation had **not borne out the belief**, and the disc and balloon were **being transported to Wright Field by special plane for examination**.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.fbi.gov/video-repository/inside-the-fbi-ufos-102921.mp4/view" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fbi.gov">[FBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Inside the FBI Podcast: UFOs — FBI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Inside the FBI Podcast: UFOs — FBI...</span></span></span>

That discrepancy is more useful than attempts to force the two records into a dramatic conspiracy narrative. Several ordinary explanations remain possible. Information could have been moving rapidly and inconsistently through Eighth Air Force headquarters. The FBI message may have embodied information received somewhat earlier than its transmission time. Different officials may have had different understandings of whether the Wright Field flight remained necessary. A decision to cancel the examination could have been made while the teletype was being prepared or transmitted.

The surviving documents do not allow those possibilities to be ranked confidently. What they do demonstrate is that the transition to the balloon explanation was not documented identically across every channel.

## Wright Field is the unresolved hinge

Wright Field matters because it was the Army Air Forces' major technical and research centre in Ohio. Early Associated Press reporting had already said that General Roger [Ramey]({{ 'ramey/' | relative_url }}) described the recovered object as being shipped by air to the Army Air Forces research centre there. A contemporary AP report printed by the *Seattle Daily Times* on 8 July said Ramey had received the object from Roswell and that it was being shipped to Wright Field.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/rw/p/seattledailytimes8jul1947.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOS at close sight: The Roswell 1947 incident in the Press, The Seattle Daily Times, July 8, 1947July 8, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

The FBI teletype fits that early version. It says the disc and balloon were being transported to Wright Field by special aircraft for examination and adds that Wright Field would be asked to advise the FBI's Cincinnati office of the examination results.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/rw/f/fbimemo01.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOS at close sight: Roswell 1947, declassified official documents, FBI memo of July 8, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

Yet the later public account said the Fort Worth identification made the journey unnecessary. That creates a concrete historical question: was the material actually sent?

The GAO tried to answer it in the 1990s and could not substantiate the journey or examination from surviving Wright Field records. Because the FBI teletype specifically mentioned transportation to Wright Field, GAO investigators reviewed Air Materiel Command records from 1947 through 1950. They found **no records mentioning the Roswell crash or an examination of Roswell debris by Air Materiel Command personnel**. They also reviewed FBI Dallas and Cincinnati activity abstracts. A Dallas abstract dated 12 July summarised the 8 July message, but the Cincinnati abstracts contained no mention of the Roswell crash or recovery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sgp.fas.org/othergov/roswell.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sgp.fas.org">[FAS Project on Government Secrecy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sgp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project on Government Secrecysgp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FAS Project on Government Secrecysgp.fas.org...</span></span></span>

That is an important negative finding. It means the teletype documents an intention or reported action, not proof that the examination actually occurred. Claims that the document proves Roswell debris reached Wright Field go beyond the available record.

Equally, the absence of a surviving Wright Field examination report cannot demonstrate that nothing was transported. The correct evidential statement is simply that the FBI message says transportation was under way, whereas GAO's later search found no surviving Air Materiel Command record confirming either receipt or examination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sgp.fas.org/othergov/roswell.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sgp.fas.org">[FAS Project on Government Secrecy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sgp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project on Government Secrecysgp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FAS Project on Government Secrecysgp.fas.org...</span></span></span>

## Why the FBI was receiving the information

The closing lines of the teletype help explain its existence. Eighth Air Force supplied the information because of the “national interest” in the case and because the National Broadcasting Company, Associated Press and others were trying to break the story of the disc's location. The message then says that no further FBI investigation was being conducted.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/rw/f/fbimemo01.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOS at close sight: Roswell 1947, declassified official documents, FBI memo of July 8, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

This is an important corrective to the common description of the document as an “FBI investigation” of Roswell. It was not that. The Bureau was a recipient of information supplied by the military. Nothing in the message indicates that FBI personnel collected the Roswell debris, inspected it, interviewed the principal Roswell witnesses or reached an independent conclusion about its origin.

The FBI itself now presents the document in those limited terms: on 8 July its Dallas Field Office sent a teletype concerning a flying disc that resembled a high-altitude weather balloon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://vault.fbi.gov/search?SearchableText=roswell" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vault.fbi.gov">[FBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vault.fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">— Federal Bureau of Investigation</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FBI — Federal Bureau of Investigation...</span></span></span>

The media references also establish how closely the communication was tied to the rapidly moving news cycle. Roswell Army Air Field had publicly announced possession of a “flying disc”, and contemporary Associated Press reporting said the object had been inspected at Roswell and passed to higher headquarters. Reporters were then trying to determine where it was and what the Army Air Forces had recovered.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/rw/p/seattledailytimes8jul1947.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOS at close sight: The Roswell 1947 incident in the Press, The Seattle Daily Times, July 8, 1947July 8, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

The FBI message therefore belongs to a communications crisis as much as an intelligence file. Eighth Air Force was dealing simultaneously with Roswell personnel, Wright Field, federal law enforcement and national news organisations. That environment makes small differences in wording especially important — but also cautions against assuming every inconsistency represents a deliberate deception.

## What the GAO search adds to the teletype

Nearly five decades later, the GAO conducted a broad federal records search at the request of Representative Steven Schiff. Its findings substantially increase the teletype's historical importance because they demonstrate how sparse the surviving contemporary Roswell record actually is.

GAO reported finding only **two government records originating in 1947 concerning the Roswell incident**: the July 1947 history of the combined 509th Bomb Group and Roswell Army Air Field, and the 8 July FBI teletype. The unit history said the Roswell public information office had been busy answering enquiries about the “flying disc” and that the object ultimately “turned out to be a radar tracking balloon”. The FBI document independently recorded the Eighth Air Force's weather-balloon/radar-reflector description on the day the story broke.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sgp.fas.org/othergov/roswell.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sgp.fas.org">[FAS Project on Government Secrecy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sgp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project on Government Secrecysgp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FAS Project on Government Secrecysgp.fas.org...</span></span></span>

GAO also discovered that Roswell Army Air Field administrative records covering March 1945 through December 1949 and outgoing messages covering October 1946 through December 1949 had been destroyed. The disposition documentation did not specify who destroyed them, when the destruction occurred or the authority under which it happened. The National Personnel Records Center's chief archivist told GAO that missing disposition citations were not unique to Roswell-era Air Force records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sgp.fas.org/othergov/roswell.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sgp.fas.org">[FAS Project on Government Secrecy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sgp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project on Government Secrecysgp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FAS Project on Government Secrecysgp.fas.org...</span></span></span>

This matters because it limits attempts to reconstruct the exact sequence of communications on 8 July. The destruction of records is not, by itself, evidence that incriminating Roswell material was deliberately eliminated. GAO did not reach such a conclusion. But it means that the surviving FBI teletype cannot simply be checked against a complete archive of outgoing Roswell messages.

That scarcity gives the 6:17 p.m. record disproportionate importance: it is one of the few contemporary bureaucratic traces left from the day itself.

## How the teletype bears on the official Project Mogul explanation

The Air Force's 1994 reinvestigation concluded that the most likely source of the recovered material was a balloon train associated with Project Mogul, a classified programme intended to help detect Soviet nuclear activity. The Air Force emphasised that Mogul configurations used balloons and radar reflectors, making the physical description preserved in the FBI message broadly consistent with that class of equipment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.af.mil/The-Roswell-Report/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: af.mil">[Air Force]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Air Force The Roswell Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Air Force The Roswell Report</span></span></span>

The teletype therefore supplies genuine contemporary support for the material side of the later balloon explanation. A hexagonal object associated with a balloon and compared with a radar reflector is much easier to reconcile with balloon-borne radar equipment than with later stories of an intact extraterrestrial spacecraft.

But the teletype does **not** identify Project Mogul. It does not use that name, identify a flight number, describe acoustic sensors or say that officials knew the material belonged to a classified detection programme. The connection to Mogul is a later historical reconstruction made from other records. The distinction is important: the 1947 document supports the existence of a balloon/radar-reflector interpretation, while the [1994 Air Force]({{ '1994-air-force/' | relative_url }}) investigation supplied the more specific Mogul attribution.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.af.mil/The-Roswell-Report/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: af.mil">[Air Force]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Air Force The Roswell Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Air Force The Roswell Report</span></span></span>

That distinction also explains why the teletype can simultaneously support the broad official explanation and reveal uncertainty within the first evening's communications. There is nothing contradictory about officials recognising that debris looked like balloon equipment while still checking precisely what equipment it was.

## What the teletype fixes in the July 8 timeline

For reconstructing the Roswell reversal, the teletype establishes several points more securely than later recollections can.

**First, the balloon interpretation was already circulating on 8 July.** It was being communicated privately from Eighth Air Force headquarters to the FBI, not merely announced to reporters or invented retrospectively.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.fbi.gov/video-repository/inside-the-fbi-ufos-102921.mp4/view" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fbi.gov">[FBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Inside the FBI Podcast: UFOs — FBI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Inside the FBI Podcast: UFOs — FBI...</span></span></span>

**Second, the internal wording preserved at 6:17 p.m. was less categorical than at least one public statement attributed to Eighth Air Force intelligence.** The FBI received “resembles” and “had not borne out this belief”; the *Dallas Morning News* reported Kirton saying that the Fort Worth identification was “final”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.fbi.gov/video-repository/inside-the-fbi-ufos-102921.mp4/view" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fbi.gov">[FBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Inside the FBI Podcast: UFOs — FBI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Inside the FBI Podcast: UFOs — FBI...</span></span></span>

**Third, Wright Field's role was changing in the surviving accounts.** Early AP coverage and the FBI message contemplated shipment there; Kirton's reported public statement said that shipment was unnecessary. GAO later found no Wright Field record proving that the examination occurred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/rw/p/seattledailytimes8jul1947.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[patrickgross.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOS at close sight: The Roswell 1947 incident in the Press, The Seattle Daily Times, July 8, 1947July 8, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

**Fourth, the FBI itself was not pursuing a separate Roswell case.** The teletype explicitly terminated its account with “no further investigation being conducted”, and GAO found no Cincinnati follow-up in the July activity abstracts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/rw/f/fbimemo01.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOS at close sight: Roswell 1947, declassified official documents, FBI memo of July 8, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

These are modest findings, but they are documentary rather than retrospective.

## What the teletype means for the Ramey memo debate

The FBI document is especially valuable when placed beside the disputed Ramey memo visible in photographs taken in General Roger Ramey's Fort Worth office. The University of Texas at Arlington preserves the original J. Bond Johnson photographic material and notes that the telegram or memo in Ramey's hand has been intensely studied but has **not been definitively deciphered**.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/spcouta/36080122212" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: flickr.com">[Flickr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">flickr.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Roswell UFO Incident, July 1947 &#124;...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 1947</span></span></span>

That evidential difference should govern how the two records are used.

The photographed Ramey document is potentially fascinating because it was physically present at headquarters during the reversal. But attempts to extract words such as “victims”, “disc”, “weather balloons” or other contested readings depend upon image enhancement and interpretation of extremely degraded text. The FBI teletype, by contrast, is a legible contemporary government communication whose authenticity was confirmed to GAO by the FBI.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://sgp.fas.org/othergov/roswell.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sgp.fas.org">[FAS Project on Government Secrecy]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sgp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project on Government Secrecysgp.fas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FAS Project on Government Secrecysgp.fas.org...</span></span></span>

Accordingly, speculative readings of the Ramey memo should not be used to overwrite what the FBI document actually establishes. The teletype supplies a firmer baseline for the information environment surrounding Ramey's headquarters: balloon-and-radar-reflector language was already present, Wright Field had been consulted, confirmation was expressed with qualification, further examination was reportedly planned, and intense press interest was shaping communications.

## The strongest conclusion the document supports

The FBI teletype is neither the “smoking gun” sometimes claimed by Roswell proponents nor an unambiguous record of a completely settled weather-balloon identification.

Its physical description weighs towards balloon-borne equipment. Its provenance shows that this interpretation came from Eighth Air Force headquarters on 8 July 1947. Its explicit statement that the FBI was conducting no further investigation undermines portrayals of the document as evidence of a secret Bureau investigation into an extraterrestrial crash. The later Air Force Mogul explanation is also broadly compatible with the balloon, cable and radar-reflector features preserved in the message.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.fbi.gov/video-repository/inside-the-fbi-ufos-102921.mp4/view" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fbi.gov">[fbi.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Inside the FBI Podcast: UFOs — FBI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Inside the FBI Podcast: UFOs — FBI...</span></span></span>

But its chronology prevents an equally simplistic reading in the other direction. At 6:17 p.m., the internal message still said “resembles”; it still recorded that a conversation with Wright Field had not borne out the belief; and it still described transportation to Wright Field for examination. Meanwhile, contemporary newspaper reporting attributed to Major Kirton a much firmer public position: the Rawin identification was final and Wright Field examination was no longer necessary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.fbi.gov/video-repository/inside-the-fbi-ufos-102921.mp4/view" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fbi.gov">[FBI]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Inside the FBI Podcast: UFOs — FBI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Inside the FBI Podcast: UFOs — FBI...</span></span></span>

That is the distinctive contribution of the FBI teletype to the [July 8]({{ 'july-8/' | relative_url }}) Roswell reversal. It does not tell us that the balloon explanation was false. It shows that the explanation did not appear in every surviving channel at precisely the same stage of certainty.

For the history of the Ramey memo and the Fort Worth reversal, that distinction is crucial. The photographs show a document in Ramey's hand while the public story was changing, but their text remains disputed. The FBI teletype gives us something firmer: a readable, authenticated, time-stamped record from the same evening. It captures Eighth Air Force headquarters not before the balloon hypothesis existed and not after every question had disappeared, but in the narrow interval when **the balloon explanation had become the leading account while its status, its Wright Field confirmation and even the planned disposition of the debris were still represented differently across internal and public communications**.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.fbi.gov/video-repository/inside-the-fbi-ufos-102921.mp4/view" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fbi.gov">[fbi.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fbi.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Inside the FBI Podcast: UFOs — FBI</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Inside the FBI Podcast: UFOs — FBI...</span></span></span>
