## Introduction

Yes. Image straightening can change what a blurred [Ramey memo]({{ 'ramey-memo/' | relative_url }}) character appears to be, even when the underlying photograph has not changed. The reason is that rectification is not simply a neutral rotation of the picture. Correcting a folded, obliquely photographed sheet requires an analyst or algorithm to decide how points on the bent page should map to positions on an imagined flat page. Different control points, target proportions and local warp strengths can therefore stretch, compress or curve the few pixels that make up an uncertain character.

This is especially important in the [Ramey]({{ 'ramey/' | relative_url }}) memo because the surviving photograph records the document at limited resolution while the paper itself is folded and differently oriented across the frame. The University of Texas at Arlington archive identifies the preserved image as a 1947 Fort Worth Star-Telegram [negative]({{ 'negative/' | relative_url }}) showing General Roger Ramey holding the telegram.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mavmatrix.uta.edu/specialcollections_startelegram1940s/35502/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mavmatrix.uta.edu">[MavMatrix]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mavmatrix.uta.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mav Matrix General Ramey holding enlargement of telegram</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">General Ramey holding enlargement of telegramJuly 8, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: [July 8](&amp;#123;&amp;#123; &#x27;july-8/&#x27; &#124; relative_url &amp;#125;&amp;#125;), 1947</span></span></span> Rectification may make that photograph easier to inspect, but a more letter-like result is not automatically stronger evidence for that letter.

## How control points determine the warp

A simple perspective correction works reasonably well when a document can be treated as one flat plane. Four corners can define a transformation that turns a photographed quadrilateral back towards a rectangle. A folded sheet is fundamentally different: its upper section, central crease and lower section need not share the same orientation. Correcting those areas requires a non-linear or piecewise transformation rather than one global adjustment.

Modern document-dewarping research makes the mechanics explicit. One published method estimates corresponding **control points** on the distorted and rectified documents, interpolates between those points and then remaps the source pixels into the flattened image. Importantly, its authors describe the control points as adjustable and note that their number can be varied for different applications.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.10543" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">arXiv Document Dewarping with Control Points</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Document Dewarping with Control PointsMarch 20, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 20, 2022</span></span></span> More recent rectification systems likewise use grids of predicted control points to construct the mapping between distorted and supposedly flat document geometry.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41157359/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DocCPLNet: Document Image Rectification via Control Point and Illumination Correction - PubMedOctober 11, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 11, 2025</span></span></span>

That matters because the transformation between control points is an inference about geometry. Imagine three faint pixels that seem to form the diagonal stroke of a letter. If nearby control points tell the software that this patch must be stretched vertically, those pixels move farther apart vertically. Move the control points slightly differently and the same pixels can become wider, narrower or differently sloped. The processing has not discovered another ink mark; it has changed the spatial relationship between already recorded samples.

The Ramey memo provides a particularly direct example because David [Rudiak]({{ 'rudiak/' | relative_url }}) has described doing exactly this kind of geometric reconstruction. In a 2018 discussion of his processing, he said that he used Abrosoft FantaMorph to flatten and straighten the message, attempting to correct perspective, make letters more uniform in size and spacing, straighten text lines and compensate for [folds]({{ 'folds/' | relative_url }}). He also said that poorly visible letters required some “educated guesswork” about their positions and that those choices affected his warp model.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2018/12/david-rudiaks-latest-on-ramey-memo.html?showComment=1546118226661" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: David Rudiak&#x27;s Latest on the Ramey MemoDecember 19, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 19, 2018</span></span></span> Earlier, he similarly described using commercial morphing software to flatten the memo and undo folds and curves.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2016/05/the-ramey-memo-negative.html?showComment=1462541425906" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: The Ramey Memo Negative</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: The Ramey Memo NegativeMay 5, 2016...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 5, 2016</span></span></span>

That acknowledgement identifies the crucial evidential issue. If the position of an indistinct character helps determine the warp, and the resulting warp then makes that character look clearer, the corrected appearance is not wholly independent evidence for the proposed character. Some of the apparent regularity has been supplied by the geometric model.

## Why bent regions are unusually sensitive

The problem becomes most acute where the paper bends sharply. In a relatively flat portion of the memo, neighbouring characters have experienced roughly similar perspective distortion, so a modest geometric correction may be reasonably stable. Around a fold, however, the orientation of the paper changes over a short distance. The correction consequently has to change rapidly as well.

Current document-rectification research treats folds, curls and wrinkles as non-linear geometric distortions rather than ordinary skew. Techniques designed for these documents explicitly model local as well as global deformation, because straightening only the page as a whole does not adequately restore distorted text lines.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12567372/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov">[PubMed Central (PMC)]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PubMed Central (PMC)DocCPLNet: Document Image Rectification via Control Point and Illumination Correction - PMC...</span></span></span> Multi-stage approaches have likewise used page-level correction followed by finer line-level dewarping where granular corrections remain necessary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.06872" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Multistage Curvilinear Coordinate Transform Based Document Image Dewarping using a Novel Quality EstimatorMarch 15, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 15, 2020</span></span></span>

The Ramey photograph contains precisely these difficult conditions. Rudiak identifies the area above the upper fold, the middle fold and the left side near Ramey's thumb as especially distorted. He reports that his flattening process can smear characters there and that shadows and deformation make the original letter positions difficult to establish.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2018/12/david-rudiaks-latest-on-ramey-memo.html?showComment=1546118226661" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: David Rudiak&#x27;s Latest on the Ramey MemoDecember 19, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 19, 2018</span></span></span> Independent descriptions of the surviving image also note that portions of the memo were folded and did not directly face the camera.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/09/ramey-memo-update.html?showComment=1443576324320" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: Ramey Memo Update</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: Ramey Memo UpdateSeptember 26, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 26, 2015</span></span></span>

There are several ways a disputed mark can consequently change appearance during rectification:

* **Vertical stretching can change proportions.** Rudiak himself argues that perspective compression can make one character resemble another and has demonstrated processing that stretches the memo vertically to compensate for the presumed compression.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2018/12/david-rudiaks-latest-on-ramey-memo.html?showComment=1546118226661" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: David Rudiak&#x27;s Latest on the Ramey MemoDecember 19, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 19, 2018</span></span></span> The important methodological point is not whether his proposed correction is right, but that choosing a different correction changes the visual classification problem.
* **Local warping changes stroke angles.** A short oblique feature can become steeper or flatter depending on how its neighbourhood is remapped. At low resolution, that may alter whether the viewer perceives a diagonal, vertical or curved stroke.
* **Interpolation creates new output pixels.** Once pixels are moved to non-integer positions, software normally calculates new pixel values from neighbouring samples. Forensic guidance treats interpolation as an important technical consideration when resizing imagery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.swgde.org/documents/published-complete-listing/22-v-001-swgde-fundamentals-of-resizing-imagery-and-considerations-for-legal-proceedings/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: swgde.org">[SWGDE - SWGDE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">swgde.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">SWGDE Fundamentals of Resizing Imagery and Considerations for Legal Proceedings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SWGDE Fundamentals of Resizing Imagery and Considerations for Legal Proceedings</span></span></span>
* **Sparse features are less stable than clear ones.** A large, well-resolved letter may survive several reasonable transformations with the same recognisable structure. A disputed Ramey character represented by only a small blurred pattern has much less redundancy, so relatively small geometric changes can affect its apparent identity.

This does not mean rectification is improper. On the contrary, document-dewarping research demonstrates that geometric correction can substantially improve readability and optical character recognition when the underlying distortion can be estimated successfully.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/html/Li_Foreground_and_Text-lines_Aware_Document_Image_Rectification_ICCV_2023_paper.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: openaccess.thecvf.com">[CVF Open Access]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">openaccess.thecvf.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Li Foreground and Text lines Aware Document Image Rectification ICCV 2023 paper</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CVF Open AccessICCV 2023 Open Access Repository...</span></span></span> The problem arises when *improved legibility* is treated as equivalent to *new information*.

## A straighter letter is not necessarily a better transcription

The safest distinction is between two questions: **Does this transformation plausibly undo the geometry of the photographed paper?** and **Does the transformed image prove what the original character was?** The first can sometimes be answered positively without the second following from it.

Professional forensic-image guidance provides a useful principle here. The Scientific Working Group on Digital Evidence says processing can legitimately reveal information that is not readily apparent, but warns against introducing artefacts or losing image detail in ways that could produce erroneous interpretations. It also stresses preservation of the original, documentation of processing and identification of the result as a processed image. For restoration specifically, the guidance notes that information which has been completely lost cannot be replaced.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.swgde.org/documents/published-complete-listing/15-m-002-swgde-image-processing-guidelines/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: swgde.org">[SWGDE - SWGDE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">swgde.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">SWGDE Image Processing Guidelines</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SWGDE Image Processing Guidelines</span></span></span>

Applied to the Ramey memo, this suggests that rectification should be treated as an **interpretive viewing aid**, not as a substitute original. A persuasive reading should ideally remain recognisable across a reasonable family of geometrically plausible corrections. If a supposed letter appears convincing only when control points are placed one particular way, or only after a narrowly chosen amount of stretching, that dependence should reduce [confidence]({{ 'confidence/' | relative_url }}) in the transcription.

This is more than a theoretical concern because the memo already has a documented history of incompatible readings. The 2002 Houran-Randle study tested whether contextual suggestion influenced attempts to decipher the photograph. Participants were assigned different descriptions of what the document supposedly concerned, including Roswell, atomic-bomb testing or no supplied context. The study arose specifically from the concern that ambiguous visual material could invite expectation-driven interpretation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span> Published comparisons of proposed Ramey transcriptions likewise show substantial disagreement among investigators over portions of the text.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://projectaquarius.mufon.com/wp-content/uploads/NewsClippings/MUFON-Regional-Newsletters/MUFON-Minnesota-newsletter/MUFON-Minnesota-Journal-No-102.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: projectaquarius.mufon.com">[Project Aquarius]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">projectaquarius.mufon.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Project Aquarius Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project Aquarius Microsoft Word</span></span></span>

Geometric processing adds another potential pathway for such judgement to enter the analysis. The analyst does not have to deliberately manipulate anything. A perfectly sincere attempt to make text lines straight can embody assumptions about where a faint line runs, where a character begins, how tall the characters should be and how strongly a fold compressed them. Once rectified, the visually tidy result can make those assumptions harder to notice.

## How to separate cleanup from evidence

A stronger approach would therefore test the *stability* of proposed letters rather than selecting the most readable rectification. The raw scan or photograph should remain the reference image, while every geometrically corrected version is explicitly treated as a derivative. This follows the general forensic principle that processing should occur on working copies and be documented sufficiently for another trained examiner to understand and reproduce it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.swgde.org/documents/published-complete-listing/15-m-002-swgde-image-processing-guidelines/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: swgde.org">[SWGDE - SWGDE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">swgde.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">SWGDE Image Processing Guidelines</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SWGDE Image Processing Guidelines</span></span></span>

For the Ramey memo, three checks would be particularly informative.

**First, vary the geometry rather than committing immediately to one model.** Analysts could construct several defensible warp solutions using slightly different control-point locations, fold curves and target character proportions. If a proposed character remains recognisably the same across them, rectification has probably not created its decisive features. If it oscillates between plausible letters, the correct result is uncertainty.

**Second, inspect disputed characters alongside the unrectified pixels.** A transformed image may show a beautifully regular text line, but the evidential question is which of its apparent strokes can actually be traced back to recorded tonal structure in the source. This is especially important around the upper and central folds, where even an advocate of extensive rectification reports smearing and uncertain character positions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2018/12/david-rudiaks-latest-on-ramey-memo.html?showComment=1546118226661" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: David Rudiak&#x27;s Latest on the Ramey MemoDecember 19, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 19, 2018</span></span></span>

**Third, separate geometric assumptions from linguistic assumptions.** If equal character spacing, all-capital typography or a particular word length is imposed while constructing the warp, those constraints should not subsequently be counted as independent visual evidence that the resulting transcription has the expected spacing, capitals or word length. The 2002 discussion of the memo itself proposed analysing structural features such as character spacing and positions, but the continuing disagreement over readings shows why assumptions and observations need to be kept distinct.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=e162b50a3885a28c826cb31c3deca69abaaba9ee&amp;repid=rep1&amp;type=pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: citeseerx.ist.psu.edu">[CiteSeerX]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">citeseerx.ist.psu.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on psu.edu.</span></span></span>

The most revealing test is therefore not “Which corrected image looks clearest?” but “Which features survive when reasonable correction choices change?”

## What rectification can and cannot establish

Rectification has a legitimate role in studying the Ramey memo. The sheet is plainly not presented to the camera as a single flat, front-facing document, and correcting perspective and curvature can make comparisons between characters easier. The general success of document-dewarping methods confirms that such transformations can recover a more useful representation of photographed text when the page geometry is sufficiently constrained.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.10543" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">arXiv Document Dewarping with Control Points</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Document Dewarping with Control PointsMarch 20, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 20, 2022</span></span></span>

But the Ramey problem reverses the usual circumstances of document scanning. Modern dewarping systems normally have thousands or millions of useful image samples across a comparatively legible document and can evaluate success against text-line geometry or OCR performance. Here, researchers are trying to identify individual disputed characters in a tiny, blurred portion of a 1947 photograph. The very marks needed to judge whether the geometric correction is right may themselves be uncertain.

That creates an unavoidable distinction between **restoring geometry** and **recovering character identity**. Straightening can rearrange the evidence into a more intelligible form; it cannot guarantee that the original photograph contained enough information to distinguish every competing letter. Forensic guidance is explicit that restoration is limited by noise, by the accuracy of the assumed degradation model and by information that was never successfully recorded or has been lost.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.swgde.org/documents/published-complete-listing/15-m-002-swgde-image-processing-guidelines/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: swgde.org">[SWGDE - SWGDE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">swgde.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">SWGDE Image Processing Guidelines</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SWGDE Image Processing Guidelines</span></span></span>

For disputed Ramey memo letters, the most credible result is therefore one that is robust to plausible warp choices. A character that remains recognisable through modest changes in control points, interpolation and fold correction deserves more weight than one that becomes persuasive only under a particular rectification. Where reasonable geometries produce materially different letter shapes, the rectification has located an uncertainty rather than solved it.
