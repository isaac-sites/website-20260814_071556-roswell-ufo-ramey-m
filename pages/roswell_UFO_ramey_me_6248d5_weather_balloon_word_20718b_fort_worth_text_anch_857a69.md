## Introduction

“FORT WORTH, TEX.” is one of the most useful fragments attributed to the photographed [Ramey memo]({{ 'ramey-memo/' | relative_url }}) precisely because it does **not** require a complete transcription to be informative. In a controlled 2002 reading experiment, participants given different contextual suggestions repeatedly identified “Fort Worth TX” in the same part of the document. That makes the place-name a comparatively strong anchor amid text whose finer details remain disputed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://noufors.com/Documents/Books%2C%20Manuals%20and%20Published%20Papers/Specialty%20UFO%20Publications/Journal%20of%20Scientific%20Exploration/jse_16_1_houran.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: noufors.com">[Noufors]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">noufors.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Noufors&#x27;&#x27;A Message in a Bottle:&quot; Confounds in Deciphering the Ramey Memo from the Roswell UFO CaseMay 23, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 23, 2026</span></span></span>

What the phrase reveals, however, is narrower than some Roswell arguments suggest. Fort Worth was genuinely central to the events of 8 July 1947: the Roswell debris was brought there, Brigadier General Roger [Ramey]({{ 'ramey/' | relative_url }}) was photographed there, and contemporary news reports were datelined there. Consequently, “FORT WORTH, TEX.” helps demonstrate that some real textual signal survives in the photograph and provides a useful test for competing [transcriptions]({{ 'transcriptions/' | relative_url }}). It does **not**, by itself, establish whether the sheet was an internal military message, a press or wire-service text, or something else.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=e162b50a3885a28c826cb31c3deca69abaaba9ee&amp;repid=rep1&amp;type=pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: citeseerx.ist.psu.edu">[psu.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">citeseerx.ist.psu.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Journal of Scientific Exploration, Vol. 16, No. 1, pp. 45–66, 2002October 19, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 19, 2025</span></span></span>

## How consistently readers identify Fort Worth

The strongest evidence for treating “FORT WORTH, TEX.” differently from the memo's more spectacular proposed readings comes from James Houran and Kevin Randle's 2002 study, “A Message in a Bottle: Confounds in Deciphering the Ramey Memo from the Roswell UFO Case”. The researchers were interested specifically in a problem that dogs attempts to read badly resolved text: people can unconsciously turn ambiguous marks into words that fit what they expect the document to say.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://noufors.com/Documents/Books%2C%20Manuals%20and%20Published%20Papers/Specialty%20UFO%20Publications/Journal%20of%20Scientific%20Exploration/jse_16_1_houran.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: noufors.com">[Noufors]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">noufors.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Noufors&#x27;&#x27;A Message in a Bottle:&quot; Confounds in Deciphering the Ramey Memo from the Roswell UFO CaseMay 23, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 23, 2026</span></span></span>

They therefore divided 176 participants among three conditions. One group received a Roswell/UFO-oriented suggestion, another was given an atomic-bomb-related suggestion, and a third operated without that sort of suggested interpretation. The results demonstrated a substantial suggestion effect: readers generated words associated with the contexts they had been given. That finding is an important warning against treating a plausible-looking transcription as automatically authentic.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://noufors.com/Documents/Books%2C%20Manuals%20and%20Published%20Papers/Specialty%20UFO%20Publications/Journal%20of%20Scientific%20Exploration/jse_16_1_houran.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: noufors.com">[Noufors]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">noufors.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Noufors&#x27;&#x27;A Message in a Bottle:&quot; Confounds in Deciphering the Ramey Memo from the Roswell UFO CaseMay 23, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 23, 2026</span></span></span>

Against that noisy background, however, a few readings crossed the experimental boundaries. “Fort Worth TX” was reported by **52 participants** in the same location: 24 in the pro-UFO condition, 17 in the atomic-bomb condition and 11 in the blind condition. “[Story]({{ 'story/' | relative_url }})” and “weather balloons” also produced cross-group agreement, although at different frequencies. Houran and Randle concluded that this convergence with earlier investigators suggested that parts of the photographed document really were legible, even though the meaning surrounding those words remained uncertain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://noufors.com/Documents/Books%2C%20Manuals%20and%20Published%20Papers/Specialty%20UFO%20Publications/Journal%20of%20Scientific%20Exploration/jse_16_1_houran.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: noufors.com">[Noufors]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">noufors.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Noufors&#x27;&#x27;A Message in a Bottle:&quot; Confounds in Deciphering the Ramey Memo from the Roswell UFO CaseMay 23, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 23, 2026</span></span></span>

That distinction is more important than simply counting how many Roswell researchers have endorsed a reading. Researchers can influence one another through published transcriptions, enlarged images and [prior knowledge]({{ 'prior-knowledge/' | relative_url }}) of the case. The experimental readers did not all receive the same interpretive cue, yet “Fort Worth” continued to appear. It is therefore a better candidate for surviving photographic information than a phrase seen principally by readers already expecting a particular Roswell narrative.

The historical transcription record points in the same direction. Published comparisons of proposed readings show investigators disagreeing sharply over much of the surrounding text while repeatedly placing “AT FORT WORTH, TEX.” or a close equivalent on the same line. Kevin Randle has consequently described “Fort Worth, Texas” and “weather balloons” as nearly universal readings, while debates have continued over almost everything needed to turn those fragments into a coherent message.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/10/digital-image-of-ramey-memo.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[kevinrandle.blogspot.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">A Different Perspective: Digital Image of the Ramey Memo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">October 29, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 29, 2015</span></span></span>

That does not make every letter indisputable. “Fort Worth TX”, “FORT WORTH, TEX.” and “AT FORT WORTH, TEX.” are already slightly different claims. The defensible conclusion is therefore not that a particular reconstruction of the entire line has been proved. It is that **Fort Worth is unusually persistent at approximately the same textual location across readers and transcription schemes**.

## Why a geographic anchor helps test transcriptions

A recognisable place-name is unusually valuable when working with severely degraded text because it supplies more structure than an isolated short word. “Fort Worth” contains two words with distinctive lengths and letter patterns, while “TEX.” or “TX” provides an adjoining geographical abbreviation that makes sense in period prose. A proposed transcription of the surrounding line can therefore be checked against a relatively constrained sequence rather than against featureless photographic marks.

The external historical record provides a second check. The photograph itself came from the Fort Worth episode. The 2002 paper identifies the scene as the 8 July 1947 [photo session]({{ 'photo-session/' | relative_url }}) at Fort Worth Army Air Field, where Ramey was photographed beside the debris and happened to hold the sheet whose faint text became the “Ramey memo”. The original photographic material belongs to the Fort Worth *Star-Telegram* collection at the University of Texas at Arlington.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=e162b50a3885a28c826cb31c3deca69abaaba9ee&amp;repid=rep1&amp;type=pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: citeseerx.ist.psu.edu">[CiteSeerX]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">citeseerx.ist.psu.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Journal of Scientific Exploration, Vol. 16, No. 1, pp. 45–66, 2002October 19, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 19, 2025</span></span></span>

Contemporary reporting independently confirms how naturally the location belonged in texts concerning the episode. An Associated Press account reproduced in the *Cincinnati Enquirer* began “Fort Worth, Tex., July 8” before reporting that the supposed flying disc had been identified as a weather balloon. Other contemporary accounts likewise carried Fort Worth datelines while describing Ramey's explanation and the debris brought from Roswell.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufologie.patrickgross.org/press/cincinattienquirer9jul1947.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOS at close sight: the newspapers, Roswell explained, and the Ohio Roswell, USA, July 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 1947</span></span></span>

That creates a useful but easily misunderstood test. If a proposed transcription places recognisable letter forms corresponding to “FORT WORTH, TEX.” where independent readers also see them, it gains a small measure of credibility at that location. If the same reconstruction requires the relatively stable Fort Worth shapes to mean something completely different, that is a warning sign. The anchor can therefore help judge neighbouring readings without requiring confidence in the entire document.

The method works asymmetrically. A transcription that fails at a strong anchor can be weakened quite substantially; a transcription that gets the anchor right is not thereby proved. Anyone who already knows that the photograph concerns the Fort Worth Roswell episode has a strong contextual reason to expect the words “Fort Worth”. The Houran-Randle experiment reduces that concern because the place-name survived across differently primed groups, but it cannot eliminate ambiguity inherent in the source image.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://noufors.com/Documents/Books%2C%20Manuals%20and%20Published%20Papers/Specialty%20UFO%20Publications/Journal%20of%20Scientific%20Exploration/jse_16_1_houran.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: noufors.com">[Noufors]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">noufors.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Noufors&#x27;&#x27;A Message in a Bottle:&quot; Confounds in Deciphering the Ramey Memo from the Roswell UFO CaseMay 23, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 23, 2026</span></span></span>

This is especially important when moving from readable fragments to controversial sentences. Several published reconstructions agree on “FORT WORTH, TEX.” while proposing dramatically different wording immediately before and after it. In the comparison reproduced by Houran and Randle, proposed line-three readings include versions such as “TEAM AT FORT WORTH, TEX.” and simply “AT FORT WORTH, TEX.” The shared place-name therefore cannot be used to choose automatically between competing interpretations of what the preceding noun or verb says.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://noufors.com/Documents/Books%2C%20Manuals%20and%20Published%20Papers/Specialty%20UFO%20Publications/Journal%20of%20Scientific%20Exploration/jse_16_1_houran.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: noufors.com">[Noufors]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">noufors.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Noufors&#x27;&#x27;A Message in a Bottle:&quot; Confounds in Deciphering the Ramey Memo from the Roswell UFO CaseMay 23, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 23, 2026</span></span></span>

In other words, the fragment behaves more like a **calibration point** than a Roswell smoking gun. It tells us that interpreters are not necessarily extracting everything from random grain. But it simultaneously exposes how quickly confidence falls away when they move from the comparatively stable place-name into less resolved characters.

## Fort Worth also fits contemporary news copy

The phrase has another significance because “Fort Worth, Tex.” was not peculiar to secret or internal [military communications]({{ 'military-communication/' | relative_url }}). It was ordinary journalistic geography.

Associated Press reporting on the Roswell story used precisely that sort of Fort Worth dateline. One surviving 9 July newspaper version begins “Fort Worth, Tex., July 8 — (AP)” and proceeds directly into the identification of the supposed disc as a weather balloon. Another AP-derived account begins “FORT WORTH, TEX., July 9” before describing Ramey, the Eighth Air Force headquarters and the debris brought from New Mexico.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufologie.patrickgross.org/press/cincinattienquirer9jul1947.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOS at close sight: the newspapers, Roswell explained, and the Ohio Roswell, USA, July 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 1947</span></span></span>

That matters because one longstanding dispute concerns what kind of document Ramey was holding. Barry Greenwood and other sceptical interpreters have explored whether it could have been related to journalistic or wire-service material rather than a confidential military communication. Greenwood noted similarities between portions of proposed memo readings and contemporary reporting, while other researchers have disputed how far those parallels can be taken.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://id.scribd.com/document/314034746/2004-I-11-An-Observation-on-the-Ramey-Memo-ed-Barry-Greenwood-pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: id.scribd.com">[Scribd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">id.scribd.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Analysis of the Ramey Memo &#124; PDF &#124; Travel &#124; Young Adult</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Analysis of the Ramey Memo &#124; PDF &#124; Travel &#124; Young AdultMarch 1, 2004...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 1, 2004</span></span></span>

Punctuation has even entered that argument. A critical analysis of competing interpretations noted that the comma in “Fort Worth, Tex.” looks more characteristic of journalistic dateline style than of some examples of military teletype formatting. That observation is suggestive rather than decisive: one cannot identify a document's institutional origin securely from a single punctuation pattern, especially when the punctuation itself is being read from a low-resolution photograph.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.astronomyufo.com/UFO/Ramey.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: astronomyufo.com">[astronomyufo.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">astronomyufo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Ramey Document: Smoking gun or empty water pistol?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Ramey Document: Smoking gun or empty water pistol?</span></span></span>

The important point is therefore comparative. **Finding “FORT WORTH, TEX.” is consistent with a document concerning the Roswell/Fort Worth episode, but it is compatible with more than one kind of document that could plausibly have been present in Ramey's office.** The same phrase that helps authenticate the subject matter cannot authenticate a particular theory about the sheet's origin.

## Why Fort Worth does not prove a secret military message

The geographical fit can tempt interpreters into circular reasoning. The photograph was taken in Fort Worth during the Roswell press episode; therefore a document mentioning Fort Worth looks highly relevant. But relevance is not provenance. A military message about the incident might mention Fort Worth. So might an incoming or outgoing press communication, a wire-service report, notes derived from press coverage, or another document connected with the rapidly developing story.

Even the US Air Force's historical investigation could not solve the problem by extracting the text. According to the account of its photographic examination, investigators obtained first-generation prints and negatives from the University of Texas at Arlington and noticed the writing on the paper in Ramey's hand. The image was sent to a national-level organisation for digitisation and photographic interpretation, but the organisation reported in July 1994 that the photographs were insufficient to recover the requested detail.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ramey-memo.com/national-level-organization-failed/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ramey-memo.com">[ramey-memo.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ramey-memo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National-Level Organization FAILED! – Unlocking The Mystery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National-Level Organization FAILED! – Unlocking The Mystery</span></span></span>

That failure is worth setting beside the 2002 experiment. The two findings are not necessarily contradictory. The Air Force-era analysis could not produce a reliable decipherment of the document; Houran and Randle later found statistical convergence on a limited number of words. Together they support a middle position: **the photograph may preserve enough signal for some fragments to be recognised repeatedly without preserving enough information for a definitive line-by-line transcription**.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ramey-memo.com/national-level-organization-failed/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ramey-memo.com">[ramey-memo.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ramey-memo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">National-Level Organization FAILED! – Unlocking The Mystery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National-Level Organization FAILED! – Unlocking The Mystery</span></span></span>

The Fort Worth reading is a particularly good example of that distinction. It gives no independent answer to whether proposed neighbouring terms such as “victims”, “wreck”, “disc” or particular destinations are correct. Those readings must succeed on their own visual and comparative evidence. They cannot inherit the comparatively stronger status of “Fort Worth” merely because they appear beside it in somebody's reconstruction.

## What “FORT WORTH, TEX.” can actually tell us

The safest interpretation is also the most useful. “FORT WORTH, TEX.” supplies three limited but meaningful conclusions about the Ramey memo.

First, it supports the proposition that **some linguistic information survives in the photograph**. Cross-group identification in the Houran-Randle experiment is difficult to reconcile with treating every visible character as wholly meaningless photographic noise. The researchers themselves nevertheless stressed that suggestion strongly affected interpretations and that the context of the agreed words remained ambiguous.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://noufors.com/Documents/Books%2C%20Manuals%20and%20Published%20Papers/Specialty%20UFO%20Publications/Journal%20of%20Scientific%20Exploration/jse_16_1_houran.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: noufors.com">[Noufors]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">noufors.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Noufors&#x27;&#x27;A Message in a Bottle:&quot; Confounds in Deciphering the Ramey Memo from the Roswell UFO CaseMay 23, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 23, 2026</span></span></span>

Second, the place-name gives researchers a **fixed geographical benchmark** against which image processing and proposed transcriptions can be judged. A method that produces a plausible Fort Worth sequence in the established location has at least recovered something consistent with independent readers and the historical setting. That is a useful validation check, though far short of validating everything else the same method generates.

Third, it places the document comfortably within the vocabulary of the Fort Worth phase of the Roswell story without identifying what kind of document it was. On 8–9 July 1947, Fort Worth appeared naturally in press reports because Ramey's Eighth Air Force headquarters was there and the Roswell debris had been brought there for examination and presentation. Contemporary newspaper copy demonstrates that “Fort Worth, Tex.” was already part of the ordinary textual environment surrounding the story.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufologie.patrickgross.org/press/cincinattienquirer9jul1947.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOS at close sight: the newspapers, Roswell explained, and the Ohio Roswell, USA, July 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 1947</span></span></span>

That is why the fragment is more valuable as a control than as a revelation. “FORT WORTH, TEX.” does not decode the Ramey memo, prove an extraterrestrial crash, establish a cover story or identify the document's sender. What it does is establish a comparatively firm patch of ground inside an exceptionally uncertain image. For assessing the readable “weather balloons” wording and the far more disputed text around it, that modest anchor is precisely what makes Fort Worth significant.
