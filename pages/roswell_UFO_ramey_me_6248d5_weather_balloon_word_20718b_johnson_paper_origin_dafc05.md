## Introduction

The [provenance]({{ 'provenance/' | relative_url }}) of the sheet in Brigadier General Roger Ramey’s hand is unusually important because the photographer, J. [Bond Johnson]({{ 'johnson/' | relative_url }}), gave conflicting later accounts of how it got there. In one version, Johnson said he carried a wire-service “flash” from the *Fort Worth Star-Telegram* newsroom to Ramey’s office and handed it to the general. In another, he said the paper was already on Ramey’s desk and that he handed it to Ramey as part of posing the photograph.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2009/10/ufos-ramey-memo-j-bond-johnson-and-me.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: UFOs, the Ramey Memo, J. Bond Johnson and MeOctober 9, 2009...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 9, 2009</span></span></span>

Those are not minor differences in remembered choreography. If the photographed sheet was newsroom wire copy, readable expressions such as “WEATHER BALLOONS” could simply belong to contemporary press traffic about the rapidly developing Roswell [story]({{ 'story/' | relative_url }}). If it was a document already in [Ramey]({{ 'ramey/' | relative_url }})’s office, the possibility of military provenance becomes stronger, although not proven. Johnson’s changing recollections therefore prevent his testimony from securely settling what kind of document researchers are trying to decipher.

## Johnson's wire-material recollection

Johnson was a reporter and back-up photographer for the *Fort Worth Star-Telegram* when he was sent to Fort Worth Army Air Field on 8 July 1947. In a later recorded recollection reproduced by Roswell researcher Kevin Randle, Johnson said his involvement began because the Associated Press had picked up word that material was being flown from Roswell to Fort Worth. His city editor asked whether he had his camera and sent him to Ramey’s office. Johnson described the newsroom receiving a teletype or “flash” alert about the developing story.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2009/10/ufos-ramey-memo-j-bond-johnson-and-me.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: UFOs, the Ramey Memo, J. Bond Johnson and MeOctober 9, 2009...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 9, 2009</span></span></span>

At one stage Johnson went considerably further than merely remembering that such an alert existed. In an account subsequently reproduced by Randle and elsewhere, he said his editor gave him the wire-service “flash” and that he took it with him to the airfield. When Ramey entered the office, according to this version, Johnson handed him the printed announcement; Ramey read it, and Johnson then photographed him while the sheet remained in his hand.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2009/10/ufos-ramey-memo-j-bond-johnson-and-me.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: UFOs, the Ramey Memo, J. Bond Johnson and MeOctober 9, 2009...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 9, 2009</span></span></span>

If accurate, that recollection would provide an uncomplicated explanation for the famous paper: it was not a secret message accidentally exposed to Johnson’s camera but press material that Johnson himself had supplied. It would also explain naturally why the document might contain words connected with Roswell, Fort Worth, a “disc” and balloons. News services were already reporting the Roswell announcement and the movement of the recovered material towards [higher headquarters]({{ 'higher-hq/' | relative_url }}) in Fort Worth.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2009/10/ufos-ramey-memo-j-bond-johnson-and-me.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: UFOs, the Ramey Memo, J. Bond Johnson and MeOctober 9, 2009...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 9, 2009</span></span></span>

The difficulty is that Johnson did not consistently stand by this account. Later discussions of the dispute report that he withdrew or repudiated the claim that the photographed paper was the wire bulletin he had brought from the newsroom. Randle, despite favouring the wire-copy possibility, acknowledged in 2015 that Johnson had made that claim in earlier interviews but rejected it in later ones. David [Rudiak]({{ 'rudiak/' | relative_url }}), who argues for a military interpretation of the document, likewise cited a later Johnson interview in which the earlier AP-bulletin identification was characterised as speculation rather than firm memory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/12/jan-aldrich-barry-greenwood-and-ramey.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: Jan Aldrich, Barry Greenwood and the Ramey MemoDecember 4, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 4, 2015</span></span></span>

That distinction matters. There is good reason to accept the basic newsroom setting: Johnson was dispatched because information about the Roswell story was circulating through the press system. What is much less secure is the additional proposition that the particular wire sheet associated with that alert was the particular sheet photographed in Ramey’s hand.

## The account placing the paper on Ramey's desk

A substantially different version subsequently attributed to Johnson has the photographed paper originating inside Ramey’s office. Randle summarised Johnson’s later account as saying that he picked up a document from Ramey’s desk and gave it to the general so that Ramey would have something in his hand for the photograph. A recent specialist reconstruction of the photographic episode describes the same contradiction: first the paper was supposedly Johnson’s newsroom telegram; later it became a document already lying on Ramey’s desk.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2009/10/ufos-ramey-memo-j-bond-johnson-and-me.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: UFOs, the Ramey Memo, J. Bond Johnson and MeOctober 9, 2009...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 9, 2009</span></span></span>

This version changes the evidential problem but does not solve it. A paper found on the commanding general’s desk is more plausibly associated with his work than a sheet carried in by a newspaper photographer. Yet “on Ramey’s desk” does not logically mean “classified military message”, much less establish who wrote it, to whom it was addressed or whether its contents represented an internal account of Roswell. An office could contain press releases, incoming news copy, notes, correspondence and other unclassified material alongside official communications.

There is also disagreement among researchers over how firmly the desk version itself can be documented. Randle presented it as something Johnson later told him, whereas Rudiak wrote in a 2015 discussion that he was unaware of an interview in which Johnson specifically said he had found the message on Ramey’s desk. That leaves an important source-critical problem: much of the provenance debate comes through later interviews, transcripts, quotations and competing researchers’ descriptions of Johnson’s changing testimony rather than a single contemporaneous statement from July 1947 identifying the paper.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2009/10/ufos-ramey-memo-j-bond-johnson-and-me.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: UFOs, the Ramey Memo, J. Bond Johnson and MeOctober 9, 2009...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 9, 2009</span></span></span>

The photograph itself supplies no decisive answer. It securely establishes that Ramey was holding a typed sheet when Johnson photographed him; it does not record how the sheet entered his hand. Other possibilities have consequently been proposed, including the simple scenario that Ramey already had the document when he entered or prepared for the photographic session. That possibility demonstrates why Johnson’s recollections cannot be converted into a binary choice in which disproving the wire-sheet story automatically proves the desk story.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2009/10/ufos-ramey-memo-j-bond-johnson-and-me.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: UFOs, the Ramey Memo, J. ## Why the contradiction is hard to resolve

The central problem is the distance between the event and the detailed provenance claims. Johnson unquestionably took photographs in Ramey’s office, and archival photo credits independently establish his role. But the elaborate arguments about who supplied the sheet developed decades after July 1947, when investigators began recognising that enlargements might contain partly recoverable text.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2009/10/ufos-ramey-memo-j-bond-johnson-and-me.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: UFOs, the Ramey Memo, J. Bond Johnson and MeOctober 9, 2009...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 9, 2009</span></span></span>

That creates a familiar historical-source problem. Johnson was an eyewitness to the photographic session, giving his recollections genuine value, but eyewitness status does not make every later detail equally reliable. His changing accounts are particularly consequential because they concern precisely the detail that later acquired enormous significance: whether the blurred text was civilian press material or an internal document.

Randle’s treatment illustrates the problem well because he both preserves useful Johnson testimony and strongly criticises its instability. He says his early conversations with Johnson were recorded and quotes Johnson at length about receiving the news alert, travelling to the base and photographing Ramey. Yet Randle also concluded that Johnson repeatedly changed parts of his story. Whatever one thinks of Randle’s judgement about Johnson’s motives — which is an interpretation rather than an independently established fact — the contradiction about the paper itself is explicit in Randle’s account.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2009/10/ufos-ramey-memo-j-bond-johnson-and-me.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: UFOs, the Ramey Memo, J. Bond Johnson and MeOctober 9, 2009...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 9, 2009</span></span></span>

The safest historical conclusion is therefore narrower than either side of the Roswell argument sometimes prefers. Johnson provides evidence that newsroom wire traffic helped send him to Ramey’s office. He also at one point specifically identified the sheet in the photographs as a wire-service flash he had brought with him. But because he later retreated from that identification and a competing desk-origin account emerged, his recollection cannot independently establish the photographed document’s provenance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/12/jan-aldrich-barry-greenwood-and-ramey.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: Jan Aldrich, Barry Greenwood and the Ramey MemoDecember 4, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 4, 2015</span></span></span>

## Why document origin changes how balloon wording is read

The dispute becomes especially relevant when researchers turn from the document’s origin to its relatively recognisable wording. “WEATHER BALLOONS” is among the fragments on which different attempts at reading the Ramey paper have shown comparatively strong agreement. But identifying words and interpreting why they appear are separate tasks.

Under the wire-copy hypothesis, balloon wording has an obvious potential context: Ramey’s public explanation was becoming a major news story. A civilian bulletin could mention Fort Worth, Roswell, the reported “disc” and balloon explanations without revealing anything about what military officers privately believed. Barry [Greenwood]({{ 'greenwood/' | relative_url }}) and others have consequently explored whether the document could be news copy rather than a military message. The hypothesis remains disputed, including over whether proposed readings and formatting actually resemble contemporary wire-service material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362854511_A_Grounded_Theory_Update_on_the_Roswell_UFO_Incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate(PDF) A Grounded Theory Update on the Roswell UFO Incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ResearchGate(PDF) A Grounded Theory Update on the Roswell UFO IncidentAugust 22, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 22, 2022</span></span></span>

Under the office-document hypothesis, the same words potentially carry greater historical weight. If the paper originated within Ramey’s command and discussed the Roswell material internally, “WEATHER BALLOONS” could form part of military discussion of the explanation being given to the press. Researchers favouring a military provenance have accordingly tried to reconstruct surrounding passages as communications between officers. But that interpretation depends on many less-secure readings surrounding the relatively recognisable words, as well as assumptions about the document’s format, sender and recipient. The existence of balloon wording by itself does not establish any of those things.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/362854511_A_Grounded_Theory_Update_on_the_Roswell_UFO_Incident" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Research Gate(PDF) A Grounded Theory Update on the Roswell UFO Incident</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ResearchGate(PDF) A Grounded Theory Update on the Roswell UFO IncidentAugust 22, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 22, 2022</span></span></span>

Johnson’s contradiction therefore imposes a useful limit on what can responsibly be claimed from the readable fragments. The words may provide evidence about the sheet’s subject matter, but his testimony does not give researchers a dependable external key telling them what *kind* of sheet they are reading. Treating the early wire-service account as certain risks reducing an internal document to press copy; treating the later account as proof of military provenance risks turning an uncertain recollection about a desk into evidence of a classified communication.

## Where the evidence leaves the paper

Three propositions have quite different levels of support. Johnson was the photographer responsible for the relevant Ramey photographs; contemporary photographic records and the surviving images make that secure. He was sent to Fort Worth Army Air Field after news of the Roswell material reached the *Star-Telegram* through press channels; his recorded recollections consistently provide that general setting. But his identification of the paper itself is substantially less secure because his later accounts conflict.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2009/10/ufos-ramey-memo-j-bond-johnson-and-me.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: UFOs, the Ramey Memo, J. Bond Johnson and MeOctober 9, 2009...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 9, 2009</span></span></span>

The contradiction is therefore not an incidental curiosity in the Ramey memo story. It blocks one of the seemingly easiest ways of interpreting otherwise tantalising readable wording. Had Johnson consistently remembered carrying the photographed sheet from his newsroom, “WEATHER BALLOONS” could be assessed primarily against contemporary press traffic. Had he consistently remembered taking the sheet from Ramey’s desk, researchers would have stronger grounds for testing it against military correspondence and [message formats]({{ 'message-format/' | relative_url }}). Instead, both possibilities remain hypotheses requiring evidence independent of Johnson’s memory.

That is the most defensible significance of his conflicting accounts. They do not prove that the Ramey paper was civilian wire copy, and they do not prove that it was an internal military message. They show why even comparatively readable wording such as “WEATHER BALLOONS” cannot safely be interpreted without first confronting an unresolved provenance problem: more than half a century after taking the photograph, the man behind the camera supplied different answers to the basic question of where the paper in Ramey’s hand came from.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2009/10/ufos-ramey-memo-j-bond-johnson-and-me.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: UFOs, the Ramey Memo, J.
