## Introduction

The two surviving photographs of Major [Jesse Marcel]({{ 'marcel/' | relative_url }}) kneeling with debris in Brigadier General Roger Ramey’s Fort Worth office created an unusually specific problem for the Roswell photographic record: did *Fort Worth Star-Telegram* reporter-photographer J. [Bond Johnson]({{ 'johnson/' | relative_url }}) take them? Johnson unquestionably photographed Ramey and Colonel Thomas DuBose on 8 July 1947, but in his earliest recorded discussions with researcher Kevin Randle he did not recognise the Marcel photographs as his own. Later arguments proposed another photographer and even a separate photographic session. Yet archival placement, the appearance and continuity of the photographs, and a reconstruction of the sequence all increasingly pointed back towards Johnson.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2009/10/ufos-ramey-memo-j-bond-johnson-and-me.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: UFOs, the Ramey Memo, J. Bond Johnson and MeOctober 9, 2009...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 9, 2009</span></span></span>

The most defensible conclusion is therefore not that Johnson’s authorship is proven beyond doubt, but that it is considerably more plausible than his uncertain recollection initially suggested. That matters to the [Ramey-memo]({{ 'ramey-memo/' | relative_url }}) debate because it offers a compact demonstration of a larger evidential problem: a participant’s memory can diverge sharply from a photographic record whose physical and archival relationships survive independently of that memory.

## Why the Marcel frames became disputed

Johnson’s initial recollection was surprisingly narrow. In a March 1989 interview reproduced by Randle, he remembered taking only two essentially duplicate photographs, using one double-sided film holder, and specifically identified his subject as [Ramey]({{ 'ramey/' | relative_url }}). When Randle told him there was also a photograph of Marcel holding the wreckage, Johnson responded by asking whether Randle was sure the man was not Ramey. Randle later established that Johnson had actually carried more film and that the Fort Worth session involved more exposures than Johnson initially remembered.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/11/j-bond-johnsons-statement-on-his-visit.html?showComment=1447183915509" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: J. That discrepancy became more significant once the known Fort Worth photographs were considered as a set. Randle described six closely related images: two of Ramey, two of Ramey with DuBose and two of Marcel. A separate photograph of weather officer Irving [Newton]({{ 'newton/' | relative_url }}) looked sufficiently different to be attributed to another photographer. Johnson, however, had at various times maintained that he was the only photographer present during his visit. The Marcel pair therefore presented an awkward choice: either Johnson had forgotten taking a substantial part of his own assignment, or an unidentified photographer had produced photographs that subsequently became entangled with Johnson’s work.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2010/07/roswell-ron-regehr-and-me.html?showComment=1280431997611" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: Roswell, Ron Regehr, and Me</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: Roswell, Ron Regehr, and Me</span></span></span>

The archival evidence made the second possibility harder to accept casually. The University of Texas at Arlington (UTA), which preserves the *Star-Telegram* photographic collection, catalogues a Marcel [negative]({{ 'negative/' | relative_url }}) from 8 July 1947 in the newspaper collection as AR406-6 7/8/1947 2026 #3. It describes Marcel in Ramey’s office holding foil debris. Another Marcel image carries the closely associated collection designation #4, while the well-known Ramey photograph with the document carries #2 in reproduced metadata. The numbering does not by itself identify the person who pressed the shutter, but it establishes that these images belong to a tightly associated archival group rather than appearing as unrelated photographs with an independently documented [provenance]({{ 'provenance/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mavmatrix.uta.edu/specialcollections_startelegram1940s/35500/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mavmatrix.uta.edu">[uta.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mavmatrix.uta.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mav Matrix</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Major Jesse A. Marcel of Houma, Louisiana...</span></span></span>

Randle added a still more specific archival observation: one Marcel negative had been stored in the same envelope as a Ramey negative. He recalled that this discovery originally led both Johnson and researchers around him towards the straightforward explanation that Johnson had taken the Marcel pictures but simply did not remember doing so.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2010/07/roswell-ron-regehr-and-me.html?showComment=1280431997611" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: Roswell, Ron Regehr, and Me</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: Roswell, Ron Regehr, and Me</span></span></span>

That explanation requires no extraordinary failure of memory. Johnson was 21 in 1947, while his detailed interviews about the assignment began more than four decades later. His earliest recollection was demonstrably wrong about something as basic as the amount of film he had carried and the number of exposures he had made. The *Star-Telegram* itself now describes Johnson as having shot six frames of Ramey and other officers during the assignment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/11/j-bond-johnsons-statement-on-his-visit.html?showComment=1447183915509" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: J. ## The alternative-photographer theory

The case did not end there because members of the Roswell Photo Interpretation Team (RPIT), with which Johnson later worked, developed a technical argument for separating the Marcel photographs from his session.

One version concerned apparent differences in focal length. Randle reported that Ron Regehr told him an analysis of the photographs indicated that the Marcel picture had been made with a different lens from the Ramey and Ramey-DuBose photographs. The difference was said to be small enough that Johnson would have had little reason to change lenses merely to photograph Marcel. On that reasoning, the Marcel frames implied another camera and therefore another photographer. Randle reported that Johnson accepted this interpretation after seeing the calculations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2010/07/roswell-ron-regehr-and-me.html?showComment=1280431997611" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: Roswell, Ron Regehr, and Me</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: Roswell, Ron Regehr, and Me</span></span></span>

But the technical premise was challenged. Researcher David Rudiak argued that the analysis inferred focal-length differences from the changing angles of stripes in the office carpet without adequately accounting for perspective. The Ramey-DuBose pictures had been taken from a higher position looking down, whereas the photographer was much lower for the kneeling Marcel. A change in camera height and viewpoint can alter the apparent spread of parallel lines without requiring a different lens. If that objection is correct, one of the main technical reasons for inventing a second photographer disappears.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2010/07/roswell-ron-regehr-and-me.html?showComment=1280431997611" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: Roswell, Ron Regehr, and Me</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: Roswell, Ron Regehr, and Me</span></span></span>

A second RPIT argument attempted to date the photographs from sunlight and shadows visible through Ramey’s curtains. The team placed the Marcel pictures at roughly 3.15 pm on 8 July. Because Johnson said his editor sent him only after news of the Roswell material reached the newsroom, that early time would place the Marcel session before Johnson could plausibly have arrived. The implication was an unidentified photographer working in Ramey’s office before him.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/11/the-timing-of-roswell-photographs.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on blogspot.com.</span></span></span>

This reconstruction initially appears attractive because it offers a concrete explanation for Johnson’s lack of memory: he could not remember taking the Marcel photographs because he had not taken them. But it creates problems of its own. The method depends on accurately reconstructing the office orientation, exterior reference points, solar geometry and photographic viewpoint from limited visual information. Randle ultimately regarded the 3.15 estimate as assumption-heavy rather than decisive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/11/the-timing-of-roswell-photographs.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on blogspot.com.</span></span></span>

There was also no securely identified alternative photographer. The RPIT theory required somebody else to have reached Ramey’s office, photographed Marcel and produced pictures that entered the *Star-Telegram* archive alongside Johnson’s work. Johnson at one point invoked a story that Ramey had alerted the newspaper and that another *Star-Telegram* photographer had gone out earlier, but Randle noted that this person was never identified. No photographer subsequently emerged to claim the two famous Marcel frames.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/11/the-timing-of-roswell-photographs.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on blogspot.com.</span></span></span>

An older specialist account illustrates how far the alternative chronology could be pushed. In a 2007 UFO Updates exchange, Neil Morris argued that Johnson had taken four photographs around 3.15 pm and that the Marcel and Newton photographs belonged to a later session involving other photographers. Yet that chronology sits uneasily with the competing RPIT account, reported by Randle, in which the 3.15 timing itself was used for the Marcel photographs. The disagreement within the reconstruction is important: the supposedly precise timing was not a stable, independently established fact.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gbppr.net/ufoupdates/pdf/2007-05.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gbppr.net">[GBPPR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gbppr.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO UpDates: May 2007October 19, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 19, 2025</span></span></span>

## Randle's reconstruction of the photographic sequence

By 2015, Randle had moved towards a simpler interpretation: Johnson probably took the six closely related photographs, while somebody else later photographed Newton.

Several details supported that reconstruction. First was the archival association of the Marcel and Ramey negatives. Second was the absence of an identified second newspaper photographer. Third was Johnson’s own insistence that he had been the *Star-Telegram* photographer dispatched to the office. Fourth was what Randle regarded as visual continuity between the first six photographs. He noted, for example, that Ramey’s overseas cap and tie remained on the radiator through those pictures but were absent from the Newton image, supporting the idea that Newton’s photograph belonged to a later, distinct session.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2010/07/roswell-ron-regehr-and-me.html?showComment=1280431997611" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: Roswell, Ron Regehr, and Me</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: Roswell, Ron Regehr, and Me</span></span></span>

Rudiak proposed an even more specific sequence from visual comparison: two photographs of Ramey alone, followed by two of Ramey and DuBose, then the two Marcel photographs, with the Newton photograph following separately. That ordering is a reconstruction rather than contemporary documentation, but it explains the surviving photographs without requiring an otherwise unattested photographer to enter between the Ramey and Marcel frames.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2010/07/roswell-ron-regehr-and-me.html?showComment=1280431997611" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: Roswell, Ron Regehr, and Me</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: Roswell, Ron Regehr, and Me</span></span></span>

Changes visible in the office do not necessarily defeat that interpretation. Chairs occupy different positions and the debris shifts between photographs, but Johnson himself remembered posing Ramey and arranging material for the camera. A photographic session in which subjects, chairs and debris were deliberately repositioned would naturally produce such differences. Randle also pointed to smaller continuity clues in the room when arguing that the six frames belonged together.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/11/the-timing-of-roswell-photographs.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on blogspot.com.</span></span></span>

The newsroom chronology supplied another circumstantial argument. Johnson remembered returning to the *Star-Telegram* to find people waiting urgently for his film; he was told to process it and produce a wet print for rapid transmission. Randle reasoned that if another newspaper photographer had already photographed Marcel substantially earlier, that photographer should have enjoyed a major head start in getting an image onto the wires. The extraordinary urgency surrounding Johnson’s return becomes harder to explain if equivalent *Star-Telegram* pictures had already been available for hours.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/11/the-timing-of-roswell-photographs.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on blogspot.com.</span></span></span>

This is not proof. News photographs could be acquired, filed or transmitted in ways that no surviving paperwork now records. Nor does a *Star-Telegram* collection number automatically constitute a photographer credit. But the alternative theory accumulates assumptions: another photographer must have been present; that person must have produced the Marcel pictures; the photographs must then have entered the same newspaper archive group; and the photographer’s identity must subsequently have disappeared despite the later fame of the images.

The Johnson hypothesis needs fewer moving parts. It requires mainly that Johnson forgot two exposures from a routine newspaper assignment more than forty years before researchers began asking him about them. Given that he initially misremembered his film supply and total number of exposures as well, that is a comparatively modest explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/11/j-bond-johnsons-statement-on-his-visit.html?showComment=1447183915509" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: J. ## What the archive establishes — and what it does not

UTA's catalogue provides a valuable fixed point because it separates surviving photographic evidence from later witness narratives. Its Marcel record dates the negative to 8 July 1947, places Marcel in Ramey’s office and assigns it to the *Fort Worth Star-Telegram* Collection. UTA's catalogue record for the Marcel photograph<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mavmatrix.uta.edu/specialcollections_startelegram1940s/35500/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mavmatrix.uta.edu">[mavmatrix.uta.edu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mavmatrix.uta.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mav Matrix</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Major Jesse A. The modern *Star-Telegram* account goes further and treats Johnson as the photographer responsible for six frames of Ramey and other officers. Its illustration of Marcel is credited through the J. Bond Johnson/*Fort Worth Star-Telegram* collection at UTA. That is meaningful provenance evidence, especially because it comes from the newspaper whose archive contains the negatives. It nevertheless remains a modern attribution, not a surviving 1947 photographer’s log spelling out each exposure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.star-telegram.com/opinion/bud-kennedy/article289713499.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: star-telegram.com">[Fort Worth Star-Telegram]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">star-telegram.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fort Worth Star-Telegram‘Roswell Incident’ UFO crash in 1947 stirred Fort Worth &#124; Fort Worth Star-Telegram...</span></span></span>

The official Air Force investigation of Roswell also treated the Ramey and Marcel photographs as a *Star-Telegram* photographic group when investigators obtained first-generation prints and negatives from UTA. Its concern was primarily what the photographs showed rather than which individual made every exposure, so it does not independently settle Johnson’s authorship. It does, however, reinforce the archival unity of the Ramey-Marcel material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.scribd.com/document/529806374/The-Roswell-Report-book" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scribd.com">[Scribd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scribd.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Roswell Report book</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Roswell Report book</span></span></span>

This distinction is important. The surviving evidence supports three propositions with different strengths:

* **Certain or nearly so:** Johnson photographed in Ramey’s office on 8 July, and the *Star-Telegram* archive preserves closely associated Ramey and Marcel photographs from that event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mavmatrix.uta.edu/specialcollections_startelegram1940s/35500/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mavmatrix.uta.edu">[MavMatrix]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mavmatrix.uta.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mav Matrix</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Major Jesse A. Marcel of Houma, Louisiana...</span></span></span>
* **Strongly plausible:** Johnson took the two Marcel photographs as part of the same six-frame sequence, despite failing to remember them accurately decades later.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/11/the-timing-of-roswell-photographs.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on blogspot.com.</span></span></span>
* **Not demonstrated:** that the Marcel pair can be assigned to another photographer on the basis of the proposed focal-length or shadow analyses. Those arguments remain disputed, and no alternative photographer has been securely identified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2010/07/roswell-ron-regehr-and-me.html?showComment=1280431997611" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: Roswell, Ron Regehr, and Me</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: Roswell, Ron Regehr, and Me</span></span></span>

That is a more cautious conclusion than simply treating either Johnson’s memory or a modern archive caption as infallible.

## What authorship uncertainty reveals about Johnson's memory


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

The Marcel photographs are especially useful because the dispute does not depend on deciphering microscopic lettering in the Ramey memo. The issue is much simpler: Johnson either took two readily recognisable photographs or he did not. Yet his recollection still proved unstable.

</div>

His early interviews show genuine uncertainty rather than a neat retrospective story. He remembered Ramey but apparently not Marcel; he thought he had brought one film holder and made two exposures; later investigation indicated a larger photographic set. Randle's published transcript has Johnson reacting to the Marcel photograph as something he did not know about at the time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2009/10/ufos-ramey-memo-j-bond-johnson-and-me.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: UFOs, the Ramey Memo, J. Bond Johnson and MeOctober 9, 2009...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 9, 2009</span></span></span>

That makes the Marcel frames a useful control case when assessing Johnson's later recollections concerning the Ramey memo. A witness can be completely genuine about having attended an event while being wrong about specific details of what he photographed, carried or encountered. The surviving photograph establishes Johnson's presence far more securely than his memory establishes the precise sequence of events around each exposure.

There is an important counterweight. Johnson's failure to remember the Marcel pictures cannot itself prove he took them. Nor should disagreements among Roswell researchers be converted into certainty merely because one reconstruction appears simpler. The most responsible formulation remains probabilistic: the archival grouping, lack of a credible alternative photographer, problems with the different-lens argument and the internal continuity of the images collectively make Johnson's authorship increasingly plausible, but surviving documentation does not provide an exposure-by-exposure contemporary credit sheet.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2010/07/roswell-ron-regehr-and-me.html?showComment=1280431997611" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: Roswell, Ron Regehr, and Me</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: Roswell, Ron Regehr, and Me</span></span></span>

The historical lesson is therefore narrower but stronger than either side of the Roswell controversy sometimes makes it. The disputed Marcel frames do not establish what the Roswell debris was, and resolving their photographer would not decipher the Ramey memo. What they demonstrate is that Johnson's retrospective recollection cannot safely be used as the sole authority for reconstructing the Fort Worth photographic session. Where his memory and the surviving photographic or archival record pull in different directions, the contemporary material deserves priority.
