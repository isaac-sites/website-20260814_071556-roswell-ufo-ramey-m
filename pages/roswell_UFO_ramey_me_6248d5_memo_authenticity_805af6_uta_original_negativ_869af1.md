## Introduction

For the [Ramey memo]({{ 'ramey-memo/' | relative_url }}), the most important [provenance]({{ 'provenance/' | relative_url }}) fact is unusually concrete: the University of Texas at Arlington (UTA) preserves the *Fort Worth Star-Telegram* photographic archive in which the 8 July 1947 Roswell-assignment negatives reside. UTA’s catalogue identifies the Ramey image under collection reference **AR406-6 07/08/1947 2026**, dates it to 8 July 1947, and records its original format as a black-and-white negative. The surrounding archive is not a Roswell collection assembled by UFO researchers; it is a vast newspaper photographic collection containing an estimated six million film and glass-plate negatives.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mavmatrix.uta.edu/specialcollections_startelegram1940s/35502/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mavmatrix.uta.edu">[MavMatrix]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mavmatrix.uta.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mav Matrix General Ramey holding enlargement of telegram</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">General Ramey holding enlargement of telegramJuly 8, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

That distinction gives the [Ramey]({{ 'ramey/' | relative_url }}) photograph a stronger chain of custody than the innumerable scans, enlargements and processed copies subsequently circulated online. The surviving [negative]({{ 'negative/' | relative_url }}) belongs to the newspaper archive that created and retained the photographic material, which was later transferred institutionally to UTA. The archival trail does not prove any proposed reading of the paper in Ramey’s hand. It does, however, substantially narrow a different question: whether researchers are analysing a modern reconstruction or an image derived from the historical *Star-Telegram* photographic record.

## From newspaper archive to university custody

The chain begins with the *Fort Worth Star-Telegram* photographic operation. UTA describes its collection as news photography created by *Star-Telegram* photographers and says the holdings encompass roughly 179,500 prints dating from about 1849 to 1986 and an estimated six million film and glass-plate negatives dating from 1915 to 2003. The scale matters. The Roswell material sits within the normal record-keeping system of a working metropolitan newspaper rather than in a later collection assembled specifically to substantiate the Roswell story.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mavmatrix.uta.edu/specialcollections_startelegram/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mavmatrix.uta.edu">[MavMatrix]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mavmatrix.uta.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on uta.edu.</span></span></span>

UTA's finding aid provides the crucial institutional-transfer history. It says Special Collections director Charles C. Colley learnt in 1983 that the newspaper was looking for a permanent custodian for its archives. Colley and Fort Worth lawyer Jenkins Garrett were involved in bringing the collection to UTA, with support from university president Wendell Nedderman and libraries director John A. Hudson. The newspaper's corporate headquarters and its administrative, editorial and library management approved the donation in 1983. The archive then arrived at UTA in increments from late June through October 1984, with later donations of negatives received in 1989, 1996 and 1999. The finding aid consequently describes acquisition as a gift beginning in 1984 and continuing thereafter.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://txarchives.org/utarl/finding_aids/00147.xml" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: txarchives.org">[Texas Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">txarchives.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Texas ArchivesFort Worth Star-Telegram Collection, Series 7: Printed Material: A Guide - TARO...</span></span></span>

This is a stronger provenance trail than simply saying that UTA now possesses an old photograph. There are identifiable custodial stages: the photograph originated in the newspaper's photographic operation; the relevant negatives remained part of the newspaper's archival holdings; the newspaper arranged institutional preservation of those archives; and UTA Special Collections became their permanent archival custodian. The documentation does not establish every occasion on which an individual negative may have been printed, inspected or handled over the decades, but it does establish the institutional route by which the collection reached UTA.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://txarchives.org/utarl/finding_aids/00147.xml" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: txarchives.org">[Texas Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">txarchives.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Texas ArchivesFort Worth Star-Telegram Collection, Series 7: Printed Material: A Guide - TARO...</span></span></span>

The Roswell negatives are catalogued within that broader system. UTA's current MavMatrix record for the Ramey photograph gives the archival date as **1947-07-08**, the collection as the *Fort Worth Star-Telegram Collection*, the collection number as **AR406-6 07/08/1947 2026**, and the original format as **Negatives, Black & White**. The digital JPG displayed online is therefore a representation of an archival item whose catalogue record identifies a photographic negative as its source format; the JPG itself is not the archival original.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mavmatrix.uta.edu/specialcollections_startelegram1940s/35502/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mavmatrix.uta.edu">[MavMatrix]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mavmatrix.uta.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mav Matrix General Ramey holding enlargement of telegram</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">General Ramey holding enlargement of telegramJuly 8, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

That distinction is especially important in discussions of the Ramey memo because UTA continues to describe the Roswell photographs and negatives as physical Special Collections holdings. In a 2025 university publication, UTA said the photographs and negatives associated with the 1947 Roswell incident were among the most frequently requested material in Special Collections.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.uta.edu/news/publications/uta-magazine/summer2025/130-things" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uta.edu">[UTA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uta.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">130 Things You Might Not Know About UTA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">130 Things You Might Not Know About UTA - News Center - The University of Texas at Arlington...</span></span></span>

## The Roswell frames belong to a larger photographic sequence

The catalogue structure supplies another useful provenance check: the memo photograph is not an archival orphan. Other photographs from the same Fort Worth episode appear under the same date and closely related collection numbering.

For example, UTA catalogues an image of Major Jesse Marcel in Ramey's office holding debris as **AR406-6 7/8/1947 2026 #3**. Its archival date is likewise 8 July 1947 and its original format is again identified as a black-and-white negative.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mavmatrix.uta.edu/specialcollections_startelegram1940s/35500/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mavmatrix.uta.edu">[MavMatrix]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mavmatrix.uta.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mav Matrix&quot;Major Jesse A. Marcel of Houma, Louisiana</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MavMatrix&quot;Major Jesse A. Marcel of Houma, Louisiana&quot;July 8, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span> The Ramey memo image is catalogued as **AR406-6 07/08/1947 2026**.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mavmatrix.uta.edu/specialcollections_startelegram1940s/35502/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mavmatrix.uta.edu">[MavMatrix]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mavmatrix.uta.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mav Matrix General Ramey holding enlargement of telegram</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">General Ramey holding enlargement of telegramJuly 8, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span> Taken together, these records place the disputed memo frame within the same dated newspaper photographic assignment rather than treating it as a stand-alone image of uncertain origin.

The archive also contains related Fort Worth Army Air Field photography immediately afterwards. UTA catalogues photographs taken on 11 July 1947 showing airmen demonstrating a radar device attached to a weather balloon under the succeeding reference **AR406-6 07/11/1947 2027-1**.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mavmatrix.uta.edu/specialcollections_startelegram1940s/35509/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mavmatrix.uta.edu">[MavMatrix]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mavmatrix.uta.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mav Matrix&quot;A radar device being attached to a weather balloon</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MavMatrix&quot;A radar device being attached to a weather balloon&quot;July 11, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 11, 1947</span></span></span> This does not tell us what Ramey's paper said, but it shows how the Roswell-era material sits within a chronological newspaper filing structure.

The numbering is therefore useful provenance evidence in a modest but important sense. It connects the famous image to neighbouring *Star-Telegram* negatives from the same assignment and period. It is much harder to posit that the Ramey frame entered the historical record independently at a late date when UTA preserves it as part of the newspaper's dated negative files alongside the other Fort Worth photographs.

UTA's own institutional description reinforces that status. In 2017, *UTA Magazine* described the Ramey memo image as part of the *Fort Worth Star-Telegram Collection* and said the material was housed in UTA Libraries' Special Collections.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.uta.edu/utamagazine/spring-2017/stories/collected.php" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: uta.edu">[UTA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">uta.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Ramey Memo &#124; UTA Magazine</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Ramey Memo &#124; UTA Magazine...</span></span></span>

## Why the original negative outranks an online copy

For provenance purposes, an archival negative and an internet JPG answer different questions. A JPG can show what the Ramey photograph looks like; it cannot by itself demonstrate where the underlying image came from, how many generations separate it from the camera exposure, whether it has been cropped or sharpened, or what processing occurred before it reached the viewer.

UTA's catalogue makes that distinction explicit. For the Ramey record, **“Negatives, Black & White”** is the original format while **JPG** is the file format supplied digitally.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mavmatrix.uta.edu/specialcollections_startelegram1940s/35502/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mavmatrix.uta.edu">[MavMatrix]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mavmatrix.uta.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mav Matrix General Ramey holding enlargement of telegram</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">General Ramey holding enlargement of telegramJuly 8, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span> Across the wider *Star-Telegram* collection, UTA similarly distinguishes archival negatives from their downloadable digital derivatives. The institution's collection description says only digitised collection materials are being provided through the online repository; the physical collection is much larger.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mavmatrix.uta.edu/specialcollections_startelegram/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mavmatrix.uta.edu">[MavMatrix]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mavmatrix.uta.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on uta.edu.</span></span></span>

That hierarchy matters particularly when attempting to recover tiny type on the paper Ramey was holding. Every intermediate operation — photographic printing, enlargement, copying, scanning, compression, contrast adjustment, sharpening and resampling — can alter the visual information available to an analyst. Enhancement may make patterns easier to see without actually recovering information absent from the source. For serious comparison between competing decipherments, knowing which generation of the image was examined is therefore essential.

The history of Ramey-memo research illustrates the problem. A 2002 study by James Houran and Kevin Randle used several different image sources, including an enlargement derived from the *Star-Telegram* Photograph Collection at UTA, an enlargement made by UTA for researcher Stanton Friedman, and other scans and prints. The authors described the memo as difficult to read even under favourable conditions and found that suggested context influenced what participants believed they could decipher.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

That finding separates two issues sometimes conflated in Roswell discussions. **Provenance can be strong while legibility remains poor.** UTA's custody can give researchers considerable confidence about the historical photographic source without making every proposed word on Ramey's sheet reliable. Indeed, the availability of an original-generation archival source makes methodological caution more, not less, important: analysts can distinguish limitations inherent in the 1947 exposure from artefacts introduced by later copies.

## What archival handling adds to provenance

UTA's stewardship supplies several safeguards that informal possession cannot. The negative has an institutional collection identity, archival date, collection number, stated original format and documented relationship to a much larger body of *Star-Telegram* material. UTA also identifies Special Collections and Archives as the provider of the collection and directs reproduction enquiries through Special Collections rather than presenting the online file as an ownerless image.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mavmatrix.uta.edu/specialcollections_startelegram1940s/35502/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mavmatrix.uta.edu">[MavMatrix]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mavmatrix.uta.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mav Matrix General Ramey holding enlargement of telegram</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">General Ramey holding enlargement of telegramJuly 8, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

The wider archive provides useful internal controls as well. Other *Star-Telegram* catalogue entries use the same AR406 collection framework and identify black-and-white negatives as their original format. For example, a 1946 photograph of Ramey is catalogued as **AR406-6-423**, while another 1946 Ramey assignment is **AR406-6-1887**.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mavmatrix.uta.edu/specialcollections_startelegram1940s/30575/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mavmatrix.uta.edu">[MavMatrix]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mavmatrix.uta.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mav Matrix&quot;Brigadier General R. M. Ramey</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MavMatrix&quot;Brigadier General R. M. Ramey&quot;May 7, 1946...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 7, 1946</span></span></span> The Roswell files therefore conform to the archive's broader cataloguing pattern rather than carrying a provenance scheme created specially for UFO material.

There is nevertheless an important limit to what should be called a “chain of custody”. In forensic usage, that phrase can imply a continuous log documenting every transfer and every person who handled a piece of evidence. The public archival records located for the Ramey negatives do not provide that level of item-by-item documentation from Johnson's camera in 1947 to the present. What they establish much more securely is **institutional provenance**: the negative is part of the *Star-Telegram* photographic archive, and UTA documents how that newspaper archive came under university custody beginning in 1984.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://txarchives.org/utarl/finding_aids/00147.xml" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: txarchives.org">[Texas Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">txarchives.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Texas ArchivesFort Worth Star-Telegram Collection, Series 7: Printed Material: A Guide - TARO...</span></span></span>

That distinction prevents the provenance argument from becoming stronger than the records justify. It is reasonable to say that the surviving negative has a documented archival lineage through the newspaper collection. It would be excessive to claim that a publicly available handling log accounts for every movement or examination of that individual negative over nearly eight decades.

Handling itself may also matter to image analysis. Houran and Randle's 2002 paper referred to an older print made before the original negative had undergone as much handling, alongside later enlargements and scans.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=e162b50a3885a28c826cb31c3deca69abaaba9ee&amp;repid=rep1&amp;type=pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: citeseerx.ist.psu.edu">[CiteSeerX]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">citeseerx.ist.psu.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Journal of Scientific Exploration, Vol. 16, No. 1, pp. 45–66, 2002...</span></span></span> Researcher Kevin Randle has separately reported inspecting the original negative at UTA and has published views showing the complete negative and its edge markings, although this is a researcher's account rather than UTA's formal catalogue documentation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2016/05/the-ramey-memo-negative.html?showComment=1462514194505" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: The Ramey Memo Negative</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: The Ramey Memo Negative</span></span></span> It is therefore useful evidence about research access and physical examination, but secondary to the university's own collection and acquisition records when reconstructing custody.

## What the chain of custody does — and does not — establish

The strongest conclusion is narrower than many arguments about the Ramey memo suggest. UTA's holdings make the photograph's **photographic provenance** considerably easier to establish than the memo's **textual meaning**.

The evidence supports several linked propositions. The image belongs to the *Fort Worth Star-Telegram* photographic archive; UTA catalogues the relevant 8 July 1947 material as black-and-white negatives; related photographs from the Roswell assignment occupy the same dated archival grouping; and UTA documents the institutional transfer of the newspaper archives into Special Collections beginning in 1984.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mavmatrix.uta.edu/specialcollections_startelegram1940s/35502/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mavmatrix.uta.edu">[MavMatrix]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mavmatrix.uta.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mav Matrix General Ramey holding enlargement of telegram</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">General Ramey holding enlargement of telegramJuly 8, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

This substantially reduces the plausibility of a late photographic fabrication being introduced into the Roswell literature and passed off as Johnson's 1947 image. It also gives researchers something unusually valuable in a controversial historical case: a physical archival source against which later prints, scans and enhancements can in principle be checked.

What it does **not** establish is equally important. Archival custody cannot identify the author of the sheet, prove that it was a military telegram, establish its security classification, determine whether Ramey had just received or sent it, or validate a disputed transcription. UTA's own catalogue labels the image “General Ramey holding enlargement of telegram”, but archival description is metadata, not an independent forensic determination of every characteristic of the photographed document.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mavmatrix.uta.edu/specialcollections_startelegram1940s/35502/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mavmatrix.uta.edu">[MavMatrix]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mavmatrix.uta.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mav Matrix General Ramey holding enlargement of telegram</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">General Ramey holding enlargement of telegramJuly 8, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

Nor does an authentic negative create detail that Johnson's camera failed to record. The experimental work on memo decipherment found significant effects from suggestion and expectation, despite using relatively good source material derived from the UTA holdings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span> The persistent disagreement over individual words is therefore compatible with a perfectly genuine 1947 photograph.

For the wider debate over Ramey [memo authenticity]({{ 'authenticity/' | relative_url }}), this is the key distinction. The strongest part of the case is not that UTA's negatives prove a particular Roswell interpretation. It is that researchers do not have to begin with an anonymous scan of uncertain ancestry. Behind the copies is a catalogued negative embedded in the *Star-Telegram*'s historical photographic archive, and behind that archive is a documented institutional transfer into UTA Special Collections. That gives the Ramey photograph an unusually firm physical provenance while leaving the far harder question — what the tiny photographed text actually says — unresolved.

    if (persist) {
