## Introduction

The atomic-test condition in James Houran and Kevin D. Randle’s 2002 experiment is important because it provides a direct comparison for claims that Roswell-specific words can simply be “read” from the [Ramey memo]({{ 'ramey-memo/' | relative_url }}) photograph. Participants who were told that the blurred document concerned secret atomic-bomb testing began reporting terms such as “flash”, “glasses”, “atomic” and “laboratory” — words that fitted the supplied [story]({{ 'story/' | relative_url }}) but were not the signature Roswell readings promoted by earlier investigators. Participants given a Roswell framing, by contrast, tended to report Roswell-compatible words. That comparison does not prove that every claimed word in the [Ramey]({{ 'ramey/' | relative_url }}) memo is imaginary. ## The alternative cover story

Houran and Randle recruited 176 participants, with an average age of 37.4, and randomly assigned them to three conditions. Fifty-nine were told that the photograph showed a military memo that might contain information about the crash, recovery and cover-up of an extraterrestrial craft near Roswell. Fifty-eight were told instead that it might contain information about secret atomic-bomb testing. The remaining 59 were given no suggested subject at all and were simply asked to study a photograph of a document.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://abcdocz.com/doc/32352/%E2%80%9Ca-message-in-a-bottle-%E2%80%9D-confounds-in-deciphering-the-j-h" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abcdocz.com">[Abcdo cz]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abcdocz.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Abcdo cz“A Message in a Bottle:” Confounds in Deciphering the J H</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Abcdo cz“A Message in a Bottle:” Confounds in Deciphering the J H</span></span></span>

The distinction matters. The atomic-test group was not the neutral control in the strict experimental sense; the blind group filled that role. Rather, atomic testing served as an **alternative prime**: an invented context strong enough to give readers expectations, but different from the Roswell narrative whose influence the researchers wanted to test. If participants produced Roswell language regardless of what they had been told, that would support the idea that those word-shapes were genuinely visible. If changing the story changed the apparent words, [expectation]({{ 'expectation/' | relative_url }}) would be implicated.

The participants did not merely receive a poor photocopy and guess at it. According to the study, they were shown an enlarged print of the memo area together with several high-resolution scans then available from material derived from the Fort Worth Star-Telegram photograph collection. They could use a magnifying glass and view computer images individually; a research assistant who was blind to the researchers’ hypotheses was available to help manipulate the images. Participants recorded what they thought they could read line by line, rather than selecting words from a prepared list.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://abcdocz.com/doc/32352/%E2%80%9Ca-message-in-a-bottle-%E2%80%9D-confounds-in-deciphering-the-j-h" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abcdocz.com">[Abcdo cz]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abcdocz.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Abcdo cz“A Message in a Bottle:” Confounds in Deciphering the J H</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Abcdo cz“A Message in a Bottle:” Confounds in Deciphering the J H</span></span></span>

That last point makes the atomic condition especially revealing. The experimenters did not have to insert “atomic”, “flash” or “laboratory” into a multiple-choice menu. Supplying the broad narrative appears to have been enough for participants to generate nuclear-themed readings themselves.

## Atomic-themed decipherments

The numerical pattern was striking. The Roswell-primed participants reported an average of 4.6 words, while the atomic-test group reported 4.8. The blind group averaged only 1.6. Houran and Randle reported a statistically significant effect of suggestion condition, with both primed groups producing significantly more proposed words than the blind condition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://abcdocz.com/doc/32352/%E2%80%9Ca-message-in-a-bottle-%E2%80%9D-confounds-in-deciphering-the-j-h" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abcdocz.com">[Abcdo cz]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abcdocz.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Abcdo cz“A Message in a Bottle:” Confounds in Deciphering the J H</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Abcdo cz“A Message in a Bottle:” Confounds in Deciphering the J H</span></span></span>

Just as important was *which* words appeared. The study’s table associates the atomic-test condition with readings including “glasses”, “morning”, “flash”, “atomic”, “laboratory” and “meaning”. The Roswell group instead generated distinctive terms such as “remains”, “crash” and “UFO”, alongside words that also appeared elsewhere. The researchers explicitly described “flash”, “glasses” and “atomic” as interpretations congruent with the atomic-bomb scenario.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://abcdocz.com/doc/32352/%E2%80%9Ca-message-in-a-bottle-%E2%80%9D-confounds-in-deciphering-the-j-h" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abcdocz.com">[Abcdo cz]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abcdocz.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Abcdo cz“A Message in a Bottle:” Confounds in Deciphering the J H</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Abcdo cz“A Message in a Bottle:” Confounds in Deciphering the J H</span></span></span>

There is no independent reason to think the photographed document actually described an atomic test. That is precisely why the condition is useful. The atomic story functioned as a deliberately different interpretive frame applied to exactly the same underlying visual information. Once a nuclear context was supplied, marks that another observer might parse as unrelated letter combinations became compatible with a nuclear vocabulary.

The time spent looking at the image also changed with the framing. The Roswell group worked for about 20 minutes on average, the atomic group for about 16 minutes, and the blind group for about 14. Houran and Randle therefore cautioned that suggestion may have changed not only *what* people expected to see but also how much effort they invested in trying to extract words. An exciting account of a UFO cover-up or a secret atomic programme may simply induce more persistent pattern-searching than being handed an unexplained old document.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://abcdocz.com/doc/32352/%E2%80%9Ca-message-in-a-bottle-%E2%80%9D-confounds-in-deciphering-the-j-h" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abcdocz.com">[Abcdo cz]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abcdocz.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Abcdo cz“A Message in a Bottle:” Confounds in Deciphering the J H</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Abcdo cz“A Message in a Bottle:” Confounds in Deciphering the J H</span></span></span>

## What survived the competing primes

The experiment did not reduce the memo to complete visual noise. Some readings crossed the experimental boundaries. Across all three groups, participants showed agreement on “Fort Worth TX”, “story” and “weather balloons”; the study also reported substantial recognition of punctuation. “Land” appeared in the same location in the two primed conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://abcdocz.com/doc/32352/%E2%80%9Ca-message-in-a-bottle-%E2%80%9D-confounds-in-deciphering-the-j-h" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abcdocz.com">[Abcdo cz]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abcdocz.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Abcdo cz“A Message in a Bottle:” Confounds in Deciphering the J H</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Abcdo cz“A Message in a Bottle:” Confounds in Deciphering the J H</span></span></span>

That distinction is central to understanding what the atomic group actually demonstrates. A word that appears mainly after participants are given a matching narrative is weaker evidence than one independently reported at the same position by people exposed to incompatible stories or no story at all. Houran and Randle consequently argued that portions of the document might genuinely be legible while its broader meaning remained highly vulnerable to expectation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://abcdocz.com/doc/32352/%E2%80%9Ca-message-in-a-bottle-%E2%80%9D-confounds-in-deciphering-the-j-h" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abcdocz.com">[Abcdo cz]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abcdocz.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Abcdo cz“A Message in a Bottle:” Confounds in Deciphering the J H</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Abcdo cz“A Message in a Bottle:” Confounds in Deciphering the J H</span></span></span>

This is why the atomic-test comparison matters more than a simple observation that “people disagree”. Disagreement alone could result from differences in eyesight, equipment or patience. The experimental comparison ties particular families of readings to deliberately manipulated expectations. Roswell framing generated Roswell-compatible material; atomic framing generated atomic-compatible material; withholding a story sharply reduced the number of words people claimed to recognise.

For controversial phrases such as readings involving “victims” or a crashed “disc”, that creates a higher evidential bar. The question is not whether a reader can be shown an enhanced image and persuaded that a particular phrase looks plausible once it has been pointed out. The stronger test is whether independent readers, unaware of the hypothesised wording and its Roswell significance, repeatedly identify the same characters in the same positions.

## Limits of the experiment

The 2002 study itself has important weaknesses. Most notably, when a referee requested participant-by-participant counts for individual deciphered words, the researchers discovered that the original score sheets had been discarded by a research assistant after a computer database and notes had been prepared. As a result, detailed frequency data were retained only for words recorded as common across conditions. That loss makes the experiment less auditable than a modern replication should be.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://abcdocz.com/doc/32352/%E2%80%9Ca-message-in-a-bottle-%E2%80%9D-confounds-in-deciphering-the-j-h" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abcdocz.com">[Abcdo cz]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abcdocz.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Abcdo cz“A Message in a Bottle:” Confounds in Deciphering the J H</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Abcdo cz“A Message in a Bottle:” Confounds in Deciphering the J H</span></span></span>

The authors also acknowledged the possible motivation confound. Participants knew whether they had been given an intriguing UFO or atomic-bomb premise, while the blind group received the comparatively mundane instruction to examine an old document. The longer viewing times in the two primed conditions therefore leave open whether they found more words solely because of perceptual suggestion, because they searched more intensely, or because both effects operated together.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://abcdocz.com/doc/32352/%E2%80%9Ca-message-in-a-bottle-%E2%80%9D-confounds-in-deciphering-the-j-h" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abcdocz.com">[Abcdo cz]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abcdocz.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Abcdo cz“A Message in a Bottle:” Confounds in Deciphering the J H</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Abcdo cz“A Message in a Bottle:” Confounds in Deciphering the J H</span></span></span>

David [Rudiak]({{ 'rudiak/' | relative_url }}), whose own Ramey-memo work supported Roswell-related readings, strongly disputed Houran and Randle’s interpretation and criticised the study’s statistics, methodology and missing underlying records. His objections formed part of a prolonged debate with Houran over how the memo should be validated. Those criticisms are relevant because the experiment should not be treated as a definitive laboratory disproof of every attempted transcription.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gbppr.net/ufoupdates/pdf/2003-02.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gbppr.net">[GBPPR]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gbppr.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO UpDates: Feb 2003December 10, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 10, 2011</span></span></span>

Even so, one criticism commonly directed at the study — that brief examination by ordinary participants cannot equal hundreds of hours of specialist image work — does not remove the control-condition lesson. The experiment was not designed to produce the final transcription. Its purpose was to test whether prior narrative information could influence decipherment of the ambiguous stimulus. A specialist can spend considerably longer analysing an image and still remain vulnerable to expectation if the candidate wording is already known. ## What the comparison reveals

The atomic-test group changes how claims about the Ramey memo should be evaluated. It demonstrates that semantic coherence is not, by itself, evidence that a proposed transcription is correct. Humans are exceptionally good at assembling partial shapes into meaningful language once a context supplies likely words. In the Ramey photograph, the remaining visual information appears sufficient for some genuine common readings but also ambiguous enough for incompatible stories to produce different apparent text.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://abcdocz.com/doc/32352/%E2%80%9Ca-message-in-a-bottle-%E2%80%9D-confounds-in-deciphering-the-j-h" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abcdocz.com">[Abcdo cz]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abcdocz.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Abcdo cz“A Message in a Bottle:” Confounds in Deciphering the J H</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Abcdo cz“A Message in a Bottle:” Confounds in Deciphering the J H</span></span></span>

Houran and Randle therefore recommended blind, independent analysis by at least three qualified laboratories, using standardised [enhancement]({{ 'enhancement/' | relative_url }}) procedures and comparing proposed words at precise locations. They specifically warned against working backwards from a Roswell scenario to find historically plausible names, abbreviations or phrases in poorly formed text, because their experiment showed that changing the assumed context could change the apparent reading.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://abcdocz.com/doc/32352/%E2%80%9Ca-message-in-a-bottle-%E2%80%9D-confounds-in-deciphering-the-j-h" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abcdocz.com">[Abcdo cz]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abcdocz.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Abcdo cz“A Message in a Bottle:” Confounds in Deciphering the J H</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Abcdo cz“A Message in a Bottle:” Confounds in Deciphering the J H</span></span></span>

They also proposed a particularly useful control that goes beyond the atomic prime: recreate the photographic circumstances using the same type of camera and documents whose true wording is already known, photograph them at comparable angles and distances, then test whether the resulting degraded text can actually be recovered. Such a known-text experiment would measure what information survives the photographic process instead of assuming that sufficiently elaborate enhancement must eventually reveal the original wording.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://abcdocz.com/doc/32352/%E2%80%9Ca-message-in-a-bottle-%E2%80%9D-confounds-in-deciphering-the-j-h" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abcdocz.com">[Abcdo cz]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abcdocz.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Abcdo cz“A Message in a Bottle:” Confounds in Deciphering the J H</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Abcdo cz“A Message in a Bottle:” Confounds in Deciphering the J H</span></span></span>

Later work continued to treat priming as an unresolved methodological problem. A subsequent review in the *Journal of Scientific Exploration* noted that Houran pursued independent laboratory analyses after the 2002 study, specifically as an attempt to move beyond interpretation by Roswell advocates and towards triangulated assessment by specialists.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journalofscientificexploration.org/index.php/jse/article/view/2635/1631" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journalofscientificexploration.org">[Journal of Scientific Exploration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journalofscientificexploration.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Journal of Scientific Exploration Anomalistics</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Journal of Scientific ExplorationAnomalisticsMarch 28, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 28, 2026</span></span></span>

The atomic-test condition therefore does not settle what General Ramey was holding, nor does it establish that every Roswell-related word proposed for the memo is false. Its narrower finding is more useful: the same degraded photograph can support apparently meaningful but mutually incompatible readings when observers are supplied with different stories. Any claimed Roswell phrase that cannot survive genuinely blind comparison must consequently be treated as an interpretation of ambiguous marks, not as securely recovered documentary text.
