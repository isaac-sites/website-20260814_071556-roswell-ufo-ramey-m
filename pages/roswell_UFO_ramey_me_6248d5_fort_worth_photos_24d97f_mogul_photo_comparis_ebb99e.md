## Introduction

The strongest case that the [Fort Worth debris]({{ 'substitution/' | relative_url }}) photographs are compatible with [Project MOGUL]({{ 'project-mogul/' | relative_url }}) does not depend on the pictures showing a complete secret balloon system. They do not. What they show is a much narrower but important match: crumpled reflective sheet, thin lightweight sticks, tape, rubber-like balloon material and the collapsed framework of a radar reflector. Those are precisely the sorts of ordinary components used in the early New Mexico balloon work later identified with MOGUL. The US Air Force's reconstruction says the early trains used neoprene meteorological balloons and multi-faced radar targets built from metallic material, balsa wood, tape, twine and small fittings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://muller.lbl.gov/teaching/physics10/Roswell/USMogulReport.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muller.lbl.gov">[Muller Lab]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muller.lbl.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Muller Lab Project Mogul</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Muller Lab Project Mogul</span></span></span>

That visual correspondence makes MOGUL a substantially stronger explanation for the photographed hardware than the phrase "weather balloon" alone suggests. But it has an important limit. The photographs can establish that the material displayed in General Roger [Ramey]({{ 'ramey/' | relative_url }})'s Fort Worth office looks like balloon-and-[radar-target]({{ 'radar-target/' | relative_url }}) hardware; they cannot establish, by appearance alone, that it came from a particular MOGUL flight, or even prove that the photographed debris was identical to everything recovered near Roswell. That distinction is central to evaluating the photographs as competing evidence.

## What is actually visible in the Fort Worth photographs?

The photographs taken in Fort Worth on 8 July 1947 show a surprisingly simple collection of wreckage. Reporter and photographer J. [Bond Johnson]({{ 'johnson/' | relative_url }}) later described what he photographed as "crumpled tinfoil, broken sticks and ragged rubber". The surviving images show Brigadier General Roger Ramey, Colonel Thomas DuBose and Major [Jesse Marcel]({{ 'marcel/' | relative_url }}) with this material spread across the office floor.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.star-telegram.com/opinion/bud-kennedy/article289713499.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: star-telegram.com">[Fort Worth Star-Telegram]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">star-telegram.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fort Worth Star-Telegram‘Roswell Incident’ UFO crash in 1947 stirred Fort Worth &#124; Fort Worth Star-TelegramJuly 5, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 5, 2024</span></span></span>

The most diagnostic object is not the rubber but the angular structure mixed into the debris. Its combination of reflective faces and slender supporting members is consistent with a collapsed radar reflector, also called a RAWIN target. RAWIN — shorthand associated with radar wind observation — targets were carried beneath balloons so that ground radar could follow them and derive winds aloft. In intact form they were lightweight, multi-faced reflectors; after a crash and exposure to weather, their metallic covering and fragile frame could become the loose foil-and-stick wreckage visible in the photographs. The Air Force's later MOGUL reconstruction describes such targets as resembling box kites and being made from balsa and metallicised material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://muller.lbl.gov/teaching/physics10/Roswell/USMogulReport.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muller.lbl.gov">[Muller Lab]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muller.lbl.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Muller Lab Project Mogul</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Muller Lab Project Mogul</span></span></span>

This identification was also made at the time, rather than being invented after MOGUL was declassified. Irving Newton, the Fort Worth weather officer summoned to Ramey's office, later gave a sworn statement saying that he immediately recognised what he saw as a balloon and RAWIN target. He also recalled Marcel drawing his attention to coloured markings on pieces of the target sticks.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.users.globalnet.co.uk/~hex/ufo/text/pages026-050/page33.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: users.globalnet.co.uk">[Globalnet Users]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">users.globalnet.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Globalnet Userspage33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Air Force Roswell Report...</span></span></span>

A surviving 1947 documentary trail points in the same general direction. The Government Accountability Office's investigation found an FBI teletype of 8 July reporting that military authorities described the recovered object as resembling a high-altitude weather balloon with a radar reflector. The combined 509th Bomb Group and Roswell Army Air Field history likewise recorded that the initially announced "flying disc" was subsequently identified by military officials as a radar-tracking balloon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/nsiad" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[Government Accountability Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nsiad</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government Accountability OfficeGovernment Records: Results of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947 Crash Near Roswell, New Mexico &#124;...</span></span></span>

The important point is therefore quite specific: the Fort Worth photographs do not merely contain generic shiny debris. They contain a recognisable combination of balloon remains and radar-reflector construction.

## How MOGUL hardware was built

Project MOGUL was an experimental long-range detection programme intended to investigate whether acoustic signals in the upper atmosphere could reveal distant Soviet nuclear explosions. Its New Mexico work used balloon systems to carry experimental equipment to high altitude. The programme itself was sensitive, but much of the physical equipment employed in its balloon trains was conventional. The Air Force says that this helps explain why the wreckage could simultaneously belong to a classified programme and look thoroughly mundane.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.af.mil/The-Roswell-Report/About-Us/AF-75th-Anniversary/About-Us/AF-75th-Anniversary/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: af.mil">[Air Force]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Air Force The Roswell Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Air Force The Roswell Report</span></span></span>

Early New Mexico operations relied on trains of ordinary neoprene meteorological balloons rather than the later large polyethylene balloons associated with high-altitude research. The balloon train could also carry experimental instruments, lines, parachutes and radar reflectors. According to the Air Force reconstruction, the early MOGUL team increased the number of radar targets because a single reflector did not always provide an adequate radar return; Charles Moore recalled experiments using three to five targets.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://muller.lbl.gov/teaching/physics10/Roswell/USMogulReport.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muller.lbl.gov">[Muller Lab]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muller.lbl.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Muller Lab Project Mogul</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Muller Lab Project Mogul</span></span></span>

Those reflectors are the crucial bridge between MOGUL and the Fort Worth photographs. Air Force interviews described early targets as using aluminium foil or foil-backed paper over balsa framing, reinforced with tape and connected with nylon twine, eyelets and swivels. Photographs reproduced in the later *Roswell Report: Case Closed* similarly identify ML-307B/AP radar targets as structures of aluminised paper attached to balsa frames.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.users.globalnet.co.uk/~hex/ufo/text/pages026-050/page33.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: users.globalnet.co.uk">[Globalnet Users]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">users.globalnet.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Globalnet Userspage33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Air Force Roswell Report...</span></span></span>

This construction predicts an unusual-looking crash site without requiring unusual materials. A balloon train falling into open country could leave sheets of reflective material, snapped balsa members, lengths of cord and degraded rubber spread by wind. The complete airborne apparatus could have been hundreds of feet long, while the durable fragments gathered afterwards could fit into a comparatively small pile. That distinction matters when comparing an intact MOGUL train with the material photographed indoors: the office pictures show debris, not the original geometry of the airborne system.

There is another unusually specific correspondence. Contemporary reporting attributed to rancher W. W. Brazel described "tinfoil, paper, tape, and sticks", while later witnesses remembered coloured or patterned material that sometimes became described as strange writing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/63659/pg63659-images.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gutenberg.org">[Project Gutenberg]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gutenberg.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gutenberg.org.</span></span></span> Air Force investigators reported that some wartime-era radar targets used purplish-pink reinforcing tape bearing flower, heart or geometric designs, apparently because manufacturers had used commercially available material during wartime shortages. Moore and former MOGUL security officer Albert Trakowski both supplied information about this feature.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://muller.lbl.gov/teaching/physics10/Roswell/USMogulReport.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muller.lbl.gov">[Muller Lab]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muller.lbl.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Muller Lab Project Mogul</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Muller Lab Project Mogul</span></span></span>

Newton's recollection provides an independent connection to the Fort Worth display: he remembered faded lavender or pink figures on the sticks Marcel showed him.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.users.globalnet.co.uk/~hex/ufo/text/pages026-050/page33.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: users.globalnet.co.uk">[Globalnet Users]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">users.globalnet.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Globalnet Userspage33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Air Force Roswell Report...</span></span></span> Patterned tape therefore has greater evidential value than a generic resemblance between foil and foil. It offers a plausible explanation for a peculiar detail associated with the wreckage while remaining consistent with radar-target manufacture.

## Where the photographic match is strongest

Taken strictly as a hardware comparison, several correspondences make the MOGUL interpretation persuasive.

**Reflective sheet.** MOGUL-associated radar targets used metallic or metallicised reflecting surfaces, while reflective material dominates the Fort Worth debris. This is exactly where radar-reflector wreckage should produce a conspicuous visual signature.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/63659/pg63659-images.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gutenberg.org">[Project Gutenberg]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gutenberg.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gutenberg.org.</span></span></span>

**Light wooden members.** The photographs contain thin broken sticks, matching the lightweight balsa framework described for radar targets. The Air Force's 1994 investigation went further and reported an attempt to estimate the dimensions of sticks visible in the photographs from information about the original camera; it concluded that the measurements were compatible with radar-target wooden members. That is a reconstruction rather than a direct measurement of surviving debris, but it moves the comparison beyond appearance alone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.users.globalnet.co.uk/~hex/ufo/text/pages026-050/page33.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: users.globalnet.co.uk">[Globalnet Users]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">users.globalnet.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Globalnet Userspage33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Air Force Roswell Report...</span></span></span>

**Balloon material.** Ragged rubber appears among the photographed wreckage. The early MOGUL flights in question used neoprene meteorological balloons, so degraded rubber is expected hardware rather than an anomaly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.users.globalnet.co.uk/~hex/ufo/text/pages026-050/page33.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: users.globalnet.co.uk">[Globalnet Users]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">users.globalnet.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Globalnet Userspage33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Air Force Roswell Report...</span></span></span>

**Radar-target geometry.** Portions of the photographed debris retain enough structure to resemble a collapsed corner reflector rather than random aircraft wreckage. The Air Force's technical description of MOGUL's radar targets — a multi-faced, box-kite-like structure — corresponds closely to that geometry.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://muller.lbl.gov/teaching/physics10/Roswell/USMogulReport.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muller.lbl.gov">[Muller Lab]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muller.lbl.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Muller Lab Project Mogul</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Muller Lab Project Mogul</span></span></span>

**Coloured markings.** Newton's recollection of pink or lavender figures on the sticks fits the documented recollections about patterned reinforcing tape used on some radar targets.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.users.globalnet.co.uk/~hex/ufo/text/pages026-050/page33.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: users.globalnet.co.uk">[Globalnet Users]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">users.globalnet.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Globalnet Userspage33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Air Force Roswell Report...</span></span></span>

There is also testimony connecting the photographic appearance to the New Mexico recovery. Sheridan Cavitt, who accompanied Marcel during the investigation of the ranch, told Air Force investigators decades later that he remembered reflective material and very light, bamboo-like sticks and considered the material consistent with a weather balloon. He also reviewed the Fort Worth photographs and said their contents were consistent with what he remembered recovering. His statement is retrospective and therefore cannot carry the weight of a 1947 laboratory record, but it reinforces the physical comparison rather than contradicting it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://disclosdex.com/events/1994-usaf-roswell-report" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: disclosdex.com">[Disclosdex]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">disclosdex.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1994 usaf roswell report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1994 USAF Roswell Report Release &#124; DisclosdexSeptember 8, 1994...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 8, 1994</span></span></span>

Put together, these features make a strong case for identifying the *type of hardware* in the photographs. One need not accept every part of the later Air Force Roswell reconstruction to recognise that the photographed object closely resembles a balloon-borne radar target.

## Where the visual match stops

The photographs nevertheless cannot do everything sometimes asked of them. Most importantly, a radar target used by MOGUL was not necessarily visually distinguishable from a radar target used for ordinary meteorological work. The Air Force itself acknowledged the problem: the MOGUL balloon equipment was assembled substantially from conventional components, with the secret lying primarily in the purpose and configuration of the programme rather than in exotic-looking materials.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.af.mil/The-Roswell-Report/About-Us/AF-75th-Anniversary/About-Us/AF-75th-Anniversary/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: af.mil">[Air Force]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Air Force The Roswell Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Air Force The Roswell Report</span></span></span>

That means the visual argument has two different strengths:

1. **Strong:** the Fort Worth material looks like balloon and radar-reflector wreckage.
2. **More inferential:** that wreckage came specifically from Project MOGUL.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://muller.lbl.gov/teaching/physics10/Roswell/USMogulReport.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muller.lbl.gov">[muller.lbl.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muller.lbl.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Muller Lab Project Mogul</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Muller Lab Project Mogul</span></span></span>

The second conclusion requires chronology, flight records, trajectory reconstruction and testimony in addition to the photographs. It cannot be read directly from the foil or balsa.

This becomes especially important with the proposed source, the early-June balloon operation generally labelled MOGUL Flight No. 4. The Air Force reconstruction concluded that a balloon cluster released on 4 June 1947 was the most likely source of the ranch debris. It relied on MOGUL documentation, Albert Crary's journal, Charles Moore's recollections and the compatibility between the expected equipment and the debris descriptions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://muller.lbl.gov/teaching/physics10/Roswell/USMogulReport.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muller.lbl.gov">[Muller Lab]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muller.lbl.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Muller Lab Project Mogul</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Muller Lab Project Mogul</span></span></span>

Critics dispute that reconstruction. Crary's entry for 4 June stated that there was "No balloon flight again on account of clouds", although the same entry recorded that a regular sonobuoy was flown using a cluster of balloons. Critics therefore argue that the documentary record does not securely establish the full MOGUL train, including the radar reflectors required by the photographic comparison.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2007/08/there-was-no-flight-no-4.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: There Was No Flight No</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">426 Aug 2007 — Albert Crary&#x27;s (seen here) diary, which provides us with the only record for Mogul Flight No. 4, the culprit identified by...</span></span></span> The Air Force interpretation treats this as one of the unlogged or informally recorded service flights and relies partly on Moore's later recollection of how those early flights were configured.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://muller.lbl.gov/teaching/physics10/Roswell/USMogulReport.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: muller.lbl.gov">[Muller Lab]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">muller.lbl.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Muller Lab Project Mogul</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Muller Lab Project Mogul</span></span></span>

That dispute does not make the radar-target identification disappear. It changes what can safely be inferred from it. Even if the exact Flight No. 4 reconstruction were rejected, the Fort Worth wreckage could still be accurately identified as ordinary balloon-and-reflector material. Conversely, demonstrating that MOGUL used visually matching hardware does not prove that the particular scraps in Ramey's office travelled from Alamogordo to the Foster ranch and then to Fort Worth.

There is no visible serial number, instrument identification plate or other unique marker in the photographs that ties the debris conclusively to MOGUL. Nor is there a surviving piece of the Fort Worth material available for modern materials analysis. The comparison is consequently morphological and historical — materials, construction and context — rather than a forensic identification of a unique artefact.

## Do the Fort Worth photos match Project MOGUL gear?

Yes, at the component level, and quite closely. The reflective covering, light wooden sticks, rubber remains, tape and recognisable radar-reflector structure in the Fort Worth photographs correspond to hardware documented for the balloon operations associated with Project MOGUL. The particularly distinctive recollections of pink or purple markings also have a plausible counterpart in patterned reinforcing tape reportedly used on some radar targets. Contemporary military identification of the material as balloon-and-radar-target wreckage strengthens that comparison.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.users.globalnet.co.uk/~hex/ufo/text/pages026-050/page33.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: users.globalnet.co.uk">[globalnet.co.uk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">users.globalnet.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Globalnet Userspage33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Air Force Roswell Report...</span></span></span>

What the photographs cannot settle is provenance. They do not independently demonstrate that the material came from the MOGUL balloon operation of 4 June, because MOGUL used components that were not unique to MOGUL. Nor can photographs resolve the long-running allegation that mundane material was substituted in Fort Worth for different debris recovered in New Mexico. That allegation depends primarily on later [witness testimony]({{ 'witness-testimony/' | relative_url }}), not on a visible feature by which the photographed wreckage can be tested.

The documentary record itself is incomplete. The GAO's 1995 investigation found that some Roswell Army Air Field administrative records and outgoing messages covering the relevant period had been destroyed, with surviving disposition paperwork failing to identify clearly who destroyed them, when, or under what authority. Its search nevertheless found two contemporaneous 1947 records concerning Roswell, both reflecting the military's identification of the object as balloon or radar-tracking equipment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gao.gov/products/nsiad" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gao.gov">[Government Accountability Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gao.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nsiad</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Government Accountability OfficeGovernment Records: Results of a Search for Records Concerning the 1947 Crash Near Roswell, New Mexico &#124;...</span></span></span>

For the narrower question posed by the photographs, the most defensible conclusion is therefore stronger than "it looks like a weather balloon" but weaker than "the photographs prove MOGUL Flight No. 4". They provide substantial physical support for the presence of exactly the class of lightweight balloon and radar-reflector hardware used in the early MOGUL programme. The unresolved step is not what the photographed structure resembles; it is whether those particular photographed components can be securely traced back through Roswell to one specific MOGUL balloon train.

      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
