## Introduction

The strongest reason [independent readers]({{ 'reader-convergence/' | relative_url }}) repeatedly saw “Fort Worth” in the [Ramey memo]({{ 'ramey-memo/' | relative_url }}) is not that the phrase fits the Roswell story. It is that people given conflicting stories still reported the same place-name in the same part of the photographed document. In James Houran and Kevin Randle’s 2002 experiment, 52 of 176 participants identified “Fort Worth TX”: 24 in a Roswell-UFO group, 17 in a group told the memo concerned atomic-bomb testing, and 11 who were given no explanation of its subject.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

That pattern is unusual because the experiment also demonstrated how readily expectations changed what people thought they could read. Most proposed wording did not survive across the three conditions. “Fort Worth TX” did. The result therefore offers evidence for a modest but important proposition: this particular patch of the photograph may preserve enough visual information for multiple readers to recognise a real location independently. It does **not** establish that the whole memo is readable, or settle what happened at Roswell.

## The 52 readers who identified Fort Worth TX

Houran and Randle designed their experiment specifically because previous attempts to decipher the [Ramey]({{ 'ramey/' | relative_url }}) memo had produced radically different [transcriptions]({{ 'transcriptions/' | relative_url }}). Their concern was that researchers might be converting indistinct marks into meaningful language partly because they already knew the Roswell story and expected particular words to be present. The study therefore separated 176 participants into three randomly assigned conditions: 59 received a Roswell-UFO interpretation, 58 were told the document concerned secret atomic-bomb testing, and 59 were given no proposed subject.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

All three groups examined reproductions of the same photographed document. According to the researchers, participants had access to an enlarged print and four high-resolution versions of the memo area derived from material associated with the Fort Worth Star-Telegram photograph collection. They were asked to record whatever they believed they could decipher rather than choose words from a supplied list.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=e162b50a3885a28c826cb31c3deca69abaaba9ee&amp;repid=rep1&amp;type=pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: citeseerx.ist.psu.edu">[CiteSeerX]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">citeseerx.ist.psu.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Journal of Scientific Exploration, Vol. 16, No. 1, pp. 45–66, 2002October 19, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 19, 2025</span></span></span>

The resulting distribution makes “Fort Worth TX” especially interesting:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Roswell-UFO condition:** 24 of 59 participants reported “Fort Worth TX”.
* **Atomic-bomb condition:** 17 of 58 reported it.
* **Blind condition:** 11 of 59 reported it.
* **Overall:** 52 of 176 participants, or just under 30 per cent, independently produced the location reading.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://noufors.com/Documents/Books%2C%20Manuals%20and%20Published%20Papers/Specialty%20UFO%20Publications/Journal%20of%20Scientific%20Exploration/jse_16_1_houran.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: noufors.com">[Noufors]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">noufors.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Noufors&#x27;&#x27;A Message in a Bottle:&quot; Confounds in Deciphering the Ramey Memo from the Roswell UFO Case...</span></span></span>

</div>

A 30 per cent recognition rate would be unimpressive if readers were looking at clear typescript. Here, however, the underlying stimulus was so poor that many other supposed words attracted little agreement, and the US government's own earlier attempt to enhance the photograph had concluded that its quality was insufficient to reveal the details being sought.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

The number 52 consequently needs to be read in relation to the rest of the experiment, not as a standalone percentage. The notable result was **cross-condition convergence amid widespread disagreement**.

## Agreement survived conflicting prompts

The experiment's most useful feature for assessing “Fort Worth” is the deliberately misleading atomic-bomb condition. If recognition of the location had been mainly produced by Roswell expectations, readers told that the document concerned nuclear testing should have been much less likely to supply a place associated with the photographed Roswell episode.

Instead, 17 members of that group still produced “Fort Worth TX”. Eleven blind participants did so without being told whether the sheet concerned Roswell, atomic testing or anything else.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://noufors.com/Documents/Books%2C%20Manuals%20and%20Published%20Papers/Specialty%20UFO%20Publications/Journal%20of%20Scientific%20Exploration/jse_16_1_houran.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: noufors.com">[Noufors]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">noufors.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Noufors&#x27;&#x27;A Message in a Bottle:&quot; Confounds in Deciphering the Ramey Memo from the Roswell UFO Case...</span></span></span>

That does not eliminate suggestion. Indeed, the experiment showed precisely the opposite at the document level. Participants produced different vocabulary depending upon the [story]({{ 'story/' | relative_url }}) they had been given. The researchers concluded that suggestion and cognitive style strongly influenced attempts to decipher the memo.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

“Fort Worth” is significant because it partly resisted that effect.

The contrast becomes clearer when the groups' other responses are considered. The UFO condition generated such readings as “remains”, “crash” and “UFO”; the atomic condition generated “atomic” and “laboratory”. Those differences are exactly the sort of [expectation]({{ 'expectation/' | relative_url }})-driven interpretation the experiment was intended to detect. Yet “Fort Worth TX” appeared in all three groups in the same location.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://noufors.com/Documents/Books%2C%20Manuals%20and%20Published%20Papers/Specialty%20UFO%20Publications/Journal%20of%20Scientific%20Exploration/jse_16_1_houran.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: noufors.com">[Noufors]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">noufors.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Noufors&#x27;&#x27;A Message in a Bottle:&quot; Confounds in Deciphering the Ramey Memo from the Roswell UFO Case...</span></span></span>

There were other areas of overlap. “Story” appeared in each condition, while versions of “weather balloons” appeared in the groups at different frequencies. Houran and Randle therefore did not single out Fort Worth as proof that one complete transcription was correct. Their broader conclusion was that agreement on particular words at identical locations suggested that **some portions really were legible**, even though interpretation of the document as a whole remained vulnerable to suggestion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

That distinction is central. The experiment produced evidence for islands of recognisable text, not a reliable continuous transcript.

## Earlier readers had converged on the same place

The 2002 results did not arise in isolation. “Fort Worth” had already survived disagreements among researchers whose proposed versions of the surrounding text varied considerably.

Houran and Randle's review describes a 1998 effort organised by J. Bond Johnson, the Fort Worth Star-Telegram journalist-photographer who took most of the photographs in Ramey's office. Johnson's team proposed wording that included a reference to “Fort Worth, Tex”. Neil Morris subsequently produced a substantially different line-by-line interpretation. Much of his version diverged from Johnson's, but “Fort Worth, Texas” remained.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

John Kirby provided an even more useful comparison. According to the study, Kirby could make out relatively little despite examining the photograph with computer equipment, and he disagreed with consequential readings elsewhere in the document. Nevertheless, he identified the third-line wording as “At Fort Worth, Tex.”<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

This history matters because agreement is less persuasive when one reader simply adopts another reader's entire reconstruction. Here, the surrounding disagreements show that the analysts were not merely producing identical transcripts. They could differ over words capable of radically changing the memo's supposed meaning while continuing to recognise essentially the same Texas location.

There is still an important qualification. The early researchers were not experimentally isolated from one another. Houran and Randle noted that information had been shared among people attempting to decipher the memo, which weakens any claim that those earlier readings were completely independent.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

That weakness is precisely why the later experiment carries more weight for this narrow question. Its atomic and blind groups provide a cleaner test of whether “Fort Worth” could emerge without a Roswell-specific prompt.

## Why Fort Worth may be easier to recognise

The experiment establishes repeated recognition more securely than it establishes the perceptual reason for that recognition. The sensible inference is that the relevant sequence of marks contains a comparatively distinctive combination of surviving letter shapes and word lengths. Houran and Randle themselves interpreted agreement on words in identical locations as evidence that parts of the document were genuinely legible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

A place-name also has a useful property in damaged text: several imperfect visual clues can reinforce one another. A reader does not necessarily need every letter of both words to survive cleanly. Partial character shapes, the break between two words, their relative lengths and the following state abbreviation can collectively constrain the possibilities. That is a plausible explanation for the repeated reading, but it should be treated as an inference from the pattern rather than as a demonstrated reconstruction of every character.

The historical setting supplies a separate consistency check. The photograph actually was made in Fort Worth on 8 July 1947. The University of Texas at Arlington's description of the original Star-Telegram material identifies J. Bond Johnson's photographs as showing Brigadier General Roger Ramey with the debris at Fort Worth Army Air Field and notes that the negatives are held in its Special Collections.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/spcouta/36080122212" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: flickr.com">[Flickr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">flickr.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Roswell UFO Incident, July 1947 &#124;...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 1947</span></span></span> Contemporary Roswell material had been brought to Ramey's higher headquarters there, and the resulting Fort Worth press episode produced the photographs containing the memo.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.star-telegram.com/opinion/bud-kennedy/article289713499.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: star-telegram.com">[Fort Worth Star-Telegram]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">star-telegram.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fort Worth Star-Telegram‘Roswell Incident’ UFO crash in 1947 stirred Fort Worth &#124; Fort Worth Star-TelegramJuly 5, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 5, 2024</span></span></span>

This contextual fit strengthens the plausibility of the reading after it has been obtained, but it cannot by itself establish it. Knowing that Ramey was in Fort Worth is exactly the sort of background knowledge that could encourage a motivated Roswell researcher to see the city's name in ambiguous marks. The much more useful finding is therefore not “Fort Worth makes historical sense”; it is that **atomic-prompted and blind readers also saw it**.

## What repeated recognition can and cannot prove

The Fort Worth result supports a narrower conclusion than either enthusiasts or sceptics sometimes demand from the Ramey memo. It provides empirical reason to think that the photograph contains recoverable textual information at this particular location. Houran and Randle explicitly said that unexpectedly high agreement between their participants and earlier investigators on words appearing in identical places suggested that portions of the memo were legible without computer enhancement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

That finding has three useful [limits]({{ 'limits/' | relative_url }}).

**It is evidence for a reading, not proof of every letter.** Fifty-two participants agreeing is meaningful in such a degraded image, especially across incompatible prompts, but 124 participants did not report “Fort Worth TX”. The recognition is therefore substantially stronger than a lone researcher's interpretation without becoming equivalent to reading an intact document.

**It does not validate neighbouring words.** A reliable-looking location cannot lend its certainty automatically to “victims”, “remains”, “wreck”, “disc” or any proposed continuous sentence. The study's central warning was that expectation strongly affected interpretation. A reader can be right about one locally legible fragment and still impose meaning on the marks immediately beside it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

**It does not decide the Roswell controversy.** UTA's description of the archival photograph still states that the memo has not been definitively deciphered, while later public discussion continues to acknowledge the absence of consensus over its consequential wording.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/spcouta/36080122212" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: flickr.com">[Flickr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">flickr.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Roswell UFO Incident, July 1947 &#124;...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 1947</span></span></span> The Fort Worth reading tells us something about the photograph's information content; it does not tell us whether the Roswell debris was extraterrestrial, a balloon system or something else.

That restraint actually makes the 52-reader result more useful. The value of “Fort Worth TX” is not that it unlocks a hidden message. It provides something closer to a **control point** inside an otherwise unstable decipherment problem. Readers influenced towards UFOs, readers influenced towards atomic testing and readers given no story at all repeatedly landed on the same location while disagreeing about much of the rest.

For the question of whether “Fort Worth” is genuinely present, that cross-group persistence is considerably stronger evidence than contextual plausibility alone. For the question of what the entire Ramey memo says, it is a reminder of the opposite: one unusually stable fragment cannot turn a blurred photograph into a reliable transcript.
