## Introduction

During its 1994 Roswell inquiry, the US Air Force did something important but easily overlooked in later arguments about the [Ramey memo]({{ 'ramey-memo/' | relative_url }}): it went back to first-generation photographic material rather than relying on the enlargements and reproductions circulating in UFO literature. Investigators obtained original prints from the Fort Worth *Star-Telegram* collection at the University of Texas at Arlington (UTA), subsequently obtained first-generation negatives, noticed the text-bearing sheet in Brigadier General Roger [Ramey]({{ 'ramey/' | relative_url }})'s hand, and submitted the imagery for specialist digital analysis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/decade-1990s-roswell-report-fact-vs-fiction-1994-dtic-ada326148" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">decade 1990s roswell report fact vs fiction 1994 dtic ada326148</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyRoswell Report: Fact vs Fiction, 1994, U.S. Air Force Headquarters (Office of Air Force History) · 1994 · UFO Transparenc...</span></span></span>

The result was much less dramatic than later claimed readings of the memo. The Air Force's unnamed "national-level organization" reported on 20 July 1994 that, even after digitisation, the photographs lacked sufficient quality to resolve the requested details. That finding is crucial because it separates two questions often blurred together: whether the photograph and visible sheet are genuine historical evidence, and whether the sheet's wording can reliably be recovered. The 1994 investigation treated the former seriously without claiming success on the latter.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/decade-1990s-roswell-report-fact-vs-fiction-1994-dtic-ada326148" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">decade 1990s roswell report fact vs fiction 1994 dtic ada326148</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyRoswell Report: Fact vs Fiction, 1994, U.S. Air Force Headquarters (Office of Air Force History) · 1994 · UFO Transparenc...</span></span></span>

## How investigators obtained first-generation material

The photographic work formed one small part of a much wider records investigation undertaken after Representative Steven Schiff sought an accounting of government information concerning Roswell. The Department of the Air Force Historical Research Agency describes *The Roswell Report: Fact versus Fiction in the New Mexico Desert* as the work of Colonel Richard L. Weaver and First Lieutenant James McAndrew, produced to locate records explaining the events of July 1947 and to answer the inquiry transmitted to the Air Force and General Accounting Office (GAO).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/The-DAF-Legacy-Vault/DAF-History-Publications/igphoto/2003789023/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: dafhistory.af.mil">[DAF History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">dafhistory.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">DAF History The Roswell Report: Fact versus Fiction in the New Mexico Desert</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DAF History The Roswell Report: Fact versus Fiction in the New Mexico Desert</span></span></span>

For the photographic portion, however, the investigators had access to unusually good [provenance]({{ 'provenance/' | relative_url }}). Their report says they obtained from UTA's archives a set of "original (i.e., first generation) prints" made from the photographs taken for the Fort Worth *Star-Telegram*. It adds that a set of first-generation negatives was subsequently obtained as well. These were not simply images copied out of a Roswell book or reproduced from newspaper halftones.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.scribd.com/document/831210606/Roswel-Fact-or-Fiction" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scribd.com">[Scribd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scribd.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Roswell Report: Fact vs. Fiction &#124; PDF &#124; Unidentified Flying Object</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Roswell Report: Fact vs. Fiction &#124; PDF &#124; Unidentified Flying ObjectJanuary 1, 1995...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 1995</span></span></span>

UTA's present digital archive independently anchors the photograph in that newspaper collection. Its catalogue identifies an image of Ramey holding the enlarged telegram under the Fort Worth *Star-Telegram* Collection, gives the archival date as 8 July 1947 and identifies the original format as black-and-white negatives.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mavmatrix.uta.edu/specialcollections_startelegram1940s/35502/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mavmatrix.uta.edu">[MavMatrix]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mavmatrix.uta.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mav Matrix General Ramey holding enlargement of telegram</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">General Ramey holding enlargement of telegramJuly 8, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

That provenance matters to the 1994 episode in two ways. First, the Air Force was working from archival material close to the original photographic generation. Secondly, its report explicitly incorporated observations made from those photographs into its reconstruction of the Roswell evidence. Whatever one thinks of the Air Force's broader [Project Mogul]({{ 'project-mogul/' | relative_url }}) conclusion, the Ramey photograph was not dismissed as a dubious later artefact. It was treated as historical material worth obtaining, comparing and technically examining.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/decade-1990s-roswell-report-fact-vs-fiction-1994-dtic-ada326148" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">decade 1990s roswell report fact vs fiction 1994 dtic ada326148</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyRoswell Report: Fact vs Fiction, 1994, U.S. Air Force Headquarters (Office of Air Force History) · 1994 · UFO Transparenc...</span></span></span>

There is a minor publication-history wrinkle worth keeping clear. The research was conducted in 1994 and the Air Force says its exhaustive records search concluded in July of that year, while the government publication preserved by GovInfo is dated 1995. The Department of the Air Force's historical archive explains that the published volume duplicates the material supplied to the Secretary of the Air Force and GAO, with a photograph section appended to the book.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.af.mil/The-Roswell-Report/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: af.mil">[af.mil]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Air Force The Roswell Report</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Air Force The Roswell Report</span></span></span>

## Why the paper in Ramey's hand drew official analysis

The Air Force's examination was not initially framed as an attempt to prove or disprove a hidden confession about alien bodies. Its stated purpose was more practical. Investigators noticed that Ramey was holding a piece of paper in the photographs: in one view it was folded so that nothing useful could be seen, while in another there appeared to be printed text. They hoped that reading it might provide another lead to documents relevant to the Roswell records search.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.scribd.com/document/831210606/Roswel-Fact-or-Fiction" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scribd.com">[Scribd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scribd.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Roswell Report: Fact vs. Fiction &#124; PDF &#124; Unidentified Flying Object</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Roswell Report: Fact vs. Fiction &#124; PDF &#124; Unidentified Flying ObjectJanuary 1, 1995...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 1995</span></span></span>

This is a significant detail in assessing the evidential status of the "Ramey memo". The Air Force itself recognised that the marks on the sheet represented potentially recoverable text and considered that text potentially relevant to its historical investigation. In other words, the possibility of extracting information from the document was not solely an invention of later UFO researchers.

The photographs were also being used for a second, related visual question. Witness descriptions had referred to tape bearing flower-like or "hieroglyphic" designs on the recovered material. The specialist organisation was therefore asked both to investigate the writing on Ramey's paper and to inspect the digitised photographs for evidence of the disputed patterned tape.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/decade-1990s-roswell-report-fact-vs-fiction-1994-dtic-ada326148" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">decade 1990s roswell report fact vs fiction 1994 dtic ada326148</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyRoswell Report: Fact vs Fiction, 1994, U.S. Air Force Headquarters (Office of Air Force History) · 1994 · UFO Transparenc...</span></span></span>

The official conclusion covered both tasks. According to the Air Force report, the organisation reported on 20 July 1994 that, after digitisation, the photographs were of insufficient quality to visualise either detail sought in the analysis. The published account therefore did not offer a transcription of the Ramey paper—not even a tentative one—and did not claim that image enhancement had established what the document said.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/decade-1990s-roswell-report-fact-vs-fiction-1994-dtic-ada326148" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">decade 1990s roswell report fact vs fiction 1994 dtic ada326148</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyRoswell Report: Fact vs Fiction, 1994, U.S. Air Force Headquarters (Office of Air Force History) · 1994 · UFO Transparenc...</span></span></span>

That negative result is narrower than it can sound. "Insufficient quality" does not mean that no writing exists on the sheet; the investigators had already observed apparent text. Nor does it mean that the photograph was considered inauthentic. It means that the available photographic information did not permit the specialist examination to recover the requested details with sufficient confidence.

## What the 1994 analysis can and cannot establish

The strongest reading of the Air Force episode is therefore a deliberately limited one.

**It supports the photographic provenance.** The Air Force researchers went to UTA, obtained first-generation *Star-Telegram* prints and negatives, and analysed the photographs as evidence from the 1947 event. UTA continues to catalogue the relevant material as original black-and-white negatives in the newspaper's archival collection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/decade-1990s-roswell-report-fact-vs-fiction-1994-dtic-ada326148" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">decade 1990s roswell report fact vs fiction 1994 dtic ada326148</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyRoswell Report: Fact vs Fiction, 1994, U.S. Air Force Headquarters (Office of Air Force History) · 1994 · UFO Transparenc...</span></span></span>

**It confirms official awareness of the visible document.** Weaver and McAndrew explicitly recorded the sheet in Ramey's hand and the appearance of text on it. The Air Force consequently regarded the photograph as potentially capable of leading investigators towards further documentary evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.scribd.com/document/831210606/Roswel-Fact-or-Fiction" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: scribd.com">[Scribd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">scribd.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Roswell Report: Fact vs. Fiction &#124; PDF &#124; Unidentified Flying Object</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Roswell Report: Fact vs. Fiction &#124; PDF &#124; Unidentified Flying ObjectJanuary 1, 1995...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 1995</span></span></span>

**It does not authenticate any proposed transcription.** The specialist analysis reported no readable message. Consequently, phrases later attributed to the photograph—especially disputed readings involving a "disc", "victims" or a "wreck"—cannot be presented as findings of the 1994 Air Force examination. They arose from subsequent private analyses and remain contested. A 2002 study by James Houran and Kevin Randle noted substantial disagreement between interpreters and found that suggestion and expectations could influence attempts to decipher the ambiguous image.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

**It does not demonstrate that the paper was an official military message.** The photograph proves that Ramey was holding a text-bearing sheet. The 1994 analysis did not recover enough information to establish its sender, recipient, document type or complete contents. UTA's current catalogue uses the description "telegram", but that archival description should not be confused with a successful forensic reading of the photographed text.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://mavmatrix.uta.edu/specialcollections_startelegram1940s/35502/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: mavmatrix.uta.edu">[MavMatrix]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">mavmatrix.uta.edu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mav Matrix General Ramey holding enlargement of telegram</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">General Ramey holding enlargement of telegramJuly 8, 1947...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 8, 1947</span></span></span>

This distinction is particularly important in discussions of [authenticity]({{ 'authenticity/' | relative_url }}). "The Ramey memo is authentic" can mean several different things. There is strong archival evidence that the photograph itself is genuine and that the sheet really was present in Ramey's hand in 1947. That does not automatically authenticate a modern transcription of its contents.

## Why the failure to read it remains informative

At first sight, an unsuccessful photo analysis might seem to add very little. In fact, it establishes a useful evidential baseline. By July 1994, Air Force investigators had access to first-generation material, had recognised the possible importance of the writing and had subjected the imagery to digitisation and specialist interpretation. Their documented result was not a hidden message but an image-resolution limit.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/decade-1990s-roswell-report-fact-vs-fiction-1994-dtic-ada326148" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">decade 1990s roswell report fact vs fiction 1994 dtic ada326148</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyRoswell Report: Fact vs Fiction, 1994, U.S. Air Force Headquarters (Office of Air Force History) · 1994 · UFO Transparenc...</span></span></span>

Later claims therefore have a clear hurdle to clear. Anyone claiming to recover substantially more text must explain why their source image, scanning method or analytical procedure extracts reliable information that the 1994 examination could not. Simply enlarging a photograph does not create spatial information absent from the underlying negative, while enhancement can make existing contrasts easier to perceive without necessarily making ambiguous character shapes objectively identifiable.

Later research illustrates the problem. Houran and Randle's 2002 study observed that some portions of the document may indeed be legible: participants showed notable agreement on certain words and locations. But the authors also found that interpretation of the document was influenced by suggestion, and they warned that the broader meaning remained ambiguous. Their work is especially relevant to the 1994 Air Force result because it shows that "some visual patterns can be read" and "a defensible continuous transcription exists" are not equivalent propositions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

The same paper records a history of sharply differing attempted readings. Investigators using computer equipment and improved scans did not converge on one complete text; some rejected words or phrases proposed by others. One specialist quoted in the study concluded that interpreters were deriving readings that the image did not adequately support.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

That later disagreement makes the Air Force's restrained 1994 conclusion more consequential, not less. Its failure does not prove that every character is forever unrecoverable, but it cautions against treating a visually plausible phrase as though it were equivalent to ordinary document transcription.

## The unnamed analysis organisation

One weakness in the published Air Force account is its lack of transparency about who performed the technical work. Weaver and McAndrew referred only to a "national-level organization". The report provides the date of its conclusion and describes the requested tasks, but does not identify the organisation in the passage reporting the analysis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/decade-1990s-roswell-report-fact-vs-fiction-1994-dtic-ada326148" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">decade 1990s roswell report fact vs fiction 1994 dtic ada326148</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyRoswell Report: Fact vs Fiction, 1994, U.S. Air Force Headquarters (Office of Air Force History) · 1994 · UFO Transparenc...</span></span></span>

That omission subsequently became part of the controversy. Roswell researcher Kevin Randle reported in 2016 that he later asked Weaver directly and that Weaver identified the organisation as the National Photographic Interpretation Center (NPIC), then associated with the Central Intelligence Agency. Randle also published an account of subsequent Freedom of Information requests seeking documentation of the examination. This identification is therefore a later report attributed to Weaver rather than information stated in the original Air Force publication, and it should be labelled accordingly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2016/05/the-cia-and-ramey-memo.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: The CIA and the Ramey Memo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: The CIA and the Ramey Memo</span></span></span>

The distinction matters when evaluating the 1994 exercise. The official report establishes that an outside national-level organisation performed digitisation and photo interpretation and returned its conclusion on 20 July. The publicly available report does not, by itself, provide the technical parameters needed to reproduce the analysis: scanner resolution, exact source negative or print used for each operation, processing sequence, software, analyst observations and intermediate imagery are not laid out alongside the conclusion.

That makes the result useful but not equivalent to a modern reproducible forensic-imaging study. Readers can know what the Air Force says it asked and what answer it received; they cannot reconstruct the complete analytical workflow from that passage alone.

## Authentic photograph, unresolved message

The central lesson of the 1994 Air Force examination is the gap between **provenance** and **legibility**. On provenance, the episode strengthens an already strong chain: Air Force investigators obtained first-generation *Star-Telegram* material directly from UTA's archive, examined it as part of their official Roswell inquiry and recognised the paper in Ramey's hand as a potentially relevant document. UTA still preserves and catalogues the underlying photographic material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/decade-1990s-roswell-report-fact-vs-fiction-1994-dtic-ada326148" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">decade 1990s roswell report fact vs fiction 1994 dtic ada326148</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyRoswell Report: Fact vs Fiction, 1994, U.S. Air Force Headquarters (Office of Air Force History) · 1994 · UFO Transparenc...</span></span></span>

On legibility, the result goes the other way. The specialist organisation reported that digitisation did not produce sufficient image quality to resolve the requested details. The Air Force therefore supplied no authoritative transcription in its Roswell report.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/decade-1990s-roswell-report-fact-vs-fiction-1994-dtic-ada326148" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">decade 1990s roswell report fact vs fiction 1994 dtic ada326148</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyRoswell Report: Fact vs Fiction, 1994, U.S. Air Force Headquarters (Office of Air Force History) · 1994 · UFO Transparenc...</span></span></span>

Later investigators have argued that improved scans and processing reveal individual words or longer passages, while others have challenged those readings. The experimental work published by Houran and Randle offers a reason for caution: observers can sometimes agree on portions of an extremely degraded document while suggestion and expectation still influence what they perceive elsewhere.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

For the Ramey memo authenticity debate, that is the most defensible interpretation of the 1994 event. The Air Force's inquiry helps establish that the photographed sheet is part of genuine 1947 photographic evidence and was considered worthy of official technical examination. It does **not** establish the wording now attributed to it. The historical image is considerably more secure than any complete reading of the text it contains.
