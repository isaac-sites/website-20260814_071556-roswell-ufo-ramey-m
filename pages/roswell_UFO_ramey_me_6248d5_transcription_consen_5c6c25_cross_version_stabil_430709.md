## Introduction

A claimed [Ramey memo]({{ 'ramey-memo/' | relative_url }}) word should count as strong evidence only if its decisive letter shapes survive across legitimate versions of the photograph. That means the same strokes should remain recognisable in different high-quality scans and in several conservative renderings of those scans—not appear only after one particular contrast setting, geometric correction or aggressive [enhancement]({{ 'enhancement/' | relative_url }}). This is especially important because researchers have not worked from one immutable digital image. The published Ramey literature includes enlargements, negative scans and several high-resolution image files, while later work on the original negative produced dozens of additional captures under different conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>



<figure class="article-media-figure article-media-figure-illustration"><img src="{{ "/assets/images/roswell_UFO_ramey_me_6248d5_transcription_consen_5c6c25_cross_version_stabil_430709-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Version Stability illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/roswell_UFO_ramey_me_6248d5_transcription_consen_5c6c25_cross_version_stabil_430709-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/roswell_UFO_ramey_me_6248d5_transcription_consen_5c6c25_cross_version_stabil_430709-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" width="1920" height="1080" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low"><figcaption class="article-media-caption"><span class="article-media-caption-label">Explanatory illustration 1</span></figcaption></figure>

The evidence therefore supports a distinction between **version-stable features** and **enhancement-dependent readings**. A word that retains the same identifying strokes across independent source versions is much more persuasive than one whose crucial letter appears only in a favoured processed image. For the disputed [Ramey]({{ 'ramey/' | relative_url }}) text, that test is demanding—and the most consequential claimed readings have not yet cleared it convincingly.

## What cross-version stability actually requires

The Ramey memo is not simply a low-resolution photograph waiting for enough magnification. The text occupies a small, oblique and partly folded area of a 1947 photographic negative. Some character information was never captured clearly in the first place, while film grain, focus, perspective, folds and subsequent damage complicate what remains. Kevin Randle's account of the 2015 examination says that investigators made dozens of new scans under varying conditions but still encountered letters lost amid negative defects and areas where the folded paper was not facing the camera directly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/09/ramey-memo-update.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: Ramey Memo Update</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: Ramey Memo Update</span></span></span>

There were already multiple source versions before that work. James Houran and Kevin Randle's 2002 study describes four high-resolution images supplied on Stanton Friedman's CD-ROM: a negative image of the memo portion, an enlargement made by the University of Texas at Arlington (UTA), an older 8-by-10 print produced before additional handling of the negative, and an 11-by-14 version made in 1998. Participants also had an enlarged print obtained from the Fort Worth Star-Telegram Photograph Collection at UTA.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

That diversity is useful because it creates a natural robustness test. Suppose an analyst proposes that a faint group of pixels is the upper loop and descending stem of a particular letter. The important question is not merely whether that interpretation fits one image. The analyst should be able to locate corresponding photographic information in the other good versions.

A rigorous version-stability test would therefore ask four related questions:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Position:** Does the proposed character occur at the same physical location on the memo after the versions are geometrically registered?
* **Structure:** Do the strokes that distinguish it from competing letters persist, rather than merely the overall dark patch?
* **Independence:** Does the feature survive in images derived through genuinely different capture or processing paths, rather than several copies of the same enhanced file?
* **Tolerance:** Does the reading remain plausible when contrast, sharpening and noise reduction are varied conservatively?

</div>

Passing those tests would not prove that the resulting word is correct. It would establish something narrower but essential: the proposed characters are being driven by persistent photographic information rather than by one processing choice.

## Which character features should remain visible

Cross-version comparison needs to operate below the level of whole words. Asking whether several images all “look like VICTIMS”, for example, already supplies the observer with a powerful linguistic template. The more diagnostic test is whether the particular strokes needed to distinguish the proposed letters survive.

That distinction matters because the published transcriptions diverge sharply once analysts move away from relatively conspicuous fragments. Houran and Randle reproduced competing readings from several investigators. In the same positions, analysts could propose substantially different sequences even though they were looking at the same underlying memo. Yet some fragments converged much better: several transcriptions placed “AT FORT WORTH, TEX.” in the same location, while multiple readers identified “DISC” or “DISK” on another line.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://noufors.com/Documents/Books%2C%20Manuals%20and%20Published%20Papers/Specialty%20UFO%20Publications/Journal%20of%20Scientific%20Exploration/jse_16_1_houran.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: noufors.com">[Noufors]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">noufors.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Noufors&#x27;&#x27;A Message in a Bottle:&quot; Confounds in Deciphering the Ramey Memo from the Roswell UFO CaseMay 23, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 23, 2026</span></span></span>

Barry Greenwood later made essentially the same distinction. His examination accepted “AT FORT WORTH, TEX.” and the quoted “DISC” as comparatively persuasive while expressing considerable doubt about decipherment of the remainder. His importance to a version-stability discussion is precisely that he did not simply accept an entire Roswell-oriented transcription because a few portions appeared readable.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fr.scribd.com/document/314034746/2004-I-11-An-Observation-on-the-Ramey-Memo-ed-Barry-Greenwood-pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fr.scribd.com">[Scribd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fr.scribd.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Analysis of the Ramey Memo &#124; PDF &#124; Travel &#124; Young Adult</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Analysis of the Ramey Memo &#124; PDF &#124; Travel &#124; Young AdultMarch 1, 2004...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 1, 2004</span></span></span>

Those comparatively stable fragments illustrate what a stronger candidate looks like. Their value comes from recognisable structure in a fixed location combined with agreement across analysts and image treatments. Even here, “agreement” should not be inflated into certainty: analysts may have seen previous transcriptions, and agreement between researchers is not the same thing as independently demonstrating every letter from raw pixels.

The threshold should rise dramatically for consequential readings such as “VICTIMS”. A claim of that importance depends not simply on finding approximately word-shaped darkness of the correct length. Its diagnostic strokes need to discriminate *VICTIMS* from plausible alternatives. If the feature interpreted as part of a **V**, **C**, **T** or **M** changes materially between source versions, disappears under modest processing changes or requires reconstruction to become recognisable, the evidential status of the whole word falls with it.

This is why a useful transcription should ideally carry [confidence]({{ 'confidence/' | relative_url }}) at character level. A sequence might properly be recorded as something like a secure three-letter cluster surrounded by uncertain characters rather than being promoted immediately to the most contextually attractive complete word. The Ramey photograph does not oblige an analyst to choose a dictionary word where the image preserves only fragments.

## The scans are not interchangeable evidence

Calling several files “different images” can exaggerate their independence. Two differently contrasted exports made from one scan are not equivalent to two independent captures of the negative. They contain the same underlying sampled information, transformed in different ways. Genuine cross-version confirmation is strongest when the comparison moves back towards independently acquired source material.

The history of the Ramey images provides several such levels. Earlier investigators used photographic enlargements and scans; the 2002 experiment incorporated four high-resolution versions of differing provenance; and the 2015 project returned to the negative and generated new digital material using modern equipment and varied capture conditions. Randle subsequently published examples produced with different contrasts and filters and noted that some of the working files were extremely large.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span>

That hierarchy matters. If a disputed stroke occurs in an old enlargement, a high-resolution scan and a later independent capture of the original negative, its persistence is meaningful. If it appears in five Photoshop treatments derived from one file, those five appearances provide much less independent confirmation.

The provenance of the 2015 material is therefore particularly important. Randle reported that the University of Texas at Arlington regarded the negatives in its collection as the originals received from the Fort Worth Star-Telegram, and the examination included viewing them under magnification and on a light table.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2016/05/the-ramey-memo-negative.html?showComment=1462541425906" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: The Ramey Memo Negative</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: The Ramey Memo Negative</span></span></span> This does not make any proposed transcription correct, but it makes comparisons against those captures more informative than comparisons among repeatedly processed web images.

There is another complication: different legitimate versions need not look identical. A scratch introduced after an older print was made can appear in a later negative scan but not in the earlier print. Different exposure or digitisation settings can make weak tonal information easier or harder to see. Perspective correction can move pixels substantially even without inventing them. Version stability therefore means preservation of the **underlying character geometry**, not pixel-for-pixel identity.

## When one enhancement becomes too much evidence

Enhancement is legitimate when it exposes information already present in the image. The danger begins when the processing operation becomes inseparable from the claimed letter.

The later Ramey work illustrates how extensive these transformations can become. David Rudiak described processing that included rotation, cropping, vertical stretching, lightening and more elaborate attempts to flatten the memo, correct perspective distortion, regularise letter dimensions, straighten lines and compensate for folds. He also criticised low-resolution processing because reducing the available detail necessarily introduces artefacts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2018/12/david-rudiaks-latest-on-ramey-memo.html?showComment=1546118226661" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: David Rudiak&#x27;s Latest on the Ramey MemoDecember 19, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 19, 2018</span></span></span>

Some of those operations have perfectly defensible purposes. Rotating an image does not inherently create a word, and correcting the memo's perspective can make comparison easier. But every additional transformation introduces assumptions. Straightening a folded sheet requires assumptions about its original geometry. Rescaling alters the appearance of strokes. Noise suppression can erase weak real features or preserve grain structures that resemble typography. Sharpening can turn gradual tonal transitions into apparently crisp edges.

Consequently, an enhancement should be treated as a **view of the evidence**, not as new evidence itself. A persuasive workflow would retain the untouched source scan, document every operation and show the proposed characters beside several minimally processed versions. The decisive strokes should be traceable backwards through that chain.

This produces a useful practical rule: **the more interpretive processing a word needs, the more independent source versions it should have to survive**. A word visible in several raw or lightly corrected scans requires comparatively little argument about the enhancement. A word visible only after one complex workflow carries the burden of showing that the workflow has not manufactured its defining features.

The 2015 scans are revealing in this respect. They were explicitly produced under multiple conditions in the hope that better technology might yield enough information for [consensus]({{ 'consensus/' | relative_url }}), yet the work did not simply resolve the document into clean typography. Randle acknowledged persistent loss of character information, and later accounts continued to describe the overall transcription as unresolved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/09/ramey-memo-update.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: Ramey Memo Update</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: Ramey Memo Update</span></span></span>

## Stable fragments do not validate unstable sentences

One of the easiest mistakes in evaluating the Ramey memo is to transfer confidence from its clearest fragments to its weakest ones. If “FORT WORTH, TEX.” looks persuasive, that demonstrates that *some* photographic text can survive. It does not demonstrate that every neighbouring grey patch can be decoded with comparable reliability.

The published comparison of transcriptions makes this particularly clear. “AT FORT WORTH, TEX.” repeatedly appears in approximately the same location, whereas other lines produced dramatically different proposed wording. Line seven, for example, generated readings containing “WEATHER BALLOONS” but with sharply divergent surrounding language. Line four produced agreement around “DISC” or “DISK” while the rest of the line varied substantially.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://noufors.com/Documents/Books%2C%20Manuals%20and%20Published%20Papers/Specialty%20UFO%20Publications/Journal%20of%20Scientific%20Exploration/jse_16_1_houran.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: noufors.com">[Noufors]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">noufors.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Noufors&#x27;&#x27;A Message in a Bottle:&quot; Confounds in Deciphering the Ramey Memo from the Roswell UFO CaseMay 23, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 23, 2026</span></span></span>

That pattern is exactly what would be expected from an image containing an uneven mixture of usable and unusable information. Some character groups rise sufficiently above the photographic noise to constrain interpretation; others do not. It would therefore be methodologically unsound to insist on either extreme—that the entire memo is readable because several fragments appear stable, or that nothing in it can be read because much of it remains ambiguous.

The official Air Force examination provides a useful counterweight to expansive readings. During its Roswell investigation, a national-level organisation was asked to digitise and examine photographs for the memo text and other fine details. According to the Air Force account, the organisation reported in July 1994 that the photographs supplied were of insufficient quality to visualise the requested details.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ramey-memo.com/national-level-organization-failed/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ramey-memo.com">[Ramey Memo]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ramey-memo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ramey Memo National-Level Organization FAILED! – Unlocking The Mystery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ramey Memo National-Level Organization FAILED! – Unlocking The Mystery</span></span></span> Later civilian researchers had access to other and better image material, so the 1994 result cannot by itself settle what can be recovered from the best surviving negative. It does, however, underline how close the problem lies to the information limit of the photograph.

## The strongest test is deliberately unfriendly to the preferred reading

A proper version-stability experiment should be designed to make a false word disappear. That means starting with the best available source captures, registering them independently and asking analysts to identify character features without first being shown a complete proposed transcription.

This safeguard is particularly important because Houran and Randle experimentally demonstrated contextual influence in Ramey-memo reading. Their 176 participants were divided among Roswell, atomic-testing and no-context conditions. People given contextual suggestions reported more readable material, and their proposed words tended to reflect the supplied story.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228706129_A_Message_in_a_Bottle_Confounds_in_Deciphering_the_Ramey_Memo_from_the_Roswell_UFO_Case" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: researchgate.net">[ResearchGate]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">researchgate.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on researchgate.net.</span></span></span> Version testing cannot remove that psychological problem if the analyst already knows which word an enhancement is supposed to reveal.

A strong protocol would therefore preserve separate source images, disclose all processing steps and require the reader to mark individual strokes before seeing candidate words. Only afterwards would the results be compared across versions. Where one version supports an identifying stroke and another equally good version does not, that discrepancy should remain visible in the final assessment rather than being reconciled by choosing whichever rendering best supports the preferred transcription.

The outcome need not be binary. A fragment can be classified as highly stable, moderately stable, enhancement-sensitive or unsupported. That approach fits the Ramey data better than attempting to force one confidence rating onto the entire memo.

## What version stability currently allows us to say

The surviving evidence supports a limited but important conclusion. The Ramey photograph appears to contain **some textual features robust enough to attract repeated readings**, with “AT FORT WORTH, TEX.” among the strongest examples and “DISC” or “DISK” receiving substantial agreement among several investigators.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fr.scribd.com/document/314034746/2004-I-11-An-Observation-on-the-Ramey-Memo-ed-Barry-Greenwood-pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fr.scribd.com">[Scribd]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fr.scribd.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Analysis of the Ramey Memo &#124; PDF &#124; Travel &#124; Young Adult</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Analysis of the Ramey Memo &#124; PDF &#124; Travel &#124; Young AdultMarch 1, 2004...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 1, 2004</span></span></span> Those fragments demonstrate that the version-stability question is worth asking rather than dismissing the photograph as uniformly featureless.

But the same record does not establish comparable stability for every dramatic proposed phrase. The multiple transcriptions published by Houran and Randle show substantial disagreement at precisely the points where the image becomes ambiguous, while their experiment demonstrates how easily expectation can influence what observers recover from those ambiguous forms.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://noufors.com/Documents/Books%2C%20Manuals%20and%20Published%20Papers/Specialty%20UFO%20Publications/Journal%20of%20Scientific%20Exploration/jse_16_1_houran.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: noufors.com">[Noufors]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">noufors.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Noufors&#x27;&#x27;A Message in a Bottle:&quot; Confounds in Deciphering the Ramey Memo from the Roswell UFO CaseMay 23, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 23, 2026</span></span></span> Later rescanning of the original negative produced much more material for comparison but did not yield an uncontested complete transcription.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/09/ramey-memo-update.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: Ramey Memo Update</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog A Different Perspective: Ramey Memo Update</span></span></span>

That leaves a straightforward evidential standard for any proposed Ramey word. Its crucial strokes should be identifiable in the same places in multiple high-quality source versions; they should tolerate conservative changes in contrast and geometry; and they should remain recognisable when analysts are not told what word to expect. Features that disappear when the favoured enhancement is removed should lose evidential weight accordingly.

For the wider question of consensus standards, this is the central lesson of version stability: **a convincing Ramey word should belong to the photograph, not to one rendering of the photograph**.
