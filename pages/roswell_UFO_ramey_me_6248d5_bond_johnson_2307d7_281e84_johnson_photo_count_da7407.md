## Introduction

J. [Bond Johnson]({{ 'johnson/' | relative_url }})’s memory of how many Roswell photographs he took did not remain fixed. In an early recorded account, the *Fort Worth Star-Telegram* reporter-photographer remembered going to Brigadier General Roger Ramey’s office with just one double-sided 4-by-5 film holder and making two exposures. By December 1990, according to interviewer Kevin Randle, Johnson was saying that he had carried two holders, enough for four sheets of film. Later still, Johnson accepted a six-exposure reconstruction involving three holders and photographs of Ramey, Colonel Thomas DuBose and Major [Jesse Marcel]({{ 'marcel/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2009/10/ufos-ramey-memo-j-bond-johnson-and-me.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: UFOs, the Ramey Memo, J. Bond Johnson and MeOctober 9, 2009...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 9, 2009</span></span></span>

That progression matters because the surviving Fort Worth photographs provide an unusual check on retrospective testimony. Johnson unquestionably photographed the scene; the dispute is narrower. As additional images were located and attributed, his remembered inventory expanded to accommodate them. A modern *Star-Telegram* retrospective now describes the assignment simply as a six-frame shoot.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.star-telegram.com/opinion/bud-kennedy/article289713499.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: star-telegram.com">[Fort Worth Star-Telegram]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">star-telegram.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fort Worth Star-Telegram‘Roswell Incident’ UFO crash in 1947 stirred Fort Worth &#124; Fort Worth Star-TelegramJuly 5, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 5, 2024</span></span></span> The episode therefore offers a useful warning for the [Ramey memo]({{ 'ramey-memo/' | relative_url }}) debate: Johnson’s recollections can illuminate the assignment, but precise details reconstructed decades later should not automatically outrank the photographic and archival record.

## The original two-exposure recollection

When Randle interviewed Johnson decades after the 1947 assignment, Johnson initially described a remarkably small photographic operation. Asked whether he had taken only one picture, Johnson replied that he had taken two essentially duplicate shots and had brought only one film holder. Elsewhere in the same account he was explicit that this meant two pieces of 4-by-5 black-and-white film.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2009/10/ufos-ramey-memo-j-bond-johnson-and-me.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: UFOs, the Ramey Memo, J. Bond Johnson and MeOctober 9, 2009...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 9, 2009</span></span></span>

That description made technical sense on its own terms. Johnson was using a 4-by-5 Speed Graphic, the standard sort of press camera in which individual sheets of film were carried in holders rather than advanced through the camera as a roll. A conventional double-sided holder allowed a photographer to expose one sheet, reverse the holder and expose the second. Thus Johnson’s remembered equation was straightforward: one holder, two sheets, two photographs. His recollection that he owned and carried a 4-by-5 Speed Graphic is independently repeated in later accounts of the assignment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/11/j-bond-johnsons-statement-on-his-visit.html?showComment=1447183915509" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: J. Bond Johnson&#x27;s Statement on His Visit to General RameyNovember 9, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 9, 2015</span></span></span>

The difficulty was not the mechanics. It was the photographic record.

Johnson initially spoke as though the assignment he remembered centred on [Ramey]({{ 'ramey/' | relative_url }}). During his conversation with Randle, he appeared surprised when told that photographs also existed of Jesse Marcel with the wreckage. Randle told him there was a photograph of Marcel; Johnson asked whether he was sure it was not Ramey.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2009/10/ufos-ramey-memo-j-bond-johnson-and-me.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: UFOs, the Ramey Memo, J. Bond Johnson and MeOctober 9, 2009...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 9, 2009</span></span></span> That exchange is particularly valuable because it captures Johnson before the later six-frame narrative had fully crystallised.

It also makes the change difficult to dismiss as a trivial verbal slip. Remembering one holder rather than three is not merely forgetting whether a shutter was pressed five or six times. The holder count was Johnson’s own explanation for why he believed he had made only two exposures. Once more photographs entered the discussion, both the number of pictures and the remembered amount of film equipment had to change.

Randle later inserted an important qualification into his published transcript: at the time of the early discussion, neither he nor Johnson realised how many photographs were involved. Randle says subsequent investigation led him to conclude that Johnson had actually used three holders and made six exposures.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2009/10/ufos-ramey-memo-j-bond-johnson-and-me.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: UFOs, the Ramey Memo, J. Bond Johnson and MeOctober 9, 2009...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 9, 2009</span></span></span> That acknowledgement is useful because it separates the original recollection from the reconstruction that followed.

## How newly identified images complicated the count

The Fort Worth material did not present researchers with a neat packet labelled as Johnson’s complete six-frame sequence. Images emerged through newspaper copies, photographic archives and later examination of the *Star-Telegram* collection. Randle has described being directed to the University of Texas at Arlington after looking for contemporary photographs and finding additional pictures there involving Ramey, DuBose and Marcel.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://doczz.net/doc/1899347/roswell--ufos-and-the-unusual" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: doczz.net">[doczz.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">doczz.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Roswell, UFOs and the Unusual</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">air force...</span></span></span>

This expanding evidence base created several competing ways of counting the assignment.

One reconstruction attributes six photographs to Johnson: two of Ramey alone, two of Ramey with DuBose and two of Marcel. Randle has argued that the visual pattern of the photographs fits precisely that three-pose, two-exposure structure, while distinguishing a later photograph of weather officer Irving [Newton]({{ 'newton/' | relative_url }}) as the work of another photographer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2010/07/?m=0" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: July 2010...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 2010</span></span></span> A specialist account associated with the Roswell Photo Interpretation Team similarly reported that Johnson eventually believed he had taken six photographs, explaining the total as three double-sided film holders used to make two exposures of each pose.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://artpike.fortunecity.ws/ramey0.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: artpike.fortunecity.ws">[ArtPike]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">artpike.fortunecity.ws</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Art Pike The Fort Worth Pictures taken by James Bond Johnson</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Fort Worth Pictures taken by James Bond Johnson.September 23, 1998...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 23, 1998</span></span></span>

There is also contemporary institutional continuity behind the six-frame attribution. A 2024 *Fort Worth Star-Telegram* retrospective states that Johnson “shot six frames” of Ramey and other officers, and it reproduces the Marcel photograph with a credit to the J. Bond Johnson/*Fort Worth Star-Telegram* Collection at UT Arlington.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.star-telegram.com/opinion/bud-kennedy/article289713499.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: star-telegram.com">[Fort Worth Star-Telegram]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">star-telegram.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fort Worth Star-Telegram‘Roswell Incident’ UFO crash in 1947 stirred Fort Worth &#124; Fort Worth Star-TelegramJuly 5, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 5, 2024</span></span></span> Johnson’s 2006 obituary likewise described a series of six photographs and said that five survived in the University of Texas at Arlington collection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.legacy.com/us/obituaries/dfw/name/j-johnson-obituary?id=11842901" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: legacy.com">[Legacy.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">legacy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">j johnson obituary</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">J. Johnson Obituary (1926 - 2006) - Long Beach, Calif., TX - Star-TelegramApril 2, 2006...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 2, 2006</span></span></span>

Yet the literature has never been completely uniform. Some Roswell chronologies attribute only four Fort Worth pictures to Johnson — two of Ramey and two of Ramey with DuBose — while treating the Marcel images as photographs by someone else.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/rw/g/pressconf.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOS at close sight: Roswell 1947, photographs, 1947 Press conference photographs...</span></span></span> The disagreement is important because it shows why simply saying that “six Roswell photographs exist” does not by itself settle authorship.

Indeed, researchers who worked with Johnson later disputed whether he could have taken the Marcel photographs. Randle records that members of the Roswell Photo Interpretation Team used the apparent sunlight and shadows visible through Ramey’s office curtains to estimate an earlier time for the Marcel images. Their argument was that, if that photographic timing calculation were correct, Johnson could not yet have been present. Randle himself questioned how securely that estimate had been derived and argued that other evidence favoured Johnson as the photographer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/11/the-timing-of-roswell-photographs.html?showComment=1447631999173" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on blogspot.com.</span></span></span>

The safest conclusion is therefore slightly more restrained than either extreme. The later documentary tradition strongly supports a six-frame Johnson assignment, and the *Star-Telegram* itself now uses that figure. But the route from Johnson’s original two-exposure memory to six was not a continuous personal recollection. It developed alongside the identification, attribution and interpretation of additional photographs.

## The count changed in stages

The evolution is easier to see as a sequence rather than as a single contradiction:

* **Early recollection:** Johnson told Randle that he had one holder containing two sheets and had made two essentially duplicate exposures, principally remembering Ramey as his subject.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2009/10/ufos-ramey-memo-j-bond-johnson-and-me.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: UFOs, the Ramey Memo, J. * **December 1990:** Randle reports that Johnson subsequently said he had carried two holders and four pieces of film.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2009/10/ufos-ramey-memo-j-bond-johnson-and-me.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: UFOs, the Ramey Memo, J. * **Later reconstruction:** Johnson came to remember or accept three poses — Ramey, Ramey with DuBose and Marcel — with two exposures of each, yielding six frames from three holders.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2009/10/ufos-ramey-memo-j-bond-johnson-and-me.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: UFOs, the Ramey Memo, J. * **Later published consensus:** Johnson’s obituary and the *Star-Telegram* retrospective both describe a six-photograph series, although accounts differ over exactly how many original negatives survive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.legacy.com/us/obituaries/dfw/name/j-johnson-obituary?id=11842901" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: legacy.com">[Legacy.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">legacy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">j johnson obituary</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">J. A late-1990s account said that four original negatives were then held by UT Arlington, representing all three proposed poses: two Marcel images, one Ramey image and one Ramey-DuBose image.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://artpike.fortunecity.ws/ramey0.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: artpike.fortunecity.ws">[ArtPike]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">artpike.fortunecity.ws</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Art Pike The Fort Worth Pictures taken by James Bond Johnson</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Fort Worth Pictures taken by James Bond Johnson.September 23, 1998...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 23, 1998</span></span></span> Johnson’s obituary instead stated that five of six photographs survived in the university collection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.legacy.com/us/obituaries/dfw/name/j-johnson-obituary?id=11842901" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: legacy.com">[Legacy.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">legacy.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">j johnson obituary</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">J. Confusing them can create an artificial contradiction. What is genuinely significant is that Johnson’s own starting memory — two sheets in one holder — could not accommodate the broader photographic record later associated with his assignment.

## Why changing film-holder memories matter

A witness forgetting an exposure count after more than forty years would normally be unsurprising. Johnson was recalling a newspaper assignment from 1947 in interviews beginning decades later, and at the time the photographs were taken there was no reason for a young reporter to assume that historians would eventually scrutinise the contents of his camera bag. The change from two to four to six should therefore not be treated as evidence that Johnson deliberately fabricated the entire [story]({{ 'story/' | relative_url }}).

But neither should the progression be ignored. The strength of this particular discrepancy is that the remembered number of film holders was a concrete logistical detail. Johnson did not merely say, in effect, “I cannot remember how many photographs I took.” He initially supplied a physical constraint — one holder with two sheets — that explained and reinforced his two-exposure recollection. Randle's published account then records a later two-holder version before the three-holder, six-frame reconstruction emerged.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2009/10/ufos-ramey-memo-j-bond-johnson-and-me.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Kevin Randle&#x27;s BlogA Different Perspective: UFOs, the Ramey Memo, J. Bond Johnson and MeOctober 9, 2009...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 9, 2009</span></span></span>

That pattern is consistent with retrospective reconstruction: old memories being revised as a witness encounters photographs, researchers’ questions and other information unavailable or forgotten when the first account was given. It does not prove that every later Johnson recollection was wrong. In fact, the six-frame reconstruction may be *more* historically accurate than his original two-frame memory because it is better aligned with the surviving photographic corpus and later *Star-Telegram* attribution.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.star-telegram.com/opinion/bud-kennedy/article289713499.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: star-telegram.com">[Fort Worth Star-Telegram]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">star-telegram.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fort Worth Star-Telegram‘Roswell Incident’ UFO crash in 1947 stirred Fort Worth &#124; Fort Worth Star-TelegramJuly 5, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 5, 2024</span></span></span> The important distinction is between **accuracy produced by corroboration** and **accuracy demonstrated by independent memory**.

That distinction is particularly relevant to the Ramey memo. Johnson’s importance to that controversy comes from one of the photographs attributed to him, in which Ramey holds the document researchers have tried to decipher. The photograph is contemporary evidence; Johnson’s later explanation of precisely how the shoot unfolded is retrospective testimony. His changing film count demonstrates that those two categories cannot safely be treated as interchangeable.

## What the photograph count can and cannot establish

The count dispute does not undermine the basic historical fact that Johnson photographed Ramey and the Roswell debris in Fort Worth. Contemporary provenance, the *Star-Telegram* collection and the surviving images establish his involvement independently of his later recollections. The newspaper’s modern account identifies him as the 21-year-old reporter sent because the regular photographers were unavailable and credits him with six frames.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.star-telegram.com/opinion/bud-kennedy/article289713499.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: star-telegram.com">[Fort Worth Star-Telegram]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">star-telegram.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fort Worth Star-Telegram‘Roswell Incident’ UFO crash in 1947 stirred Fort Worth &#124; Fort Worth Star-TelegramJuly 5, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 5, 2024</span></span></span>

Nor does the discrepancy decide who took every surviving Fort Worth photograph beyond reasonable dispute. Sources still differ over the Marcel pictures: some explicitly credit Johnson, while others assign them to an unknown photographer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.star-telegram.com/opinion/bud-kennedy/article289713499.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: star-telegram.com">[Fort Worth Star-Telegram]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">star-telegram.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fort Worth Star-Telegram‘Roswell Incident’ UFO crash in 1947 stirred Fort Worth &#124; Fort Worth Star-TelegramJuly 5, 2024...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 5, 2024</span></span></span> Arguments based on photographic timing add another unresolved layer rather than eliminating the uncertainty.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://kevinrandle.blogspot.com/2015/11/the-timing-of-roswell-photographs.html?showComment=1447631999173" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: kevinrandle.blogspot.com">[Kevin Randle&#x27;s Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">kevinrandle.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on blogspot.com.</span></span></span>

What the episode *does* establish is narrower and more useful. Johnson began with a specific memory of two exposures and one holder. That became two holders and four sheets, then a six-frame reconstruction consistent with three double-sided holders. The later six-picture account has substantial support, but Johnson did not independently remember that configuration from the outset.

For assessing J. Bond Johnson’s recollections against the photograph, that is the central lesson. His testimony remains valuable, particularly where contemporary records independently corroborate it. But the changing photograph count provides a documented example of his memory adapting as the evidential landscape around him changed. Where a later Johnson recollection supplies a crucial detail that the 1947 photographs or records do not themselves establish, it should therefore be treated as retrospective evidence requiring corroboration rather than as a fixed eyewitness record.
